test pattern (2008) is an album by Japanese electronic composer Ryoji Ikeda, released on the German label Raster-Noton as the second audio chapter of his datamatics project — an ongoing attempt to make perceptible the "invisible multi-substance of data that permeates our world" [[1](https://www.ryojiikeda.com/project/testpattern/), [2](https://kompakt.fm/releases/test_pattern)]. Its premise is severe and literal: a system that converts any data into barcode patterns, then treats the result as music.

### ⚡ The system

test pattern is, in Ikeda's description, a conversion engine: text, sounds, photos and movies are transcoded into barcode patterns and binary strings of 0s and 1s [[1](https://www.ryojiikeda.com/project/testpattern/)]. In performance and installation, a real-time computer program converts the audio signal itself into tightly synchronised black-and-white barcode graphics — what you see is a direct visualization of what you hear, with no interpretive layer between them.

### 🔊 The sound

The album distils this into machine-limit music: ultra-precise clicks, sine tones, white-noise bursts and flickering pulse-grids, sequenced at speeds and frequencies that sit deliberately at the edge of what playback equipment and human hearing can resolve. The project's stated aim is to probe "the relationship between critical points of device performance and the threshold of human perception" [[1](https://www.ryojiikeda.com/project/testpattern/)] — your speakers and your nervous system are both part of the test.

### 📺 Installation form

As an installation, test pattern has been staged as a dark room with eight computer monitors and sixteen loudspeakers laid along the floor: sixteen channels of sound mapped as a grid matrix slicing the space, each converted live into eight synchronized barcode projections [[1](https://www.ryojiikeda.com/project/testpattern/), [2](https://forma.org.uk/projects/test-pattern)]. Later versions scaled up to monumental public projections, with visitors walking on the strobing data-field. Produced with the arts agency Forma, the series became one of the most widely toured audiovisual installations of its era [[2](https://forma.org.uk/projects/test-pattern)].

### 🧬 Context and legacy

Within Raster-Noton's "glitch" milieu, Ikeda's contribution was the most uncompromising: not error as texture but data as substance. test pattern sits between datamatics (2006–) and the later test pattern live concerts, and its barcode aesthetic — sound and image as two renderings of one bitstream — has become shorthand for data-art itself, endlessly echoed in media-art festivals and commercial design alike [[1](https://www.ryojiikeda.com/project/testpattern/), [2](https://kompakt.fm/releases/test_pattern)].
