# Research-note format (data/notes/REC-XXX.md)

One markdown file per work, named by its catalogue id (`REC-026.md`). The canon page
renders it beside the work's drawing, under the heading "The work". The page already
shows title, artist, year, system and source — do NOT repeat them as a header.

## Structure

1. **Opening paragraph** (no heading): what the piece is, when/where it was made, and
   one sentence on why it matters. Cite sources inline.
2. **3–5 sections**, each headed `### <emoji> <Short Title>` — chosen to fit the work,
   typically drawn from: how it was made (the system/mechanism), the notation or
   technology, the sound/technique, premiere/dedication/context, significance and
   afterlife. Use short paragraphs and `*` bullet lists.
3. Total length **300–500 words**. Dense and factual, not padded.

## Citations

- Inline markdown links only: `[text](url)` in prose, or the bracket style
  `[[1](url), [2](url)]` after a claim.
- **Only cite URLs you actually saw in your search results or fetched.** Never invent,
  guess, or "reconstruct" a URL. Fewer real sources beat many fabricated ones.
- Prefer: Wikipedia, label/publisher pages (New World, GRM, Lovely Music, Touch,
  Smithsonian Folkways…), artist sites, serious press (Guardian, NYT, Pitchfork, Wire),
  academic pages. Bandcamp/Discogs acceptable for release facts.
- If a fact is contested or thin, say so rather than asserting.

## Style

- The register of the REC-026 example in this folder: precise, enthusiastic but
  unsentimental, technical details spelled out (mechanisms, dates, durations, names).
- For traditional/PD works (gamelan, maqam, ballads…): research the RECORDING (who,
  where, when, which ensemble/label) and the tradition it documents — both.
- For works flagged `uncertain-details`: note explicitly what is uncertain.
- Emoji headers: one emoji each, apt not cute (🎲 chance, 🎼 notation, 🎹 instrument,
  📻 broadcast, 🔁 process, 🎧 listening, 🥁 rhythm, 🕌 tradition, 🔊 sound, 🤝 people,
  🌍 place, ⚡ technology, 🧬 legacy…).
