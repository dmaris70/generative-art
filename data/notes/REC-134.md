Red Bird: A Political Prisoner's Dream (1973–77) is Trevor Wishart's tape masterpiece, four years in the making at the University of York's electronic music studio and first released in 1978 by York Electronic Studios. Part composition, part allegory of political oppression, it builds a dream-narrative entirely from sounds that turn into other sounds — most famously a human scream that becomes a chirping bird. [[1](https://trevorwishart1.bandcamp.com/album/red-bird-a-political-prisoners-dream-1973-77), [2](https://timbreandorchestration.org/writings/amazing-moments-in-timbre/2019/3/28/red-bird)]

### 🔊 A Vocabulary of Transformations

Wishart's central idea is *sound metamorphosis*: one recognizable source morphing seamlessly into another, carrying symbolic weight as it goes.

* The cast of sounds: birds, animals, machines, buzzing bees, and human voices — including fractured phonemes and the repeated injunction "listen to reason." [[1](https://trevorwishart1.bandcamp.com/album/red-bird-a-political-prisoners-dream-1973-77), [2](https://boomkat.com/products/red-bird-a-political-prisoner-s-dream)]
* These are "orchestrated" and transformed into one another, so that voice/bird/machine become opposing forces in the allegory: the mechanical world of interrogation and confinement against the organic world of flight and freedom.
* The opening scream-into-birdsong sets the terms of the whole work. [[1](https://timbreandorchestration.org/writings/amazing-moments-in-timbre/2019/3/28/red-bird)]

### ✂️ Made Before Samplers

Every metamorphosis was achieved by hand in the analog York studio — painstaking tape splicing of vocal fragments, extended vocal technique, bird calls and tape effects, years before digital sampling made such morphing routine. [[1](https://boomkat.com/products/red-bird-a-political-prisoner-s-dream)] The four-year gestation (begun after Wishart completed his doctoral work at York) reflects that labour: the piece is a catalogue of transformations each of which had to be physically constructed. [[1](https://trevorwishart1.bandcamp.com/album/red-bird-a-political-prisoners-dream-1973-77)]

### 🕊️ The Allegory

The "political prisoner's dream" frame makes Red Bird one of the few unambiguously political works of the classic tape-music era. Its symbols are deliberately legible — reason turned to cage, scream turned to bird — and Wishart has written extensively (notably in his books On Sonic Art and Audible Design) about sound-symbolism as a compositional resource. The dream logic lets the piece be simultaneously narrative and abstract.

### 🧬 Recognition

Red Bird was awarded a Euphonie d'Or at the Bourges international electroacoustic festival, marking it as a canonical work of the medium [[1](https://boomkat.com/products/red-bird-a-political-prisoner-s-dream)], and it remains Wishart's best-known piece — the foundation of a career spent building software (Composers Desktop Project) to do digitally what Red Bird did with razor blades and tape.
