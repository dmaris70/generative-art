Dots (1940) is a two-minute cameraless film by the Scottish-Canadian animator Norman McLaren in which *everything* — image and music alike — was drawn by hand with pen and ink directly onto 35mm film stock. Made in New York while McLaren was on a Solomon Guggenheim Foundation grant (its first copy sold to the Guggenheim Museum), it entered the collection of the National Film Board of Canada, which he joined in 1941 at John Grierson's invitation and where he spent his career. [[1](https://www.nfb.ca/film/dots/), [2](https://www.cinephilicmusings.com/2022/04/dots-norman-mclaren-1940-canada.html), [3](https://thecinemaofattractions.wordpress.com/2011/02/16/norman-mclarens-animated-sound/)]

### 🎨 Drawing the Picture

Dancing coloured dots bounce, split and cluster against plain grounds, animated frame by frame in ink on clear film — no camera, no photography. McLaren later noted the clear-film method's drawback: the transparent ground picks up dirt and dust, which becomes visible flicker and audible noise. [[1](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/284900520_About_Animated_Sound_Norman_McLaren's_experiences_with_synthetic_sound)]

### 🔊 Drawing the Sound

The radical move is on the left edge of the filmstrip. The optical soundtrack area — normally exposed photographically from a microphone signal — was inked by hand.

* McLaren drew dots and dashes of India ink directly onto the soundtrack strip; when run through a projector, the photocell reads the marks as sound. [[1](https://thecinemaofattractions.wordpress.com/2011/02/16/norman-mclarens-animated-sound/)]
* Size and shape of the mark set the timbre, spacing sets the pitch, and density/darkness sets the volume — sound *synthesis* by draughtsmanship, years before electronic synthesizers. [[1](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/284900520_About_Animated_Sound_Norman_McLaren's_experiences_with_synthetic_sound)]
* The result is a percussive, boinging, entirely synthetic score in perfect sync with the visuals — because both are literally the same gesture on the same strip of celluloid.

### 🔁 One of a Family

Dots belongs to a cluster of cameraless films from McLaren's New York period — Loops, Stars and Stripes, Boogie-Doodle — and the technique was demonstrated in the NFB documentary Pen Point Percussion. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boogie-Doodle), [2](https://thekidshouldseethis.com/post/pen-point-percussion-norman-mclaren)] He refined animated sound for decades, culminating in the card-catalogue optical-sound system of Synchromy (1971), and wrote up his methods in NFB technical notes. [[1](https://www3.nfb.ca/photogallery/archives_mclaren/notech/NT31EN.pdf), [2](https://www.nfb.ca/film/synchromy/)]

### 🧬 Why It Matters

Dots collapses the distinction between score, performance and recording: the composer draws the waveform itself. It stands at the head of graphical/synthetic sound — ancestor to optical-sound experiments, drawn-waveform synthesis and every practice where sound is specified as image.
