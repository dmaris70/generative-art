Klang (1982) is Jonty Harrison's acousmatic classic — a stereo tape piece built entirely from the sounds of two earthenware casserole dishes, and one of the defining works of the British electroacoustic school. Its title is onomatopoeic: the family of sharp, metallic attacks with resonances rich in harmonics that supplies the entire raw material of the piece. [[1](https://electrocd.com/en/oeuvre/13799-klang), [2](https://empreintesdigitales.bandcamp.com/track/klang)]

### 🍲 Two casseroles from Denis Smalley's kitchen

The starting point was Harrison's discovery of two earthenware casseroles in the kitchen of fellow composer Denis Smalley. Their sounds were recorded in the Electroacoustic Music Studio of the University of East Anglia (Norwich) in the summer of 1981, in two families:

* **attack/resonance** sounds, made by tapping the lids on or in the bowls;
* **continuous rolling** sounds, made by running the lids around the insides of the bowls. [[1](https://electrocd.com/en/oeuvre/13799-klang)]

From this almost absurdly modest source, the piece spins an entire sound-world — the found object elevated to orchestra, in the lineage of Schaeffer's musique concrète but with a distinctly sculptural sense of gesture and resonance.

### 🎛 Birmingham and Budapest

Harrison composed the work at the Electroacoustic Music Studios of the University of Birmingham in May 1982, with further work at Magyar Rádió in Budapest that June. The concert premiere took place on 7 December 1982 in the Elgar Concert Room at the University of Birmingham — the institution where, the same year, Harrison founded BEAST (Birmingham ElectroAcoustic Sound Theatre), the loudspeaker orchestra over which his works are diffused in concert. [[1](https://electrocd.com/en/oeuvre/13799-klang)]

### 🏆 Bourges and the Euphonie d'Or

Klang took second prize in the analogue category at the Bourges International Electroacoustic Music Competition in 1983. More tellingly, in 1992 Bourges awarded it the **Euphonie d'Or**, naming it one of the twenty most significant works heard in the competition's first twenty years — canonization by the genre's own central institution. [[1](https://electrocd.com/en/oeuvre/13799-klang)]

### 🧬 Afterlife

The piece circulates on Harrison's empreintes DIGITALes discs (notably *Évidence matérielle*) and remains a staple of electroacoustic teaching: a compact demonstration that acousmatic listening — sound cut loose from its visible cause — can make monumental drama from crockery. Its combination of recognizable source and abstract development became a signature of Harrison's aesthetic, and of the Birmingham school he trained.
