Schall (1994) is an eight-minute electroacoustic work by the Argentine-born, Paris-based composer Horacio Vaggione, composed entirely from sampled, computer-processed piano sounds and realized for eight-track fixed media. Commissioned by the IMEB in Bourges, it premiered at the Festival Synthèse there on 10 June 1994 [[1](https://brahms.ircam.fr/en/works/work/52543/)]. It is the reference work for the technique Vaggione called micromontage — composing by placing thousands of tiny sound particles directly in time — and a cornerstone of the microsound aesthetic. (The GRM association in this catalogue comes via the Recollection GRM label's later vinyl reissue; the commission itself was from Bourges [[2](https://recollectiongrm.bandcamp.com/album/schall-rechant)].)

### 🎹 A piano, atomized

Every sound in Schall — the title is German for "sound" — derives from piano samples, dismantled and rebuilt by digital means:

* **Granulation** breaks the samples into grains a few milliseconds long.
* **Convolution, waveshaping and phase vocoding** smear, colour and stretch them into low-intensity textures, against which stronger, recognisably pianistic objects strike [[1](https://www.squidco.com/r.cgi?p=34317)].
* The result keeps the piano's attack-resonance DNA audible even when no note is ever "played."

### 🔬 Composing across time scales

Vaggione's stated subject is the interaction between different temporal scales: the microtime of the grain, the mid-level figure, the phrase. Small sonic figures recur throughout with varied transformations, and figures that work at one scale deliberately do not function the same way at another [[1](https://brahms.ircam.fr/en/works/work/52543/), [2](https://www.squidco.com/r.cgi?p=34317)]. His program note describes a "polyphonic dialogue between near and far": quiet granular strata as background, emphatic objects as foreground, the music passing between liminal and turbulent states.

### 🔊 What it sounds like

Crackling swarms of piano-dust, sudden dense chords that bloom and evaporate, silences that read as structural punctuation. The eight-channel spatialization distributes the grain-clouds around the listener; in stereo reduction the depth-of-field contrast (near/far) carries the same argument.

### 🧬 Releases and legacy

Schall circulated first on an IMEB disc of Vaggione's piano-derived electroacoustic works, then in the MIT Press "Sound Anthology" and the journal Chimères' CD (2000) [[1](https://brahms.ircam.fr/en/works/work/52543/)]. Its profile widened considerably when Recollection GRM paired it with Rechant on a dedicated LP [[2](https://recollectiongrm.bandcamp.com/album/schall-rechant), [3](https://www.soundohm.com/product/schall-rechant-lp-1)]. The piece is now a standard citation in the microsound literature — the demonstration that granular technique could yield not texture-wash but precise, almost instrumental writing at the threshold of audibility.
