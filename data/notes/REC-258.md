This entry pairs a taksim (improvisation) and saz semaisi in the makam Nihavent, performed on the tanbur by Necdet Yaşar (1930–2017), the pre-eminent Turkish tanbur player of the later 20th century. The 1973 date corresponds to Yaşar's residency at the University of Washington, whose Ethnomusicology Archives hold recordings of him from that year — including a Nihavent taksim — though exactly which issued recording the catalogue refers to is uncertain (see below). [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necdet_Ya%C5%9Far), [2](https://researchworks.oclc.org/archivegrid/collection/data/1266177980)]

### 🪕 The tanbur and the lineage

The tanbur is the long-necked fretted lute at the heart of Ottoman classical music, prized for its microtonally fretted neck and shimmering sustain. Yaşar, born in Nizip near Gaziantep, studied with Mesut Cemil — son of the legendary Tanburi Cemil Bey — placing him directly in the instrument's great modern lineage. He was celebrated for his plectrum technique, named a State Artist of Turkey in 1991, and co-founded the Istanbul State Turkish Music Ensemble in 1988. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necdet_Ya%C5%9Far)]

### 🕌 Makam, taksim, saz semaisi

* A **taksim** is an unmetered solo improvisation that unfolds a makam's melodic pathway (seyir) — Yaşar's taksims were considered models of the art; experts called him "the living embodiment of seyir". [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necdet_Ya%C5%9Far)]
* A **saz semaisi** is the composed instrumental piece that conventionally follows, in the 10-beat aksak semai usul.
* **Nihavent** is the makam roughly analogous to the Western minor mode — a favorite for displaying lyricism inside classical discipline.

### 🎓 Seattle, 1973

Yaşar was resident artist in ethnomusicology at the University of Washington in 1972–73 (returning 1980–81), lecturing on the makam system; his 1972 appearance at the Society for Ethnomusicology meeting effectively introduced Ottoman classical performance to American ethnomusicology. UW's archives catalogue 1973 recording sessions and a 25 May 1973 concert, with items including a Nihavent taksim; some material streams via the UW Libraries SoundCloud. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necdet_Ya%C5%9Far), [2](https://archiveswest.orbiscascade.org/ark:80444/xv741454), [3](https://soundcloud.com/uwlibraries/wauem1989031_yasar)]

### ❓ What is uncertain

We found no verified *commercial* 1973 release titled "Nihavent Taksim / Saz Semaisi". The likeliest identification is the UW archive material above; alternatively the recording may come from Turkish radio archives or later compilations (Kalan and others anthologized Yaşar), many of which carry imprecise dates. The performer, instrument and repertoire are secure; the provenance of this specific recording is not, and we flag rather than assert it.
