Çoğluotobüsişletmesi (1975–79) is a half-hour algorithmic work for piano by Clarence Barlow, the Calcutta-born composer who became one of new music's most rigorous computational thinkers. Sketched on a bus journey through Eastern Anatolia in May 1975 and completed in late 1978, the piece was generated entirely by computer programs Barlow wrote himself in 1975 and 1978–79, and was produced at [IRCAM](https://rudigermeyer.com/words/clarence-barlow) in 1980. It stands as one of the earliest and most thoroughgoing demonstrations that tonality and metre themselves — not just notes — can be computed. [[1](https://clarlow.org/composition/cogluotobusisletmesi/), [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarence_Barlow)]

### ⚠️ Corrections to this catalogue entry

This entry was flagged uncertain, rightly:

* The title's correct spelling is **Çoğluotobüsişletmesi** (Turkish; "işletmesi" ≈ enterprise, "otobüs" = bus), not "Coglugtobusisletmesi" as listed. The name commemorates music Barlow heard on that Anatolian bus in 1975. [[1](https://clarlow.org/composition/cogluotobusisletmesi/)]
* The year "1975" is the year of conception only. Barlow's own site dates the finished work 1979; other accounts say completed late 1978, premiered after production at IRCAM in 1980. [[1](https://clarlow.org/composition/cogluotobusisletmesi/), [2](https://rudigermeyer.com/words/clarence-barlow)]

### 🧮 Computed pitch and metre fields

Composition was preceded by months of research into Plomp and Levelt's psychoacoustic theory of consonance and dissonance, alongside Barlow's own algebraic investigations of the phenomena of **tonality** and **metricity**. The programs steer the music continuously along independent axes — tonal to atonal, metric to ametric, eventful to sparse — with several layers running in parallel at different speeds, so that the piece unfolds as one organic whole rather than a sequence of sections. [[1](https://clarlow.org/composition/cogluotobusisletmesi/)]

### 🎹 A microtonally retuned piano

The pitch material uses intervals derived from quarter-tones, achieved economically: only four piano strings per octave are retuned. Textures range from pointillistic scatter to melody and chords, and the score is playable by "two or more hands" — pianist Herbert Henck made the reference recording, and in 2006 Barlow produced a version for four pianos. [[1](https://clarlow.org/composition/cogluotobusisletmesi/), [2](http://clarlow.org/compositions-by-year/cogluotobus4-pianos/)]

### 🧬 Afterlife

The work became the calling card for Barlow's whole project — his quantitative theories of harmony and rhythm were later codified in software (Autobusk) and in widely-read texts, and he taught generations of algorithmic composers in Cologne, The Hague and Santa Barbara. He used Çoğluotobüsişletmesi as the central exhibit in his own lectures on algorithmic composition. [[1](https://clarlow.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Barlow-Algorithmic-Composition-Callejon.pdf), [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarence_Barlow)]
