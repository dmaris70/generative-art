Perfect Lives (1983) is Robert Ashley's "opera for television": seven half-hour episodes conceived not for the stage but for broadcast, developed between 1977 and 1983 and realised with video director John Sanborn [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_Lives), [2](http://www.robertashley.org/productions/1977-83-perfectlives.htm)]. Ashley called it a "comic opera about reincarnation"; it is widely held to be the most fully achieved television opera ever made [[3](https://insheepsclothinghifi.com/perfect-lives-stream/)].

### 📻 Opera as Broadcast

Ashley's premise was that American opera's true house was television, not the proscenium. Each episode intertwines his continuous spoken-sung narration with layered music and Sanborn's hypnotic analog video effects — split screens, chroma-key landscapes, text overlays — so that the video processing functions as orchestration [[1](https://insheepsclothinghifi.com/perfect-lives-stream/), [2](https://www.spectacletheater.com/perfect-lives/)].

### 🤝 The Cast

Ashley himself performs as "R," the lounge-singer narrator, his Midwestern drawl sliding along the boundary of speech and song. "Blue" Gene Tyranny plays Buddy, "the World's Greatest Piano Player," improvising continuously across boogie-woogie, cocktail and gospel idioms beneath the text [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_Lives), [2](https://www.spectacletheater.com/perfect-lives/)]. The plot — insofar as it surfaces — concerns an ill-advised, metaphysically tinged bank robbery in a small Midwestern town, involving the singer, the pianist, the bank teller and an elopement [[3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_Lives)].

### 🎼 Structure

The seven episodes ("The Park," "The Supermarket," "The Bank," "The Bar," "The Living Room," "The Church," "The Backyard") each fix on one location of ordinary American life, with the text built over strict rhythmic templates — Ashley's speech riding a constant pulse the way a raga rides a tala. The language is the score: vernacular philosophy, weather reports and gossip elevated to liturgy [[1](http://www.robertashley.org/productions/1977-83-perfectlives.htm), [2](https://operawire.com/opera-meets-film-robert-ashleys-perfect-lives/)].

### 🧬 Afterlife

Produced through the downtown New York video-art infrastructure and broadcast on television in 1984, Perfect Lives circulated on Lovely Music audio editions (cassette 1983, CD 1991, reissues 2006 and 2020) and DVD (2005), with a recent Blu-ray remaster [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_Lives), [2](https://insheepsclothinghifi.com/perfect-lives-stream/)]. Its influence runs through everything that treats American speech as melody — from Ashley's own later operas to contemporary composers of "documentary" vocal music. Laurie Anderson, Cathy Berberian and rap have all been proposed as its cousins; nothing else sounds like it.
