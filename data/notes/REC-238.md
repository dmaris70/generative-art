Kyema (Intermediate States) is the first panel of Éliane Radigue's Trilogie de la Mort, the three-hour meditation on death and rebirth she composed between 1985 and 1993 on the ARP 2500 modular synthesizer. Composed in her Paris studio and premiered on 17 September 1988 at New Langton Arts, San Francisco, Kyema was issued by Phill Niblock's New York label XI Records (this catalogue dates the XI release 1990), with the complete Trilogie following as a 3-CD set in 1998. [[1](https://elianeradigue.bandcamp.com/album/trilogie-de-la-mort), [2](https://xirecords.bandcamp.com/album/trilogie-de-la-mort), [3](https://www.pinaultcollection.com/en/boursedecommerce/eliane-radigue-kyema)]

### 🕌 The Six Bardos

Kyema draws directly on the Bardo Thödol, the Tibetan Book of the Dead. Its 61 minutes unfold in six continuous sections corresponding to the six bardos — the "intermediate states" through which a being's existential continuity passes:

* birth, dream, contemplation (the states of life);
* death, clear light, and passage-and-return (the states between lives). [[1](https://www.pinaultcollection.com/en/boursedecommerce/eliane-radigue-kyema)]

Radigue had been a practising Tibetan Buddhist since the mid-1970s, studying under lama Tsuglag Mawe Wangchuk (Pawo Rinpoche); the Trilogie is the fullest musical expression of that practice. [[1](https://elianeradigue.bandcamp.com/album/trilogie-de-la-mort)]

### ⚡ The ARP 2500, Played Like a Breath

Radigue's instrument for three decades was a single ARP 2500 modular synthesizer, recorded to magnetic tape.

* Her method: minute, almost imperceptible adjustments of oscillators over long spans, mixed and assembled on tape into seamless hour-long arcs. [[1](https://www.academia.edu/109007132/%C3%89liane_Radigue_and_The_ARP_2500_Modular_Synthesiser)]
* The music moves at the threshold of perception — beating partials, slowly emerging overtones, changes you notice only after they have happened. Nothing "happens," and everything changes.

### 🎧 Listening as Practice

Like the bardo teachings themselves, Kyema is about attention during transition. Deep listening is not a metaphor here but the compositional subject: the piece trains the ear to follow continuity through transformation — precisely what the Bardo Thödol asks of consciousness at death.

### 🧬 Legacy

The Trilogie — Kyema, Kailasha (1991), Koumé (1993) — is widely considered Radigue's masterpiece and a summit of drone music. [[1](https://elianeradigue.bandcamp.com/album/trilogie-de-la-mort), [2](https://www.blankforms.org/events/eliane-radigue-trilogie-de-la-mort)] Its slow-breathing electronic minimalism, long marginal, now underwrites a whole ecology of ambient and spectral music, and Radigue's late acoustic works (the Occam series) extend the same listening discipline to live instruments.
