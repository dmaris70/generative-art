FRKWYS Vol. 14: Nue (2018) is a ten-track album by Los Angeles composer Tashi Wada with his father Yoshi Wada — Fluxus veteran and builder of monumental drone instruments — "and Friends," released 28 September 2018 by RVNG Intl. as the fourteenth volume of the label's intergenerational FRKWYS collaboration series [[1](https://igetrvng.com/products/frkwys14/), [2](https://www.factmag.com/2018/07/11/tashi-wada-yoshi-wada-frkwys-rvng/)]. Where most FRKWYS pairings connect strangers across generations, Nue is literally father and son: the first time the two Wadas were brought together on tape.

### 🤝 Family and friends

* **Yoshi Wada** (b. 1935, Kyoto), an early member of the 1960s Fluxus movement, co-composed three of the tracks [[1](https://www.factmag.com/2018/07/11/tashi-wada-yoshi-wada-frkwys-rvng/)].
* **Julia Holter** sings on two tracks; percussionist **Corey Fogel** and producer **Cole MGN** fill out the extended family [[1](https://www.factmag.com/2018/07/11/tashi-wada-yoshi-wada-frkwys-rvng/), [2](https://igetrvng.com/products/frkwys14/)].

### 🎺 Drone lineages

RVNG's notes frame the album as Tashi's "widest vision to date," drawing together his inheritances: the minimalist bagpipe music of his father, the psychoacoustic and perceptual explorations of his mentor James Tenney, and reimagined forms of ancient and devotional music [[1](https://igetrvng.com/products/frkwys14/)]. The palette runs to bagpipes, sirens, organ-like sustained tones and voice — slow-beating unisons that split and shimmer, procession-like pulses, Holter's voice floating through the overtone haze. The title Nue names a chimeric creature of Japanese folklore, apt for music assembled from spliced traditions (a reading suggested by the album's presentation; sources are thin on the Wadas' own gloss).

### 🔁 The FRKWYS premise

The FRKWYS series pairs contemporary artists with their elders and influences; Nue literalizes the series' theme of transmission — a son composing a frame for his father's sound-world, with collaborators drawn from both of their circles [[1](https://igetrvng.com/products/frkwys14/), [2](https://www.factmag.com/2018/07/11/tashi-wada-yoshi-wada-frkwys-rvng/)].

### 🧬 Afterlife

The album was followed by a companion release, Nue Remixes, with reworkings by Laurel Halo and Julia Holter [[1](https://igetrvng.com/products/frkwys14-nue-remixes/)]. Coming late in Yoshi Wada's life, Nue stands as the fullest recorded document of the father-son collaboration, and it consolidated Tashi Wada's standing as a composer extending the American drone-minimalist line — Tenney's tuning rigor, Fluxus' ceremony — into a warmer, song-adjacent register [[2](https://tashiwada.bandcamp.com/album/frkwys-vol-14-nue)].
