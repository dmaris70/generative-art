Brian Jones Presents the Pipes of Pan at Joujouka documents the trance music of the Master Musicians of Jajouka, recorded in their Moroccan mountain village on 29 July 1968 by Rolling Stone Brian Jones, and released posthumously on 8 October 1971 on the Stones' newly founded label. It is a double landmark: a document of one of North Africa's most extraordinary living traditions, and — through Jones's heavy studio processing — a founding artifact of both "world music" and psychedelic production. ### 🕌 The tradition

Jajouka, in the Jbala foothills of the Rif, maintains a hereditary ensemble of musicians whose Sufi practice centres on the festival of Aid el Kbir and the rite of **Boujeloud** — a figure sewn into freshly slaughtered goatskins who dances and runs through the village at night, flailing at onlookers, while massed double-reed rhaita pipes and drums play driving, interlocking patterns for hours. The music's ecstatic function is explicit: it heals, protects and possesses. Jones called his selection "a specially chosen representation" of the week-long festival; William Burroughs famously dubbed the ensemble "the 4,000-year-old rock 'n' roll band," folding the village's own Pan-like mythology into Beat legend. ### 🎙 The recording

Jones travelled to the village with painter Mohamed Hamri and Beat artist Brion Gysin — who had been bringing Western bohemia to Jajouka since the 1950s — plus engineer George Chkiantz and Suki Potier, taping the ceremonies on a portable Uher recorder. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Jones_Presents_the_Pipes_of_Pan_at_Joujouka)]

### 🎛 Proto-dub in London

Back in London, Jones treated the field tapes as raw material: stereo phasing, echo and cross-fades, editing a ninety-minute performance down to fourteen minutes. Purists have never forgiven the interference; The Wire later celebrated the result as a "proto-dub masterpiece" anticipating electronic treatments of recorded tradition. Jones drowned in July 1969, and the album appeared as his memorial. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Jones_Presents_the_Pipes_of_Pan_at_Joujouka), [2](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/brian-jones-presents-the-pipes-of-pan-at-joujouka/)]

### ⚖️ Afterlife and dispute

The record drew Ornette Coleman (and later the Rolling Stones themselves) to the village, and made the musicians world-tourable. A 1995 Point Music reissue triggered protests over crediting of the musicians and of Hamri; the community itself split into rival ensembles whose competing spellings — "Jajouka" (led by Bachir Attar) and "Joujouka" — persist on records and festival stages today. The 1995 edition regularized the title's spelling to match the band name.
