Different Trains (1988) is Steve Reich's three-movement work for string quartet and tape, premiered by the Kronos Quartet at London's Queen Elizabeth Hall on 2 November 1988 and recorded by Kronos for Nonesuch the following year. It fuses documentary testimony with chamber music through a new technique — speech melody — and is widely regarded as the most important response to the Holocaust in the instrumental repertoire. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Different_Trains), [2](https://www.nonesuch.com/journal/watch-steve-reich-composing-different-trains-2023-11-02)]

### 🚂 Two kinds of trains

As a child of divorced parents during 1939–42, Reich rode Pullman trains between New York and Los Angeles with his governess. Decades later he reflected that, as a Jew, had he been in Europe in those same years, the trains he rode would have been very different — the deportation trains. The piece holds those two facts side by side without commentary. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Different_Trains), [2](https://www.writing.upenn.edu/~afilreis/Holocaust/difftrains.html)]

### 🎙 Speech melody as method

* Reich recorded interviews — his former governess, a retired Pullman porter, and three Holocaust survivors — and selected short phrases whose natural spoken pitch and rhythm he transcribed for instruments.
* The viola and cello literally double the women's and men's voices respectively; the sampled speech, period train sounds and sirens run on tape alongside multiple pre-recorded quartet tracks and the live quartet.
* The three movements — America before the war, Europe during the war, and after the war — let the same method carry nostalgia, horror, and uneasy aftermath in turn. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Different_Trains), [2](https://www.laphil.com/musicdb/pieces/307/different-trains)]

This was a genuine invention: for the first time, recorded documentary speech generated the notes the instruments play, a technique Reich would extend in The Cave and City Life. [[1](https://www.nonesuch.com/journal/watch-steve-reich-composing-different-trains-2023-11-02)]

### 🏆 Recording and reception

The Kronos Quartet recording, released by Nonesuch in 1989 (paired with Electric Counterpoint played by Pat Metheny), won the 1990 Grammy Award for Best Contemporary Composition. [[1](https://www.nonesuch.com/albums/different-trains-electric-counterpoint), [2](https://kronosquartet.org/recordings/detail/steve-reich-different-trains/)]

### 🧬 Legacy

Different Trains rehabilitated the idea that process-based minimalism could bear historical and moral weight, and its sampling-into-notation method anticipated a generation of composers working between recorded speech and instruments. It remains among the most performed and taught works of late twentieth-century chamber music. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Different_Trains)]
