Automatic Writing (1979) is Robert Ashley's most intimate and unsettling record: some three-quarters of an hour of barely-voiced involuntary speech, distilled over five years of recording and released as the composer's own album on his newly founded Lovely Music label. Ashley, who wrote in the liner notes that he had "a mild form of Tourette's," treated his own compulsive utterances as found musical material — a portrait of the mind's speech before it becomes language. [[1](https://robert-ashley.bandcamp.com/album/automatic-writing), [2](https://www.forcedexposure.com/Catalog/ashley-robert-automatic-writing-cd/LCD.1002CD.html)]

### 🗣️ Four characters

Ashley recorded and analyzed his murmured, quasi-Tourettic monologue and built the piece from four interlocking "characters":

* His own close-miked involuntary speech, whispery and hypnagogic.
* A French translation murmured by Mimi Johnson, floating like a second conscience.
* A Polymoog played softly in the distance, like music from another room.
* A switching-circuit-controlled organ presence designed and built by Paul DeMarinis. [[1](https://robert-ashley.bandcamp.com/album/automatic-writing)]

The layers were recorded and mixed by Ashley at Mills College's Center for Contemporary Music (Oakland), the American Cultural Center in Paris, and Mastertone Studios, New York [[1](https://www.forcedexposure.com/Catalog/ashley-robert-automatic-writing-cd/LCD.1002CD.html)].

### 🔊 Language at degree zero

Ashley's subject is the "primitive" root of speech — the impulse to make sounds expressing an inner state whether or not anyone listens [[1](https://robert-ashley.bandcamp.com/album/automatic-writing)]. The title invokes the Surrealists' automatic writing, but Ashley's version is neurological rather than mystical: composition as eavesdropping on one's own brain. Played at the intended low volume it sits at the threshold of audibility, closer to ASMR (decades early) than to any concert genre.

### 📻 Toward television opera

Automatic Writing is the hinge of Ashley's career, connecting his 1960s ONCE-festival experimentalism to the great television operas (Perfect Lives, Atalanta) built from stylized American speech. The four-character structure is effectively an opera in miniature, and Ashley later described his whole output as opera rooted in the sound of talking [[1](https://4columns.org/frere-jones-sasha/robert-ashley)].

### 🧬 Legacy

Initially bewildering even to admirers, the record has grown steadily in stature — reissued on CD in 1996 with *Purposeful Lady Slow Afternoon* and *She Was a Visitor*, and later repressed on vinyl [[1](https://www.discogs.com/release/397910-Robert-Ashley-Automatic-Writing), [2](https://www.tinymixtapes.com/news/robert-ashleys-automatic-writing-reissue-coming-lovely-music)]. Its descendants are everywhere: whisper-voiced ambient, lowercase sound art, and every music that treats the flaws and tics of a single human voice as compositional bedrock.
