Six Fantasies on a Poem by Thomas Campion (1978–79) is a computer-music work by Paul Lansky, made at Princeton on IBM 360/91 and 3033 mainframes. Its entire sound-world derives from a single spoken reading of the Elizabethan poet Thomas Campion's "Rose-cheek'd Laura" by Hannah MacKay, transformed by linear predictive coding (LPC) into six studies of what music already lives inside one voice saying one poem. It is a landmark of voice resynthesis and a turning point in Lansky's career. [[1](https://paul.mycpanel.princeton.edu/liner_notes/fantasies.html), [2](https://www.nime.org/proceedings/2011/nime2011_523.pdf)]

### ⚡ Linear predictive coding

LPC — a speech-compression technique from telephony research — analyzes a recording into a source (pitch, timing) and a filter (the vowel/consonant resonances of the vocal tract), which can then be resynthesized independently.

* Lansky could stretch time without changing pitch, impose new pitch contours on intact speech rhythms, or keep the vowels' resonances while replacing the voice beneath them. [[1](https://www.nime.org/proceedings/2011/nime2011_523.pdf)]
* This was the first time Lansky used LPC on given sonic material; it became his signature tool (through *Idle Chatter* and beyond). [[1](https://www.nime.org/proceedings/2011/nime2011_523.pdf), [2](https://paul.mycpanel.princeton.edu/bio.html)]
* Computing was batch-mode and slow: seconds of sound took minutes of mainframe time, composed in code, heard later.

### 🎼 Six ways of hearing one reading

Each fantasy isolates an implicit musicality of MacKay's reading — her contours, vowels, resonances — moving along the continuum from speech to song, and back: the final fantasy returns the plain reading, which by then sounds unmistakably like melody. The piece's quiet thesis: the music was in the speech all along. [[1](https://paul.mycpanel.princeton.edu/liner_notes/fantasies.html), [2](https://www.nime.org/proceedings/2011/nime2011_523.pdf)]

### 🕰 Campion, closing the loop

Campion (1567–1620) was himself a composer-poet who wrote about the music of English verse; "Rose-cheek'd Laura" comes from his metrical experiments. A 20th-century computer analyzing the resonances of a 16th-century poem about music is exactly the kind of long joke Lansky relishes.

### 🧬 Afterlife

Six Fantasies is a fixture of the computer-music canon: endlessly analyzed (researchers have even built a "Six Fantasies Machine" instrument to model its phrases [[1](https://www.nime.org/proceedings/2011/nime2011_523.pdf)]), taught as the exemplary humane use of voice synthesis, and audibly ancestral to the pitched-speech textures now everywhere from Laurie Anderson's vocoder works to contemporary autotune practice. It circulates on Lansky's *Fantasies and Tableaux* CD. [[1](https://paul.mycpanel.princeton.edu/liner_notes/fantasies.html)]
