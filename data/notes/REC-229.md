Four Violins (1964) is a roughly half-hour drone work by Tony Conrad, recorded at the end of 1964 in an apartment on Ludlow Street, New York: Conrad overdubbed himself four times, stacking four violin parts into dense layers of just-intonation harmony. Made while he was still playing in La Monte Young's Theatre of Eternal Music, it is one of the earliest surviving *documents* of minimalist drone music — and for decades nearly the only one publicly hearable. [[1](https://tableoftheelements.bandcamp.com/album/four-violins-1964-edit), [2](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-lists/tony-conrad-10-essential-recordings-from-the-drone-pioneer-74932/)]

### 🎻 The recording

* Four violin parts, played by Conrad and overdubbed on tape — primitive multitracking in service of maximal sustain. [[1](https://tableoftheelements.bandcamp.com/album/four-violins-1964-edit)]
* The music holds long tones in rigorously designed microtonal relationships, letting the combination tones and beating patterns "scrape against one another in all their complexity" over the work's half-hour span. [[1](https://tableoftheelements.bandcamp.com/album/four-violins-1964-edit)]

### 🎼 Just intonation

Conrad — a Harvard-trained mathematician — tuned by whole-number frequency ratios rather than equal temperament. From 1962 his amplified strings had introduced the sustained just-intonation drone into what became "minimal" music; in Four Violins the purity of the ratios is exactly what generates the audible turbulence, as harmonics reinforce and interfere. [[1](https://www.tableoftheelements.org/tcc-08-four-violins-1964)]

### 🤝 Theatre of Eternal Music

From 1962–65 Conrad played marathon drone sessions with La Monte Young, Marian Zazeela, John Cale and Angus MacLise — the collective sometimes called the Dream Syndicate. Young has famously withheld the group's tapes, disputing Conrad's and Cale's claims of joint authorship; Four Violins, made solo at the moment that collaboration was ending, became Conrad's proof-of-concept that the drone music was his to make too. [[1](https://www.frieze.com/article/tony-conrad-1940-2016), [2](https://www.artforum.com/columns/tony-conrad-201470/)]

### 🧬 Thirty years in a drawer

The tape went unreleased until Table of the Elements issued it in the mid-1990s (and within the *Early Minimalism* box, whose other pieces are 1990s reconstructions — Four Violins is the one genuinely "early" work). Its reappearance re-litigated minimalism's origin story, putting Conrad beside Young at the root of the lineage that runs through Cale's Velvet Underground viola to drone, noise and much experimental rock since. Conrad died in 2016; Four Violins remains his cornerstone.
