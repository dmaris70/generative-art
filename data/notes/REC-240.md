Touch Works, for Hurdy Gurdy and Voice (Touch TO:49, released 2 April 2001) collects three of Phill Niblock's massive microtonal drone pieces — one built from Jim O'Rourke's hurdy-gurdy, two from Thomas Buckner's baritone voice. Niblock (1933–2024), the New York filmmaker-composer who ran the Experimental Intermedia loft for half a century, here transfers his tape-era layering method fully into the digital domain: every piece was assembled in Pro Tools as 24 tracks mixed to stereo. [[1](https://touch33.net/catalogue/to49-phill-niblock-touch-works-for-hurdy-gurdy-and-voice.html), [2](https://www.discogs.com/release/168289-Phill-Niblock-Touch-Works-For-Hurdy-Gurdy-And-Voice)]

### 🎻 Hurdy Hurry

The opener is constructed from samples of O'Rourke's hurdy-gurdy playing, recorded in New York at Robert Poss's studio (Band of Susans). Niblock multiplied the samples across 24 tracks with octave pitch-shifts — and, characteristically, kept rather than corrected an instrumental flaw: O'Rourke's instrument had a damaged wheel, and its irregularity is folded into the drone's grain. [[1](https://touch33.net/catalogue/to49-phill-niblock-touch-works-for-hurdy-gurdy-and-voice.html), [2](https://phillniblock.bandcamp.com/album/touch-works-for-hurdy-gurdy-and-voice)]

### 🗣 A Y U ("As Yet Untitled")

The two remaining tracks stack samples of Buckner — the great new-music baritone, here also using throat-singing techniques — who commissioned the piece; the material originated around a Niblock concert at Merkin Hall, New York, in October 1999. The first version layers 24 vocal tracks; the "live" version adds three further voices in performance plus four tracks of pitch shift on each, thickening the mass to dozens of near-unison lines. [[1](https://touch33.net/catalogue/to49-phill-niblock-touch-works-for-hurdy-gurdy-and-voice.html)]

### 🔊 The microtonal method

Niblock's procedure, constant since the 1960s, is to record long instrumental tones, then layer many copies tuned microtonally close to one another. The clustered pitches generate audible beating, difference tones and a slowly churning spectral halo — music with no melody, no rhythm, no development, only an enormous *inside*. He intended playback loud, so the acoustic phenomena bloom in the room; the "touch" of the title is nearly literal, sound as physical pressure. [[1](https://touch33.net/catalogue/to49-phill-niblock-touch-works-for-hurdy-gurdy-and-voice.html), [2](https://phillniblock.bandcamp.com/album/touch-works-for-hurdy-gurdy-and-voice)]

### 🧬 Context and afterlife

Issued by the London label Touch — Niblock's main outlet from the late 1990s onward — the disc sits at the meeting point of American minimalism's drone wing (Young, Radigue) and the post-rock/electronica audience O'Rourke's presence signalled. It helped introduce Niblock's sixty-year project to a new generation, and its combination of ancient drone instrument, extended voice and digital massing is as clean a summary of his aesthetic as exists on record.
