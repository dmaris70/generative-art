La fabbrica illuminata (1964) — "the illuminated factory" — is Luigi Nono's work for female voice and four-track tape, dedicated to the workers of the Italsider steelworks at Genova-Cornigliano. It is one of the defining works of politically engaged electronic music: the factory itself, recorded on site, becomes the sound-world against which a lone soprano sings of the conditions inside it. [[1](https://www.luiginono.it/en/works/la-fabbrica-illuminata/), [2](https://allthatdust.com/releases/la-fabbrica-illuminata/)]

### 🏭 The Tape

Nono assembled the four-track tape from a montage of sources:

* voices and machine noise recorded inside the Italsider plant
* the RAI chorus of Milan, recorded and transformed
* improvisations by mezzo-soprano Carla Henius
* electronically synthesised sound [[1](https://allthatdust.com/releases/la-fabbrica-illuminata/)]

The text, a montage assembled with the poet Giuliano Scabia, splices fragments of factory contracts and workers' testimony — noise levels, shift conditions, injury — and closes with lines from Cesare Pavese; the live voice ends alone on the words "passeranno i mattini / passeranno le angosce." [[1](https://www.nazioneindiana.com/2008/10/20/la-fabbrica-illuminata-1964/)]

### 📻 Censored Before Its Premiere

The work was commissioned for the opening concert of the 1964 Prix Italia, but RAI's leadership refused to perform it, judging its politicised text offensive to the government — censorship that instantly made the piece notorious. It was premiered instead on 15 September 1964 at the 27th International Festival of Contemporary Music of the Venice Biennale, with Carla Henius as soloist and Nono himself directing the sound. [[1](https://www.luiginono.it/en/works/la-fabbrica-illuminata/), [2](https://allthatdust.com/releases/la-fabbrica-illuminata/)]

### ✊ Politics as Material

Nono, a committed Communist, intended more than protest-by-dedication: he took the studio to the factory floor, treating industrial labour's actual acoustic reality as compositional material and confronting concert audiences with it. The soprano — by turns lyrical, fractured and buried in the tape — stands for the human voice inside industrial noise. The work established the template for Nono's 1960s political electronics, alongside pieces like *Ricorda cosa ti hanno fatto in Auschwitz*, with which it is often recorded. [[1](https://www.soundohm.com/product/la-fabbrica-illuminata-ha)]

### 🧬 Afterlife

A critical edition is published by Ricordi, and the piece remains repertoire: new recordings continue to appear, from the classic Henius-era documents to Sarah Maria Sun's remastered four-channel realisation with the Zurich ICST — evidence that its fusion of documentary sound, electronics and political anger has not dated.
