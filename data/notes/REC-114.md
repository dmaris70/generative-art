Exai is Autechre's eleventh studio album, released digitally by Warp on 7 February 2013 with physical editions following on 5 March [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exai)]. At seventeen tracks and 120 minutes it was the duo's longest album until the NTS Sessions, and it marks the mature public face of "the system" — Sean Booth and Rob Brown's sprawling network of custom software, built largely in Max/MSP, that has driven their work since [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exai)].

### ⚡ The System

Booth and Brown assembled a large interconnected network of software patches and synthesisers, primarily built in Max, and recorded by playing and steering it [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exai)].

* The duo were explicit that the process is not fully generative: a human must "make changes and direct the system" while a track forms [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exai)].
* They deliberately moved away from MIDI sequencing, which Booth said "wasn't quite what [they] wanted to be doing" [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exai)].
* Most of the material was already two or three years old at release — the album documents an ongoing practice rather than a session [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exai)].

The result sits precisely on this catalogue's theme: algorithmic machinery under continuous expressive supervision.

### 🔊 The Sound

Exai's tracks mutate constantly — beats that shear and re-form, bass that behaves like a physical material, melodies surfacing from granular murk ("bladelores", "vekoS", "deco Loc"). Critics heard both the duo's most forbidding density and, unusually, flashes of their early-90s warmth; The Quietus and musicOMH both reviewed it at length [[1](https://thequietus.com/quietus-reviews/autechre-exai-review/), [2](https://www.musicomh.com/reviews/albums/autechre-exai)].

### 📰 Reception

The album holds a Metacritic score of 80 from 26 reviews — "generally favorable" — with the recurring caveat that two hours of this music is a demand; it reached No. 88 on the UK Albums Chart and No. 15 on the UK Independent chart [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exai)].

### 🧬 Companions and Afterlife

The L-event EP, released later in 2013, was composed at the same time with the same custom system and functions as Exai's coda [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-event)]. The system itself kept evolving — into the elseq series, the NTS Sessions and Autechre's generative live sets — making Exai the pivot where Autechre stopped making tracks and started farming an ecosystem.
