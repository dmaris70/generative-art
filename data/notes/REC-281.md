This entry documents a 2012 concert of Ottoman court music by the Istanbul ensemble Bezmârâ — and it must be said up front that the specific concert is the uncertain part: research turned up no dated record of a 2012 Bezmârâ performance, so the date and programme here rest on the collection's own annotation. What is well documented is the ensemble itself, one of the most serious early-music projects in the Islamic world. [[1](https://www.cini.it/en/events/concert-by-the-ensemble-bezmara/), [2](https://www.last.fm/music/Bezm%C3%A2r%C3%A2/+wiki)]

### 🤝 Karakaya's project

Bezmârâ was founded in Istanbul in 1996 by Fikret Karakaya — kemençe and çeng player, musicologist, writer and instrument maker — as a musicological experiment: to perform 16th–18th-century Ottoman repertoire on the instruments for which it was actually written, rather than on the modernized ensemble of today's Turkish classical music. [[1](https://www.last.fm/music/Bezm%C3%A2r%C3%A2/+wiki), [2](https://educationcity.qa/event/maqamat-of-the-ottoman-court-with-fikret-karakaya)]

### 🎻 Resurrected instruments

The ensemble plays reconstructions of instruments that had disappeared from Turkish practice, some rebuilt from evidence in miniature paintings and poems — several are the only playable examples in existence:

* the **çeng** (open harp, played by Karakaya himself) and the **şehrûd** (great long-necked lute)
* Ottoman **kopuz**, **santur**, **tanbur**, **ney**, **kemençe**, **kanun** and **kudüm**. [[1](https://www.ressources-mcm.com/s/Ibn_Battuta/item/1743), [2](https://www.last.fm/music/Bezm%C3%A2r%C3%A2/+wiki)]

### 📜 Repertoire from three manuscripts

Bezmârâ's programmes draw on the small group of sources that preserve early Ottoman music in notation: the mid-17th-century transcriptions of Ali Ufkî (Wojciech Bobowski, a Polish convert at the Ottoman court), the alphabetic notation of Demetrius Cantemir (c. 1700–1703), and the Kevserî manuscript (c. 1720–1740) — including many pieces attributed to Persian and Azerbaijani composers, the *acemler*. [[1](https://www.cini.it/en/events/concert-by-the-ensemble-bezmara/), [2](https://www.ressources-mcm.com/s/Ibn_Battuta/item/1743)]

### ❓ What remains uncertain

Beyond the founding facts, caution is warranted: the 2012 date, venue and setlist of this recording could not be verified against any published source. The ensemble's documented output — the CDs Splendours of Topkapi and Saz ve Söz, and festival appearances from Paris to Venice — brackets the plausible repertoire (peşrev and semâî cycles in the classical makam system), but this particular event should be treated as attested only by the collection itself.
