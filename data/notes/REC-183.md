Brion Gysin's permutation poems — "I Am That I Am", "Junk Is No Good Baby", "Kick That Habit Man" and their kin, made from around 1959–62 — are among the earliest computer-assisted literary works: short phrases run through every possible word-order and read aloud until language dissolves into incantation. Gysin, painter, poet and inventor (with William S. Burroughs) of the cut-up method's literary application, treated a five-word phrase as a combinatorial system to be exhausted rather than a sentence to be believed. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brion_Gysin), [2](https://elmcip.net/creative-work/permutated-poems-brion-gysin)]

### 🔁 The permutation system

The paradigm case is the divine self-naming from Exodus. Gysin noticed the phrase's asymmetry: "all I had to do was to switch the last two words and it asked a question: 'I Am That, Am I?'" Permuting all five words yields doubt, blasphemy and nonsense from a formula of certainty — the poem is the complete set of orderings, with no order privileged. [[1](https://beats2011.wordpress.com/2011/03/02/brion-gysins-permutation-poem-i-am-that-i-am/), [2](https://glia.ca/conu/digitalPoetics/prehistoric-blog/2008/07/16/1960-brion-gysin-i-am-that-i-am/)]

### ⚡ The computer and the BBC

Around 1960 the Cambridge-trained mathematician Ian Sommerville — also the engineer behind Gysin's Dreamachine — wrote a program to generate and randomize the full permutation set, reportedly 2,420 lines for "I Am That I Am". The results were realized for BBC radio in the programme *The Permutated Poems of Brion Gysin*, with tape techniques layering and speeding the readings. One delicious footnote: the broadcast scored the second-lowest audience-approval rating in the BBC's listener poll, beaten to the bottom only by an Auden/Britten opera. [[1](https://glia.ca/conu/digitalPoetics/prehistoric-blog/2008/07/16/1960-brion-gysin-i-am-that-i-am/), [2](https://elmcip.net/creative-work/permutated-poems-brion-gysin)] Details of the hardware are contested — one source names a Honeywell Series 200, a machine line not actually introduced until the mid-1960s — so the precise machine and date (1960 vs. 1961) should be treated as uncertain. [[1](https://glia.ca/conu/digitalPoetics/prehistoric-blog/2008/07/16/1960-brion-gysin-i-am-that-i-am/)]

### 🔊 On record

Gysin performed the permutations throughout his life, and the recordings circulated posthumously on CD anthologies, notably *Self-Portrait Jumping* (1993, Made to Measure) and *Recordings 1960–81* (1995). This catalogue's attribution of the permutation recordings to Sub Rosa could not be confirmed in the sources consulted, though the Brussels label has anthologized adjacent Gysin/Burroughs tape work. [[1](https://www.discogs.com/release/358602-Brion-Gysin-Self-Portrait-Jumping), [2](https://www.discogs.com/release/636587-Brion-Gysin-Recordings-1960-81)]

### 🧬 Legacy

The permutation poems now stand at the head of the digital-poetry canon — cited as pioneering computer-generated literature decades before the term existed — and their DNA runs through sound poetry, process music and today's generative text art.
