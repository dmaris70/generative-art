Songes ("Dreams", 1979) is a fixed-media work by Jean-Claude Risset, realized at IRCAM in Paris in 1978–79, that folds computer-synthesized sound and processed instrumental recordings into a single continuous journey from the realistic to the dreamlike. IRCAM's own documentation treats it as emblematic of the institute's earliest years of musical production. [[1](https://brahms.ircam.fr/en/analyses/Songes), [2](https://brahms.ircam.fr/en/jean-claude-risset)]

### ⚡ Music V on a PDP-10

Risset — a physicist-composer who had learned digital synthesis with Max Mathews at Bell Labs — served as head of IRCAM's computer department from 1975 to 1979, and Songes was made on the institute's PDP-10 mainframe using the Music V language. Instrumental samples were treated and mixed with purely synthetic material, so that the ear repeatedly loses track of where recording ends and calculation begins. [[1](https://brahms.ircam.fr/en/analyses/Songes), [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Claude_Risset)]

### 🔊 Three scenes of a dream

The IRCAM analysis divides the piece into three sections:

* **Mirages** — recognizable instrumental sounds progressively transformed away from their sources.
* **Cloches** — synthetic, inharmonic bell-objects (a Risset signature) overlaid with spatial effects.
* **Coda** — high frequency contours circling in space above a low pedal tone.

An octatonic (tone–semitone) scale serves as a timbral rather than melodic organizing principle, and the spatial illusions draw on John Chowning's work on moving sound sources. The work exists in quadraphonic form (preferred in concert, where the illusions read fully) and in stereo. [[1](https://brahms.ircam.fr/en/analyses/Songes)]

### 🎧 The illusionist

Risset is the composer most identified with auditory illusion — his endlessly ascending Shepard–Risset glissando is the sonic equivalent of an Escher staircase — and Songes is where that laboratory craft becomes overtly poetic: paradoxical bells, sounds that seem to be several things at once, perception itself as the compositional material. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Claude_Risset), [2](https://csoundjournal.com/issue17/bain_risset_arpeggio.html)]

### 🧬 Legacy

Songes circulated widely on record alongside Passages, Sud and the Computer Suite from Little Boy, and remains a standard reference for how early computer music could sound sensuous rather than schematic. Risset later continued his research career in Marseille; on his death in November 2016 he was mourned as one of the founding figures of computer music. [[1](https://catalog.libraries.psu.edu/catalog/9161458), [2](https://www.thewire.co.uk/news/44612/french-electronic-music-composer-jean-claude-risset-has-died)]
