Breadwoman & Other Tales collects the mid-1980s recordings of Los Angeles performance artist Anna Homler singing in a wholly invented language, set to electronics composed, mixed and engineered by Steve Moshier. First circulated on cassette in 1985 and reissued by Brooklyn's RVNG Intl. on 5 February 2016 (ReRVNG06), it is one of the strangest and most tender documents of the LA performance-art underground — "the collected recordings of a language arising." ### 🍞 The Character

Breadwoman arrived unbidden around 1982, while Homler was driving an ocean-blue Cadillac through Topanga Canyon past a patch of wheat and mustard flowers: she opened her mouth and a stream of rhythmic, melodic sound came out. The figure she built around that voice — an old woman with a face of bread, "a storyteller who exists outside of time" — drew on 1980s LA performance art, DIY punk, cassette-trading culture and esoteric mysticism. Homler had studied performance with Rachel Rosenthal at Otis, where the form became "big enough to contain everything." [[1](https://igetrvng.com/products/breadwoman-anna-homler)]

### 🗣 A Language Arising

The songs — Ee Chê, Oo Nu Dah, Gu She' Na' Di — are sung in Breadwoman's own tongue: not glossolalia exactly, but a stable invented vocabulary of lyrical fragments, hypnotic and melodically pure, which listeners consistently hear as an almost-familiar folk music from nowhere. The language precedes meaning; the voice carries everything. [[1](https://igetrvng.com/products/breadwoman-anna-homler), [2](https://www.popmatters.com/anna-homler-and-steve-moshier-breadwoman-other-tales-2495454378.html)]

### 🎛 Moshier's Frame

Steve Moshier — a composer from LA's minimalist/new-music scene — treated Homler's voice as a sonic element rather than a lead vocal: he sifted her cassette transcriptions for the least song-like material, composed slow-moving analog-electronic settings around it, and engineered the results into pieces that sit somewhere between ambient, fourth-world and art song. The pairing of an oracular voice with patient, synthetic accompaniment is what makes the record feel mythological rather than whimsical. [[1](https://igetrvng.com/products/breadwoman-anna-homler), [2](https://annahomler.com/tag/steve-moshier/)]

### 🧬 Reissue and Afterlife

The 2016 RVNG edition (LP/CD/digital, seven tracks) added restored audio and extensive liner notes and interviews overseen by Andrew Choate, and introduced Breadwoman to a generation raised on vocal experimenters from Meredith Monk to Björk; reviewers placed Homler squarely in that lineage. Homler still performs as Breadwoman, the character having outlived the scene that birthed her.
