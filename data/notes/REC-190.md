It does not appear on the tracklist of Lingual Music, the 2CD Paradigm Discs collection (2007) that gathers essentially all of Greenham's available tape work from 1968–1984 [[1](https://www.forcedexposure.com/Catalog/greenham-lily-lingual-music-2cd/PD.022CD.html)], nor in the Lily Greenham estate's own work listings [[1](https://lilygreenham.org/aaol/voicing/lingual-music)]. Her documented mid-1970s pieces are Relativity (1973–74), Traffic (1975) and 7 Consonants in Space (1975); the catalogue title may be a garbled or alternate designation for one of these. What follows describes the artist and the collection with confidence, and this specific work with none.

### 🗣 Lingual Music

Greenham coined the term "lingual music" in 1973 for compositions in which speech "emerges as music": letters, syllables, words and sentences of her own recorded voice are distended, superimposed, looped and reinterpreted on reel-to-reel tape until language becomes dense musical structure [[1](https://lilygreenham.org/aaol/voicing/lingual-music), [2](https://www.e-flux.com/announcements/584918/lily-greenhaman-art-of-living)]. The work was studio-native by design — built from tape loops and multi-tracking, it could not be performed live [[1](https://lilygreenham.org/aaol/voicing/lingual-music)].

### 🤝 A Life Across the Avant-Gardes

Danish-born and raised in Vienna, Greenham (d. 2001) passed through the Wiener Gruppe's sound-poetry circle in the late 1950s and Paris's Groupe de Recherche d'Art Visuel before settling in London in 1972 [[1](https://lilygreenham.bandcamp.com/album/lingual-music), [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lily_Greenham)]. There she recorded at the BBC Radiophonic Workshop (Relativity, 1973–74) and at Goldsmiths' Electronic Music Studios (Traffic, 1975), winning an electroacoustic prize at the 1975 Bourges festival, and collaborated with musicians including John Tchicai and Barry Guy [[1](https://lilygreenham.org/aaol/voicing/lingual-music), [2](https://lilygreenham.bandcamp.com/album/lingual-music)].

### 📼 The Paradigm Collection

Lingual Music (Paradigm Discs PD 22, 2007) is the posthumous double CD that rescued this body of work: 39 tracks spanning 1968–1984, from solo performance pieces to the major tape compositions, with liner notes by composer Michael Parsons [[1](https://www.forcedexposure.com/Catalog/greenham-lily-lingual-music-2cd/PD.022CD.html), [2](https://lilygreenham.bandcamp.com/album/lingual-music)].
