Reflets is a short musique concrète study — 2 minutes 33 seconds of tape music — composed by Croatian-French composer Ivo Malec at Pierre Schaeffer's Groupe de Recherches Musicales (GRM) in Paris. IRCAM's catalogue dates its composition to 1960, at the end of Malec's first GRM training stage, with a Paris premiere in 1961; the catalogue date of 1961 attached to this recording refers to that premiere/film year. [[1](https://brahms.ircam.fr/en/works/work/47677/), [2](https://ressources.ircam.fr/en/composer/ivo-malec/workcourse)]

### 🎞️ A film's soundtrack

Reflets is bound to the moving image: in 1961 the Polish-French animator Piotr Kamler made an abstract film of the same name for which Malec's study became the sound support, preserved today in the INA archives [[1](https://brahms.ircam.fr/en/works/work/47677/)]. The intermedial pairing — abstract animation with abstract sound — typified the exploratory climate of the early GRM.

### 🔊 Sound and technique

The piece belongs, with *Dahovi* (1961), to Malec's first opus of musique concrète: music assembled on magnetic tape from recorded and transformed sound objects, following Schaeffer's discipline of composing through listening [[1](https://ressources.ircam.fr/en/composer/ivo-malec/workcourse)]. Commentators have singled out the study's poetic quality and an "outstanding fluidity of sound" remarkable for so early a tape work; it was later chosen for INA-GRM's archival CD programme [[1](https://www.soundohm.com/artist/ivo-malec?layout=list)].

### 🤝 Zagreb to Paris

Malec (1925–2019) trained in Zagreb, encountered Schaeffer's studio in the mid-1950s, and settled in Paris in 1959, joining the nascent GRM in 1960 [[1](http://www.cdmc.asso.fr/en/ressources/compositeurs/biographies/malec-ivo-1925-2019)]. Reflets is thus the pivot of his biography: the moment a conservatory-formed Croatian composer remade himself as an electroacoustic pioneer.

### ⚠️ A note on the "concrète-orchestral hybrid" label

The catalogue context describes Reflets as an early concrète-orchestral hybrid; the sources consulted do not support this for the piece itself, which IRCAM lists as fixed-media tape music with no orchestral component [[1](https://brahms.ircam.fr/en/works/work/47677/)]. What is true is that Reflets initiated the dialogue between studio and instrumental writing that became Malec's signature — later works such as *Sigma* (1963) and *Cantate pour elle* genuinely fuse orchestral and electroacoustic thinking, and IRCAM credits him as among the first to achieve that synthesis [[1](https://ressources.ircam.fr/en/composer/ivo-malec/workcourse)]. Read this miniature, then, as the seed of the hybrid rather than the hybrid itself.

### 🧬 Legacy

Malec went on to co-found the GRM's successor structures' teaching mission, professing composition at the Paris Conservatoire (1972–90) and mentoring a generation of French electroacoustic composers, while Kamler's films kept his early tape music circulating far beyond concert halls [[1](http://www.cdmc.asso.fr/en/ressources/compositeurs/biographies/malec-ivo-1925-2019)].
