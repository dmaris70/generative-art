The Long String Instrument (1985) is the first recorded document of Ellen Fullman's room-sized instrument of the same name — dozens of taut metallic wires, anchored to wooden resonators, which the performer plays by walking slowly along them with rosin-coated fingertips. The album was recorded during Fullman's 1985 residency at Het Apollohuis in Eindhoven and issued on the Dutch Apollo label the same year. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellen_Fullman), [2](https://www.superiorviaduct.com/products/ellen-fullman-the-long-string-instrument-lp)]

### ⚡ An Instrument the Size of a Room

Fullman began building the instrument in 1980, and it is inherently site-specific — restrung and retuned for every space.

* **Longitudinal vibration:** instead of plucking or bowing across the string, stroking *along* it excites the longitudinal mode, in which pitch is set by string length, not tension. As Fullman puts it, "the longitudinal mode is solely based on tuning by length; therefore a longer room offers different possibilities for low frequencies." [[1](https://www.kqed.org/arts/11122595/thirty-years-later-ellen-fullmans-the-long-string-instrument-still-resonates)]
* **Scale:** installations have used wires of 70 feet and more; the Apollohuis performance used roughly 20 strings of around 50 feet, dimensions Fullman says fluctuate with the venue. [[1](https://www.superiorviaduct.com/products/ellen-fullman-the-long-string-instrument-lp), [2](https://www.kqed.org/arts/11122595/thirty-years-later-ellen-fullmans-the-long-string-instrument-still-resonates)]
* Because retuning is laborious, Fullman adapts pieces by re-voicing rather than transposing. [[1](https://www.kqed.org/arts/11122595/thirty-years-later-ellen-fullmans-the-long-string-instrument-still-resonates)]

### 🔊 The Sound

Walking the wires produces slowly shifting clouds of harmonics — a massed, organ-like drone with the granular attack of rosin on steel, closer to a choir of overtones than to any conventional string sound. The music unfolds at walking pace, literally: the performer's steps are its tempo.

### 🤝 Apollohuis and Fellow Travellers

Het Apollohuis, Paul Panhuysen's Eindhoven art space, was a hub for sound art and long-string experimentation; composer Arnold Dreyblatt, another investigator of unorthodox string vibration, appears alongside Fullman on the recording. [[1](https://www.kqed.org/arts/11122595/thirty-years-later-ellen-fullmans-the-long-string-instrument-still-resonates), [2](https://www.discogs.com/release/713491-Ellen-Fullman-The-Long-String-Instrument)]

### 🧬 Rediscovery

Long a collector's obscurity, the LP was reissued by Superior Viaduct in 2015 and named The Wire's number-one reissue of that year — recognition of Fullman as a major minimalist voice outside the canonical "big four," and a non-academic woman composer-builder in a field that long overlooked both. [[1](https://www.superiorviaduct.com/products/ellen-fullman-the-long-string-instrument-lp), [2](https://www.kqed.org/arts/11122595/thirty-years-later-ellen-fullmans-the-long-string-instrument-still-resonates)] She has continued developing the instrument for four decades, collaborating with Pauline Oliveros, Keiji Haino and choreographer Deborah Hay.
