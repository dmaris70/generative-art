Borderlands: Wu Man and Master Musicians from the Silk Route is the tenth and final volume of the Music of Central Asia series, co-produced by Smithsonian Folkways and the Aga Khan Music Initiative and released on 29 May 2012 (this catalogue's 2011 date reflects the recording/production period; the announced release was 2012). It stages an unprecedented meeting between the Chinese pipa and the tradition-bearers of Uyghur, Tajik and Hui music along the old Silk Route. [[1](https://the.akdn/en/resources-media/whats-new/news-release/aga-khan-music-initiative-and-smithsonian-folkways-release-%E2%80%9Cborderlands-wu-man-and), [2](https://folkways.si.edu/wu-man-and-master-musicians-from-the-silk-route/music-of-central-asia-vol10-borderlands/world/album/smithsonian)]

### 🤝 The Musicians

Wu Man — the world's foremost pipa virtuoso, a founding member of the Silk Road Ensemble — leads an ensemble of masters:

* **Uyghur:** singer-dutarist Sanubar Tursun; Abdulla Majnun (diltar, dutar, tambur); Hesenjan Tursun (satar); Yasin Yaqup (dap frame drum). [[1](https://the.akdn/en/resources-media/whats-new/news-release/aga-khan-music-initiative-and-smithsonian-folkways-release-%E2%80%9Cborderlands-wu-man-and)]
* **Tajik:** Abduvali Abdurashidov (sato-tanbur) and Sirojiddin Juraev (dutar).
* **Hui:** the vocalist Ma Ersa, from China's Muslim Hui community.

Wu Man said the sessions "made my musical fantasy come true" — she had long imagined blending the pipa with the satar, tambur and dutar. [[1](https://the.akdn/en/resources-media/whats-new/news-release/aga-khan-music-initiative-and-smithsonian-folkways-release-%E2%80%9Cborderlands-wu-man-and)]

### 🌍 Borderlands as Method

The premise is historical: the pipa itself — a pear-shaped, short-necked lute established in China by the Tang era — arrived along the Silk Route from Central and West Asian ancestors. The album treats the Xinjiang–Central Asia borderlands not as a periphery but as the connective tissue between the Chinese classical world and the maqam-based traditions to its west, mixing newly arranged traditional pieces with new compositions, including material drawn from Kazakh song. [[1](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/arts-culture/listen-now-wu-man-brings-east-and-west-together-in-new-album-98605879/), [2](https://folkways.si.edu/wu-man-kazakh-song/central-asia-world/music/video/smithsonian)]

### 📀 The Package

Like the whole series, Borderlands is documentation as much as album: 14 tracks (72 minutes) with a 48-page colour booklet and a DVD containing two documentary films, a series introduction, an interactive instrument glossary and map. [[1](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/arts-culture/listen-now-wu-man-brings-east-and-west-together-in-new-album-98605879/)]

### 🧬 Closing a Series

Launched in 2006, the Music of Central Asia series set a modern standard for collaborative ethnographic release-making — musician-led, lavishly documented, aimed at living tradition rather than preservationism. Ending it with a cross-border encounter rather than a single national tradition was the point: the series' argument, in one record. [[1](https://the.akdn/en/resources-media/whats-new/news-release/aga-khan-music-initiative-and-smithsonian-folkways-release-%E2%80%9Cborderlands-wu-man-and)]
