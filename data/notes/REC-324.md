Labyrinthitis (2008) by Danish sound artist Jacob Kirkegaard is a composition made entirely from sounds generated inside the human ear — and engineered so that, at the right listening level, the listener's own ears answer back with tones of their own. Commissioned by the Medical Museion in Copenhagen and premiered in its old surgical auditorium, it was released on CD by Touch (Tone 35) in 2008. It may be the only record in this collection that physically completes itself inside your head. [[1](https://touch33.net/catalogue/tone-35-jacob-kirkegaard-labyrinthitis.html), [2](https://fonik.dk/works/Labyrinthitis.html)]

### 👂 Otoacoustic Emissions

The piece rests on a real audiological phenomenon: the healthy cochlea is not a passive receiver but an active amplifier that emits sound.

* **DPOAE:** when two pure tones at a suitable frequency ratio enter the ear, the inner ear itself generates a third tone (a distortion-product otoacoustic emission) — related to the "Tartini tone" combination tones known to violinists since the 18th century. [[1](https://fonik.dk/works/Labyrinthitis.html)]
* **Recording the ear:** Kirkegaard had tiny probe microphones inserted into his own ear canals — working with audiology researchers — and recorded the faint tones his ears produced in response to stimulus frequencies. [[1](https://fonik.dk/works/Labyrinthitis.html), [2](https://www.tokafi.com/news/jacob-kirkegaard-labyrinthitis-makes-you-hear-your-ear/)]

### 🔁 A Score for the Audience's Cochleae

The composition arranges these ear-generated tones in slowly descending pairs whose intervals are chosen precisely so that they, in turn, evoke distortion products in the ears of the audience. Listeners literally hear tones that are not on the disc — each cochlea contributes its own, slightly different, third voice. The work thereby demonstrates that one can "hear hearing," and dismantles the assumption that listening is passive. [[1](https://fonik.dk/works/Labyrinthitis.html), [2](https://medium.com/@manuelarturoabreu/hearing-hearing-a-review-of-jacob-kirkegaards-labyrinthitis-2008-cf83535a6f0e)]

### 🏛️ Installation and Release

At the Medical Museion the piece was staged with 16 speakers on rods of varying heights, spiralling up beneath the dome of the former operating theatre — a nod to the cochlea's own spiral. The Touch CD edition carries the concert version, with notes explaining the science; the title names an inner-ear disorder, placing the work on the uneasy line between wonder and pathology. [[1](https://fonik.dk/works/Labyrinthitis.html), [2](https://touch33.net/catalogue/tone-35-jacob-kirkegaard-labyrinthitis.html)]

### 🧬 Context

Kirkegaard, a longtime Touch artist, has built a career recording inaccessible vibratory worlds — Chernobyl rooms, Icelandic geysers, melting ice. Labyrinthitis remains his conceptual landmark, later exhibited internationally (including ACCA, Melbourne), and is widely cited in sound-studies literature on the active ear. [[1](https://acca.melbourne/program/jacob-kirkegaard-labyrinthitis/), [2](https://jacobkirkegaardreleases.bandcamp.com/album/labyrinthitis)]
