Bird and Person Dyning (1975) is a performance work by American experimental composer Alvin Lucier for a solo performer wearing binaural microphones, moving through a space containing loudspeakers and an electronic birdcall. Documented on LP by the Italian label Cramps in 1976, it is one of Lucier's signature demonstrations that acoustic phenomena — here feedback and heterodyning — can themselves be the music [[1](https://alvinluciercramps.bandcamp.com/album/bird-and-person-dyning), [2](https://dieordiy2.blogspot.com/2020/02/alvin-lucier-bird-and-person-dyning.html)].

### 🎧 The apparatus

* A **Sennheiser binaural microphone** — two miniature capsules worn in the performer's own ears, capturing sound as heard at the head [[1](https://alvinluciercramps.bandcamp.com/album/bird-and-person-dyning)].
* An **electronic birdcall**: a chirping Christmas-tree ornament, hidden in the space [[1](https://alvinluciercramps.bandcamp.com/album/bird-and-person-dyning), [2](https://issueprojectroom.org/video/alvin-lucier-bird-and-person-dyning)].
* Amplifiers and stereo loudspeakers, turned up until the ear-microphones and speakers form a feedback loop.

The performer's head becomes a movable microphone stand: tilting or stepping changes the feedback path, and thus the pitch.

### 🔁 What "dyning" means

The title abbreviates **heterodyning**, a term from early radio for two waves mixing in a nonlinear medium to produce new signals at their sum and difference frequencies [[1](https://alvinluciercramps.bandcamp.com/album/bird-and-person-dyning), [2](https://neural.it/2022/10/alvin-lucier-bird-and-person-dyning/)]. When the birdcall's chirps beat against the feedback strands, difference tones arise — partly in the air, partly generated inside the listener's own ears. Listeners hear phantom birds: ghost chirps at other pitches, a chorus that no microphone in the room is producing [[1](https://alvinluciercramps.bandcamp.com/album/bird-and-person-dyning)].

### 🚶 The performance

The piece is a slow hunt. The performer walks and turns with extreme deliberateness, steering feedback strands toward the birdcall's frequencies to summon the phantom flock, then letting them dissolve. Nothing is notated; the score is a set of conditions, and every room yields a different piece.

### 🧬 Legacy

The Cramps LP (in the nova musicha series) fixed one realization on record, and the work has stayed in live circulation — including latter-day performances by vocalist Elaine Mitchener [[1](https://lux.org.uk/event/jenny-brady-alvin-luciers-bird-person-dyning-elaine-mitchener/)] and Lucier's own late performances at venues like ISSUE Project Room [[2](https://issueprojectroom.org/video/alvin-lucier-bird-and-person-dyning)]. Alongside I Am Sitting in a Room, it is a purest-form statement of Lucier's career-long subject: the listener's body as the final, indispensable piece of audio equipment.
