Rhythms of Rapture: Sacred Musics of Haitian Vodou (1995) is a 20-track survey produced by ethnomusicologist Elizabeth McAlister for Smithsonian Folkways (SFW 40464), released in association with the UCLA Fowler Museum's landmark exhibition *Sacred Arts of Haitian Vodou*. It sets field recordings from live Vodou ceremonies beside studio performances by Haiti's leading roots-music bands — a two-way portrait of a religion and the popular music it feeds. [[1](https://folkways.si.edu/rhythms-of-rapture-sacred-musics-of-haitian-vodou/caribbean-world/music/album/smithsonian), [2](https://www.discogs.com/release/1731052-Various-Rhythms-Of-Rapture-Sacred-Musics-Of-Haitian-Vodou)]

### 🥁 Sequenced by regleman

The album's structure is itself liturgical: tracks follow the basic *regleman* (rules) of a Vodou service, guiding the listener through the proper "line" of spirit songs — from the "cool" **Rada** rites, through the songs for the Ogou warrior spirits, into the "hot" **Petwo** and **Banda** rhythms, then to **Bizango** (secret society) material and finally **Rara**, the processional music of the streets. [[1](https://folkways.si.edu/rhythms-of-rapture-sacred-musics-of-haitian-vodou/caribbean-world/music/album/smithsonian)] Rada and Petwo are the two great rhythmic families of Vodou drumming — cool/ancestral and hot/fiery respectively — and hearing them in ritual order is the album's pedagogical masterstroke.

### 🔊 The sound

Kreyòl call-and-response song, batteries of hand drums, the conch shell (lanbi), bells, gourd rattles and clay pots from ceremonial settings sit alongside electric guitars and synthesizers from the bands — the point being continuity, not contrast: the sacred rhythms are the engine in both. [[1](https://xpn.org/2021/01/10/haitian-vodou/)]

### 🕌 The tradition documented

Vodou is a ceremonial religion in which drumming, song and dance serve the lwa (spirits); its rhythms are functional — built to invite the spirits' presence, culminating in possession-trance, the "rapture" of the title. The album treats this respectfully as *music with a job*, annotated by McAlister's scholarship rather than exoticized. [[1](https://folkways.si.edu/rhythms-of-rapture-sacred-musics-of-haitian-vodou/caribbean-world/music/album/smithsonian)]

### 🤝 Ceremony and stage

The commercial tracks come from the mizik rasin (roots music) movement — Boukman Eksperyans, RAM, Rara Machine, Boukan Ginen — bands that in the late 1980s and 1990s carried Rada and Petwo rhythms into festival and protest contexts. Their inclusion makes the argument that Vodou's sacred musics are living, urban and political, not folkloric residue. [[1](https://folkways.si.edu/rhythms-of-rapture-sacred-musics-of-haitian-vodou/caribbean-world/music/album/smithsonian), [2](https://www.discogs.com/release/1731052-Various-Rhythms-Of-Rapture-Sacred-Musics-Of-Haitian-Vodou)]

### 🧬 Significance

Released amid the mid-1990s reassessment of Haitian art and religion, it remains a standard teaching record for Caribbean sacred music and one of Folkways' most cited surveys — a model of how to document a possession religion on disc: by its own rules, in its own order. [[1](https://folkways.si.edu/rhythms-of-rapture-sacred-musics-of-haitian-vodou/caribbean-world/music/album/smithsonian)]
