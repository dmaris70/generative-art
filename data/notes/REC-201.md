Speech Songs (1972) is a cycle of four short songs by American composer Charles Dodge, realized at Bell Laboratories on poems by Mark Strand — among the first music ever made by analyzing and resynthesizing the human voice by computer [[1](https://www.newworldrecords.org/products/charles-dodge-synthesized-voices), [2](https://deathisnot.bandcamp.com/album/synthesized-voices)]. Its wobbling, elastic, half-human computer voice founded a whole lineage of synthetic-vocal music.

### ⚡ After hours at Bell Labs

Dodge worked at Bell Labs in the evenings, after hours, using mainframe software developed for speech research — specifically the speech-synthesis system of researcher Joseph Olive, who had a musical background and actively helped adapt the tools for composition [[1](https://www.furious.com/perfect/ohm/dodge.html), [2](https://deathisnot.bandcamp.com/album/synthesized-voices)]. The pieces are thus a genuine crossover artifact: telephone-industry research infrastructure moonlighting as a musical instrument.

### 🗣 Analysis and resynthesis

The method was synthesis-by-analysis:

* Readings of Strand's poems were recorded, then computationally analyzed into parameters (pitch, timing, spectral envelope).
* Dodge then edited those parameters — stretching syllables, bending pitch contours into melody, making the voice leap registers no throat could manage — and resynthesized the result [[1](https://deathisnot.bandcamp.com/album/synthesized-voices), [2](https://nwr-site-liner-notes.s3.amazonaws.com/nwcrl348.pdf)].

Speech slides continuously into song and back: the border between the two is precisely the subject of the work.

### 📜 The four songs

The movements set four short, surreal Strand texts: "A man sitting in the cafeteria," "The days are ahead," "When I am with you," and "He destroyed her image" [[1](https://music7703lsu.wordpress.com/2017/04/18/speech-songs-by-charles-dodge/)]. The register is ironic, deadpan and funny — "The days are ahead / 1,926,346 to 1,926,345" — the machine voice's glassy affect amplifying the poems' absurdism [[2](https://www.newworldrecords.org/products/charles-dodge-synthesized-voices)].

### 🧬 Legacy

Speech Songs circulated on LP through CRI and is anthologized on the collection Synthesized Voices (now in the New World Records catalogue, with a vinyl reissue via Death Is Not The End) [[1](https://www.newworldrecords.org/products/charles-dodge-synthesized-voices), [2](https://deathisnot.bandcamp.com/album/synthesized-voices)]. Half a century on, its warped vocal plasticity sounds uncannily like a prophecy of vocoder pop, Auto-Tune and neural voice synthesis — but arrived at one FORTRAN batch job at a time, when a few seconds of song cost hours of mainframe computation.
