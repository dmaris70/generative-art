This entry documents the Xinjiang Uyghur Muqam Ensemble's contribution to Smithsonian Folkways' Silk Road survey recordings — state-ensemble performances from the **Twelve Muqam (On Ikki Muqam)**, the monumental classical repertory of the Uyghurs of Central Asia. Folkways' anthology *The Silk Road: A Musical Caravan* and its related Central Asia series carry the ensemble's muqam excerpts, such as "Chabbiyat Tazi Marghul" — a *marghul* being the instrumental double of a vocal piece, same rhythm at a faster tempo and lighter spirit. [[1](https://folkways.si.edu/the-silk-road-a-musical-caravan/central-asia-islamica-world/music/album/smithsonian), [2](https://folkways.si.edu/uyghur-muqam-ensemble/chabbiyat-tazi-marghul/central-asia-islamica-world/music/track/smithsonian)] The catalogue's 2005 date could not be pinned to a specific release in the sources consulted; 2005 is, however, the year UNESCO proclaimed the tradition a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity, and some conflation is possible. [[1](https://ar.unesco.org/silkroad/node/3085)]

### 🕌 The Twelve Muqam

The On Ikki Muqam is one of the great suite traditions of the Islamic world: twelve suites whose movements total roughly 360 distinct compositions — over twenty hours of music — combining sung classical poetry, instrumental interludes and dance sections, carried by voices with satar and tämbur long-necked lutes, dutar, and dap frame drum. Historically transmitted orally by master musicians, it was codified in the twentieth century, most famously from the memory of the singer Turdi Akhun in the 1950s. [[1](http://www.silkroadfoundation.org/newsletter/vol3num1/3_uyghur.php), [2](https://ar.unesco.org/silkroad/node/3085)]

### 🎙️ The recording and its ambiguities

The performing body here is a professional, state-sponsored ensemble — muqam as concert repertoire, arranged and rehearsed, rather than village or Sufi practice. Scholars have noted the ambiguity of Chinese "Silk Roadism": the same state apparatus that canonized and staged the Twelve Muqam also controls how, and by whom, it may be performed. [[1](http://www.silkroadfoundation.org/newsletter/vol3num1/3_uyghur.php)] UNESCO's proclamation (2005) and inscription (2008) recognized the art while binding its official custodianship to Chinese institutions. [[1](https://ar.unesco.org/silkroad/node/3085)]

### 🚨 The tradition's present condition

The current context must be stated factually. Since 2017, mass internment and cultural repression in Xinjiang have struck muqam practitioners directly: singers including Sanubar Tursun and Rashide Dawut were detained in 2019; an estimated 30–40% of Xinjiang Arts Centre employees were sent to camps; and by 2022 the Xinjiang Muqam Research Office was among some 160 Uyghur cultural-research organizations closed, with remaining heritage bodies repurposed for state messaging. Researchers describe official muqam performance today as heritage display decoupled from the community it belongs to. [[1](https://uhrp.org/report/the-complicity-of-heritage-cultural-heritage-and-genocide-in-the-uyghur-region/), [2](https://freemuse.org/news/china-sanubar-tursun-voice-of-the-uyghurs-missing-presumed-detained-in-xinjiangs-internment-camps/), [3](https://stephenjones.blog/2019/10/23/uyghur-culture-crisis/)]

These recordings are therefore doubly valuable: as documentation of a supreme musical tradition, and as testimony from before its bearers' current emergency.
