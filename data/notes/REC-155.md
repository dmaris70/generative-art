Sounds from Dangerous Places is Peter Cusack's book-plus-2CD survey of field recordings made in "sites of major environmental damage" — the Chernobyl exclusion zone, the Caspian oil fields of Azerbaijan, dam projects on the Munzur river in Kurdish Turkey, and UK nuclear and military sites. It was published as an 80-page book with two CDs by ReR Megacorp in co-production with Gruenrekorder and the DAAD's Berlin artists' programme (Gruenrekorder catalogue GrD 25 gives 2013; the ReR edition is commonly dated 2012). [[1](https://www.gruenrekorder.de/?page_id=9070), [2](https://zkm.de/en/event/2013/01/peter-cusack-sounds-from-dangerous-places)]

### 📰 Sonic Journalism

Cusack coined "sonic journalism" for the project: the aural equivalent of photojournalism.

* The premise: all sound, not just speech, carries information about places and events, and listening yields insights "different from, but complementary to, visual images and language." [[1](https://sounds-from-dangerous-places.org/sonic-journalism/)]
* Field recordings are treated as documentary evidence, not raw material for composition — each track in the book is annotated with what was recorded, where and how, alongside photographs. [[1](https://neural.it/2013/02/peter-cusack-sounds-from-dangerous-places/)]

### ☢️ Chernobyl

Disc one (37 tracks, 74 minutes) is devoted to the exclusion zone. The unsettling paradox Cusack documents is that the zone, emptied of people since 1986, *sounds* magnificent — birdsong, frogs, wind in re-wilded villages — a thriving soundscape over invisible contamination. The danger is precisely what cannot be heard. [[1](https://www.gruenrekorder.de/?page_id=9070), [2](https://sonicfield.org/sounds-from-dangerous-places/)]

### 🛢️ Caspian Oil and UK Sites

Disc two (39 tracks) pairs Azerbaijan's oil fields — pumps, mud volcanoes, pipeline towns on the Caspian — with British sites: Sellafield, Dungeness, Bradwell and Sizewell nuclear stations, and Thetford Forest beside USAF air bases. [[1](https://www.gruenrekorder.de/?page_id=9070), [2](https://neural.it/2013/02/peter-cusack-sounds-from-dangerous-places/)]

### 🧬 Influence

Cusack — a London improviser, long-time member of CRiSAP (Creative Research into Sound Arts Practice) and instigator of the "Favourite Sounds" projects — gave field recording a documentary ethics distinct from soundscape aestheticism. Sonic journalism has since become a reference point for recordists addressing climate catastrophe and extraction zones, and Cusack has continued the Dangerous Places series with work on Berlin, Beijing and beyond. [[1](https://newint.org/features/2019/10/16/mixedmedia-spotlight-peter-cusack), [2](https://sounds-from-dangerous-places.org/)]
