Hétérozygote (1963–64) is a tape piece by Luc Ferrari, co-founder of the Groupe de Recherches Musicales (GRM) in Paris, composed between December 1963 and March 1964. It is the work that broke musique concrète's taboo on recognizability: instead of abstracting recorded sounds beyond identification, Ferrari let field recordings remain audibly *themselves* — voices, places, weather — inaugurating what he called "anecdotal music". [[1](http://lucferrari.com/en/analyses-reflexion/heterozygote/), [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luc_Ferrari)]

### 🔊 Anecdotal music

Schaeffer's GRM orthodoxy asked listeners to hear sounds "reduced" — as pure sonic objects, sources bracketed out. Ferrari went the other way.

* He exploited the referential character of recorded sound to reintroduce a narrative, dramatic dimension previously absent from musique concrète. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luc_Ferrari)]
* In his own formulation: if the organisation of events is purely musical, their *choice* suggests situations — the piece operates "on the musical and on the dramatic plane" at once. [[1](http://lucferrari.com/en/analyses-reflexion/heterozygote/)]
* The title is borrowed from biology: heterozygous — of mixed heredity. The piece's genes are half music, half world. [[1](http://lucferrari.com/en/analyses-reflexion/heterozygote/)]

### 🎬 Structure: scenes and interludes

Hétérozygote is built as eight "scenes", separated (or not) by interludes — and Ferrari notes the interludes are also scenes, and the scene titles optional. The listener is handed something like a film for the ears with the plot withheld: concrete situations, suggestively ordered, never explained. [[1](http://lucferrari.com/en/analyses-reflexion/heterozygote/)]

### 🌍 The sound

Studio-made electroacoustic material coexists with unmistakably real captures — outdoor spaces, crowds, fragments of speech. The drama comes from the friction between the two registers: abstract passages suddenly opening onto a recognizable world and closing again. The piece circulates today primarily via the GRM recording reissued as *Hétérozygote / Petite symphonie intuitive pour un paysage de printemps*. [[1](https://www.soundohm.com/product/heterozygote-petite-symph), [2](https://lucferrari.bandcamp.com/album/h-t-rozygote-petite-symphonie)]

### 🧬 Legacy

Hétérozygote is the hinge between classical musique concrète and everything we now call field-recording-based composition. It leads directly to Ferrari's Presque rien series — where a whole daybreak on a beach becomes the piece — and stands behind soundscape composition, W. G. Sebald-style audio documentary hybrids, and much ambient and phonographic practice since. Ferrari (1929–2005) remains the great humanist of the GRM generation: the one who let the world stay in the tape. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luc_Ferrari), [2](http://lucferrari.com/en/analyses-reflexion/heterozygote/)]
