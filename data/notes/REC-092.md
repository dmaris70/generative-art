Anagram for Strings is Yasunao Tone's first graphic score, made in Tokyo in 1961 — years before he emigrated to New York — and published by George Maciunas's Fluxus Editions in 1963. The realization catalogued here is by Petr Kotik's S.E.M. Ensemble, the New York group long associated with Tone's music. It matters as one of the earliest works in Tone's lifelong project of converting visual and textual material into sound by impersonal rules — the project that later produced his "wounded CD" pieces. [[1](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/127570), [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasunao_Tone)]

### 🎼 The score

The score, held by MoMA, is a single sheet of ink and typewriting on paper (about 8 1/4 × 11 5/8 in.), populated with small white and black circles and dots, framed by random whole numbers — positive and negative — along its top and left edges. [[1](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/127570)]

### 🎲 How it is played

The system is arithmetic, not expressive:

* The performer draws a line across the score; where it intersects the field of circles, calculations using the edge numbers determine how a series of downward glissandi is to be performed. [[1](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/127570)]
* The sizes of the black and white circles correspond to the length of each glissando; how the sounds are actually produced is left to the player. [[1](https://www.artnews.com/art-news/news/yasunao-tone-fluxus-dead-1234742202/)]

### 🤝 Fluxus circulation

Tone was a central figure of the Tokyo avant-garde (Group Ongaku) whose scores travelled west: Maciunas's publication of Anagram for Strings helped build his international reputation, and the piece was performed during the first Fluxus festival tour of Europe in 1962. [[1](https://www.artnews.com/art-news/news/yasunao-tone-fluxus-dead-1234742202/), [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasunao_Tone)]

### ❓ What is uncertain

Details of this specific realization are thin, and the catalogue's "1991" date could not be confirmed. The documented S.E.M. Ensemble recording — issued on the split LP *Anagram for Strings / Geography & Music* — is described on Discogs as re-recorded from a documentation video of a performance at Willow Place Auditorium, Brooklyn, on 4 March 1993, running 9:50. [[1](https://www.discogs.com/release/748321-Yasunao-Tone-Anagram-For-Strings-Geography-Music)] Either an earlier 1991 S.E.M. performance exists that we have not traced, or the catalogue date is simply off by two years; we flag rather than guess. Tone continued to revisit the piece late in life, pairing with hardanger-fiddle player Cleek Schrey on *Anagram* material at Roulette. [[1](https://roulette.org/event/cleek-schrey/)] Tone died in 2025, aged 90. [[1](https://www.artnews.com/art-news/news/yasunao-tone-fluxus-dead-1234742202/)]
