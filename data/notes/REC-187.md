Just (1972) is a four-and-a-half-minute text-sound composition by Charles Amirkhanian — a piece of music made entirely from spoken words, treated as percussion. It opens *10 + 2: 12 American Text Sound Pieces*, the anthology Amirkhanian produced that effectively defined the American wing of the genre, and it remains his most-cited early work. [[1](https://othermindsrecords.bandcamp.com/album/10-2-12-american-text-sound-pieces), [2](https://www.thewholenote.com/index.php/booksrecords2/moderncontempo/26896-lexical-music-charles-amirkhanian)]

### 🔁 Words as rhythm

In text-sound composition the voice — live, recorded, or both — percussively intones decontextualized words and phrases; pitch, semantics and syntax give way to rhythm and timbre. Just runs a tiny stock of unrelated words (this catalogue lists them as "rainbow, chug, bandit, bomb"; a review of Amirkhanian's work quotes the assemblage "rainbow chug bandit") through overlapping tape loops, the words colliding and re-aligning into engaging rhythmic processes — plainly cousin to Steve Reich's contemporaneous speech-loop phasing, but comic, dry and word-loving where Reich is ecstatic. Sources describing the exact loop construction are thin; the piece is best verified by ear against the 10 + 2 recording (4:33, ℗ 1973 Arts Plural). [[1](https://www.thewholenote.com/index.php/booksrecords2/moderncontempo/26896-lexical-music-charles-amirkhanian), [2](https://othermindsrecords.bandcamp.com/album/10-2-12-american-text-sound-pieces)]

### 📻 The KPFA connection

Amirkhanian composed from inside radio: he was music director of Berkeley's KPFA-FM (1969–1992), where his *Ode to Gravity* programs broadcast sound poetry and tape music to the Bay Area — a December 1972 broadcast already featured his own sound poems. In 1972 he also encountered the European scene at Stockholm's text-sound festivals, importing the Swedish coinage "text-sound composition" into American usage. [[1](https://archive.org/details/OTG_1972_12_06), [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Amirkhanian), [3](https://www.musicandliterature.org/features/2017/2/2/restless-minimalism-lexical-music-and-the-text-sound-compositions-of-charles-amirkhanian)]

### 💿 10 + 2: the genre's best-seller

The anthology — Robert Ashley, John Cage, Clark Coolidge, Beth Anderson and others alongside Amirkhanian — was recorded in Dolby at 1750 Arch Street, produced by Amirkhanian in November 1974, and released in January 1975 as 1750 Arch Records S-1752. Billed as "the best-selling recording in the history of American sound poetry," it was a genuine novelty: unpitched speech was then rarely treated as performance material outside literary circles. Other Minds reissued it on CD in 2004. [[1](https://othermindsrecords.bandcamp.com/album/10-2-12-american-text-sound-pieces)]

### 🧬 Afterlife

Just seeded the mature loop-works collected on *Lexical Music* (1979) — "Dutiful Ducks," "Mahogany Ballpark," "Seatbelt Seatbelt" — and a practice critics have called "restless minimalism." Amirkhanian's double life as composer and broadcaster made him the great connector of American experimental music, later co-founding the Other Minds festival.
