This Nonesuch Explorer LP, recorded on 10 January 1971 by ethnomusicologist Robert E. Brown in the reception hall of the Pura Paku Alaman — one of the two royal courts of Yogyakarta, Java — documents the palace gamelan under the direction of K.R.T. Wasitodipuro (later Wasitodiningrat, also known as K.P.H. Notoprojo), among the most revered Javanese musicians of the twentieth century. The session was made by permission of Prince Paku Alam VIII for a radio broadcast honouring his birthday; sparrows nesting in the hall are audible on the recording. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_Court_Gamelan), [2](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~lingfeng/bn_pages/java-javanese-court-gamelan.html)]

### 🎼 An heirloom instrument

The court's gamelan was forged in 1755 for Paku Alam I's line — a double set spanning Java's two tuning systems: the sléndro half named Kyai Pengawé Sari ("Sir Invitation to Beauty") and the pélog half Kyai Telaga Muntjar ("Sir Lake and Fountain"). Tracks alternate between the two: Ketawang Puspawarna and the Gending Mandulpati suite in sléndro; the Gending Tejanata suite and Bubaran Hudan Mas in pélog. Three pesindhen (female vocalists) — Nyi Djiworetno, Niken Larasati and Nyi Tasri — sing with the ensemble. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_Court_Gamelan)]

### 🕌 The tradition: cycles and pathet

Central Javanese court gamelan is colotomic music: time is organized in nested cycles marked by gongs of decreasing size, the great gong ageng closing each cycle while kenong, kempul and kethuk subdivide it. Form names on this record — ketawang, ladrang, gending, bubaran — denote specific cycle lengths and gong patterns. Within the cycle, the balungan (skeletal melody) on metallophones is elaborated heterophonically by rebab, gambang, gender and voice, all governed by pathet, the modal system that constrains melodic gravity within each tuning. Soft-style pieces like Puspawarna showcase this layered refinement; loud-style pieces like the processional Hudan Mas ("Golden Rain") the ensemble's ceremonial power.

### 🚀 To interstellar space

Brown's recording of Ketawang Puspawarna ("Kinds of Flowers") — a piece associated with the Paku Alam himself — was selected for the Voyager Golden Record in 1977, making this performance one of humanity's ambassadors beyond the solar system [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_Court_Gamelan)].

### 🧬 Afterlife

Issued as the Explorer series was opening Western ears to court musics worldwide, the album (reissued on CD in 1991 and remastered as *Java: Court Gamelan* in 2003) became a standard reference for gamelan's Western reception [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_Court_Gamelan), [2](https://music.apple.com/us/album/explorer-series-indonesia-java-court-gamelan/128049636)]. Wasitodipuro himself later taught at CalArts, directly seeding the American gamelan movement and the minimalists' engagement with cyclic time.
