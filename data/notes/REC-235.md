Nothin To Look At Just A Record (India Navigation, 1982) is the recording debut of Phill Niblock (1933–2024) — remarkably, the first release by a composer then nearly fifty and already two decades into building one of minimalism's most uncompromising bodies of work. Two side-long trombone drones, its shrugging title is the aesthetic: no image, no event, just sustained sound. Alan Licht placed it at No. 5 in his famous Minimal Top Ten. ### 🎺 Two Trombone Pieces

The album comprises "A Trombone Piece" and "A Third Trombone." A note on the credit in this catalogue: the trombonist on "A Trombone Piece" was James Fulkerson, recorded by Richard Lainhart and Richard Kelly at SUNY Albany in the mid-1970s; Jon English is credited as the trombonist for "A Third Trombone," whose source tones were recorded at India Navigation in New York — so English is on the record, but not alone. Sources conflict slightly on these credits, as the original LP documentation is sparse. [[1](https://www.superiorviaduct.com/products/phill-niblock-nothin-to-look-at-just-a-record-lp), [2](https://www.allmusic.com/song/a-third-trombone-for-taped-trombone-mt0000415929)]

### 🔁 The Niblock Method

Niblock's system, constant from the late 1960s until his death, is audible here in early maturity.

* **Long tones only:** instrumentalists record sustained single pitches, specified by Niblock to the hertz.
* **Microtonal stacking:** the tones are multitracked in close, deliberately "out-of-tune" clusters, so that beat frequencies and combination tones shimmer between them — the music happens in the air, not on the tape. [[1](https://www.superiorviaduct.com/products/phill-niblock-nothin-to-look-at-just-a-record-lp)]
* **No breath, no rhythm:** breathing pauses were spliced out, unraveling the drones spatially rather than metrically — an endless exhalation no trombonist could produce. [[1](https://www.forcedexposure.com/Catalog/niblock-phill-nothin-to-look-at-just-a-record-lp/SV.033LP.html)]

Played loud — as Niblock insisted — the overtones move as the listener moves, making the room itself the instrument.

### 🏠 Experimental Intermedia

Niblock ran Experimental Intermedia from his Centre Street loft in Lower Manhattan, presenting hundreds of concerts from 1973 onward and hosting his own annual winter-solstice marathon; India Navigation, the jazz-associated label that took this record, sat in the same downtown ecosystem. His parallel career as filmmaker (*The Movement of People Working*) meant drones usually screened with images — this record's title winks at their absence. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phill_Niblock)]

### 🧬 Afterlife

Long out of print and legendary, the album was reissued on vinyl by Superior Viaduct in 2013 for Niblock's 80th birthday, cementing its status as the entry point to his catalogue. [[1](https://www.superiorviaduct.com/products/phill-niblock-nothin-to-look-at-just-a-record-lp)]
