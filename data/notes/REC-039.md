Audible Ecosystemics n.2 (Feedback Study) is a live-electronics work by Italian composer Agostino Di Scipio in which a room, a handful of microphones, loudspeakers and a computer are wired into a single self-regulating feedback organism — the machine listens to what the space does to its own output and adjusts itself accordingly. It is a central statement of Di Scipio's "ecosystemic" approach, in which composing means designing interactions rather than writing sounds. [[1](https://usoproject.blogspot.com/2010/01/agostino-di-scipio-ecosystemic-sound.html), [2](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/07494467.2014.906673)]

### 📅 A note on the date

The catalogue date of 1997 appears to be inaccurate. Every source consulted places the Audible Ecosystemics series in 2002–05; documented realizations of n.2a (Feedback Study) were recorded on 29 August 2003 at the church of Santa Caterina and Istituto Gramma, L'Aquila, and of n.2b (with vocal resonances) on 30 June 2004 at Johannes Gutenberg University, Mainz [[1](https://www.soundohm.com/product/horbare-okosysteme-live-e)]. The likeliest source of confusion is Di Scipio's *5 interazioni cicliche alle differenze sensibili* (1997–98), an earlier real-time processing work on the same CD.

### 🔁 The mechanism

There is no score of notes and no performer input in the usual sense. Instead:

* A sine-tone or the room's own ambience excites acoustic feedback between microphones and loudspeakers.
* Digital signal-processing algorithms "listen to their own results, and then modify themselves accordingly, during the course of the piece" — extracted features of the sound drive the gains and transformations that generate the next moment of sound. [[1](https://www.soundohm.com/product/horbare-okosysteme-live-e)]
* Because runaway feedback is a real danger, self-regulating protection mechanisms are composed into the system itself: the piece survives by continuously damping its own excesses.

### 🌍 The room as instrument

Di Scipio insists the loop is not man–machine but a "triangular, ecosystemic connection, man/ambience/machine": the hall's resonances, background noise, even the bodies of listeners physically alter the acoustics and therefore the piece's behaviour [[1](https://usoproject.blogspot.com/2010/01/agostino-di-scipio-ecosystemic-sound.html)]. Every room performs the work differently; the composition is the network of dependencies, not any particular sounding result. He has described this as replacing "interactive music" — where a machine reacts to a player — with a system whose interactions are internal and environmental [[1](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/234825300_Dynamic_Networks_of_Sonic_Interactions_An_Interview_with_Agostino_Di_Scipio)].

### 🧬 Significance

The series has become a touchstone for sound artists and scholars thinking about emergence, cybernetics and post-digital performance, the subject of a dedicated 2014 issue of Contemporary Music Review [[1](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/07494467.2014.906673)] and of continuing new realizations by other performers [[2](https://redshiftmusicsociety.bandcamp.com/track/audible-ecosystemics-n-3b)]. It stands with Lucier's wire and Cage's chance systems as a landmark of music made by setting a process in motion and letting it find its own equilibrium.
