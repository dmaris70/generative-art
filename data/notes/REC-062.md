Réak (1966) is a thirteen-minute work for large orchestra by the Korean-born composer Isang Yun, premiered on 23 October 1966 at the Donaueschingen Festival by the Sinfonieorchester des Südwestfunks under Ernest Bour. The title names the ritual music of Korean royal ancestral ceremonies, and the piece translates that court-music sonority into the language of the European avant-garde — the performance that made Yun's international reputation overnight. [[1](https://www.boosey.com/cr/music/Isang-Yun-Reak/1795), [2](https://yun-gesellschaft.de/en/isang-yun-en/biography/)]

### 🕌 Court Music Inside the Orchestra

Yun, who had studied Korean traditional music before emigrating to West Berlin, modelled Réak on *aak*, the slow, ornament-saturated refined court music of Korea. Boosey & Hawkes' note describes a work built on a twelve-tone series but governed less by serial logic than by the gestural language of that tradition: slowly unfolding sound-bands of hallucinatory density rather than themes and development. [[1](https://www.boosey.com/composer/Isang+Yun), [2](https://www.boosey.com/cr/music/Isang-Yun-Reak/1795)]

* The percussion section imports the Korean **bak** (a wooden clapper that marks ritual sections) and Thai gongs into an otherwise Western orchestra of winds, brass, four percussionists, harps and strings. [[1](https://www.boosey.com/cr/music/Isang-Yun-Reak/1795)]

### 🔊 The Hauptton Technique

Réak is a showcase of Yun's *Haupttontechnik* (main-tone technique): each musical unit is anchored on a single central tone, surrounded by appoggiaturas, vibrato, accents, glissandi and after-notes — the tone treated as a living, breathing event, as in Korean instrumental practice, rather than as a point in a harmonic argument. [[1](https://aboutisangyun.weebly.com/main-tone-technique.html), [2](https://www.lapl.org/collections-resources/blogs/lapl/korean-composer-yun-isang)]

### 🌍 Donaueschingen and After

The 1966 Donaueschingen premiere was Yun's breakthrough into the first rank of the avant-garde: a Korean ritual sound-world presented, without exoticism, at new music's most prestigious festival. [[1](https://yun-gesellschaft.de/en/isang-yun-en/biography/)] Barely a year later Yun was abducted from West Berlin by the South Korean secret service in the 1967 "East Berlin incident," imprisoned and sentenced, and released after an international campaign by fellow composers — a rupture that made him both a major composer and a political symbol. [[1](https://yun-gesellschaft.de/en/isang-yun-en/biography/)]
