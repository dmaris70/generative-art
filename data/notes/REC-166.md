(No Pussyfooting) (1973) is the debut collaboration between King Crimson guitarist Robert Fripp and Roxy Music synthesist Brian Eno, released by Island Records in November 1973: two side-long instrumentals built from electric guitar fed through a twin reel-to-reel tape-delay system [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/(No_Pussyfooting))]. It is the founding document of what would be nicknamed Frippertronics, and a direct ancestor of ambient music.

### 🔁 The twin-Revox system

Eno's studio rig ran a single loop of tape between two Revox reel-to-reel machines: the first recorded Fripp's guitar, the tape travelled to the second machine which played it back seconds later, and that output was fed back into the first machine's input [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frippertronics), [2](https://happymag.tv/frippertronics/)]. Each pass layers a new line over the slowly decaying accumulation, so a solo guitarist becomes an orchestra of his own past playing. Fripp later toured the system under the name Frippertronics, demonstrating it on television in 1979 [[3](https://www.guitarplayer.com/gear/watch-looping-innovator-robert-fripps-1979-frippertronics-tv-demonstration)].

### 🎸 Two sessions, a year apart

* **"The Heavenly Music Corporation"** (side one) was recorded 8 September 1972 at Eno's home studio — Fripp improvising over the accumulating loop while Eno ran the machines [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/(No_Pussyfooting))].
* **"Swastika Girls"** (side two) followed on 5 August 1973 at Command Studios, London, with Eno also contributing VCS3 synthesizer treatments [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/(No_Pussyfooting)), [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fripp_&_Eno)].

Fripp's sustained, singing lines — his celebrated fuzz-sustain tone — drift over drones that thicken and thin like weather.

### 🔊 Process as composition

Nothing here is "written": the system's delay time and decay rate are the score. The music's stasis was radical in 1973 — no drums, no songs, no development in the rock sense — from two musicians then at the centre of British art rock. It prefigures Eno's Discreet Music (whose tape system is a direct descendant) and his articulation of ambient music [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frippertronics)].

### 🧬 Legacy

Initially a commercial oddity, (No Pussyfooting) has been steadily revalued as a pioneering work of ambient and systems-based rock; Fripp and Eno reunited for Evening Star (1975) and the loop-based practice they seeded runs through minimalist guitar music, dub-influenced production and the entire live-looping culture that followed [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fripp_&_Eno), [2](https://happymag.tv/frippertronics/)].
