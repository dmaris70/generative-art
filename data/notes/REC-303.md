Music of Bulgaria is the record that introduced Western listeners to the sound of the Bulgarian women's choir: the Ensemble of the Bulgarian Republic — the state ensemble for folk songs and dances founded in 1951 — conducted by its founder Philip Koutev, recorded in Paris in 1955 while on tour and issued on Nonesuch in the label's Explorer orbit in 1966. (The catalogue's 1970 date likely reflects a later Explorer-series pressing; Discogs documents the 1966 first US issue of the 1955 recording.) [[1](https://www.nonesuch.com/albums/bulgaria-music-bulgaria), [2](https://www.discogs.com/release/8491860-Ensemble-Of-The-Bulgarian-Republic-Philip-Koutev-Music-Of-Bulgaria-Original-1955-Recording)]

### 🎤 Koutev's invention

Koutev, arguably the most influential Bulgarian musician of the 20th century, gathered singers from the villages — women from the mountains and plains carrying the rural repertoire — and arranged their songs with harmonies made deliberately more accessible to outside ears, without sanding off the voice itself. The result is a hybrid: authentic village vocal production inside composed choral frames. [[1](https://www.nonesuch.com/albums/bulgaria-music-bulgaria), [2](https://www.folkworld.eu/68/e/ecc.html)]

### 🏔 The tradition underneath

What startled Western listeners was the singing technique and the harmonic language of the village sources:

* **Open-throated, forward-placed voices** — piercing, vibrato-less, built for carrying across fields, utterly unlike bel canto.
* **Drone-based part-singing** in which close, clashing seconds are treated as consonances to be relished, ornamented with yips, glottal breaks and flourishes.
* **Asymmetric dance meters** — the additive rhythms (7/8, 9/8, 11/16 and odder) that underlie Bulgarian dance forms like the râchenitsa and kopanitsa.

Koutev's arrangements frame these traits rather than dilute them, which is why the record still functions as a document of the tradition as well as of its stage adaptation. [[1](https://www.folkworld.eu/68/e/ecc.html), [2](https://www.nonesuch.com/albums/bulgaria-music-bulgaria)]

### 🧬 An unlikely underground classic

The album became a touchstone far outside folklore circles: it was reportedly one of Frank Zappa's favourite records, and the Bulgarian harmonies are said to have influenced the vocal blend of Crosby, Stills, Nash & Young. It also prepared the ground for the later global phenomenon of Le Mystère des Voix Bulgares, which drew on the same state-ensemble lineage — making this modest 1955 Paris session the quiet origin of one of world music's great success stories. [[1](https://www.nonesuch.com/albums/bulgaria-music-bulgaria), [2](https://www.folkworld.eu/68/e/ecc.html)]
