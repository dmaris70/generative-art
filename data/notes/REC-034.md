Lexikon-Sonate (1992–) is an infinite realtime composition for computer-controlled piano by Austrian composer Karlheinz Essl. It is not a score but a computer program that composes an excerpt of a virtually endless piano piece live, each time it runs — an installation that can play for years without repeating itself. Begun in 1992 as a musical commentary on Andreas Okopenko's hypertext novel *Lexikon-Roman* (1968–70), it grew into a freestanding landmark of algorithmic composition. [[1](https://www.essl.at/works/Lexikon-Sonate.html), [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlheinz_Essl_Jr.)]

### ⚡ A Program Instead of a Score

There is no notation and no human performer: the software generates MIDI data in real time and sends it to a piano that plays itself.

* **World premiere:** 10 February 1994 in Vienna, on a Bösendorfer SE computer-controlled grand. [[1](https://www.essl.at/works/Lexikon-Sonate.html)]
* **Instruments:** player pianos such as the Yamaha Disklavier and Bösendorfer CEUS; later versions also drive software pianos and VST plugins. [[1](https://www.essl.at/works/Lexikon-Sonate.html)]
* **Longevity:** Essl kept developing the program until version 6.0 (May 2020), released as freeware under a Creative Commons licence; Robert Falesch ported it to Windows. [[1](https://www.essl.at/works/Lexikon-Sonate.html)]

### 🔁 Structure Generators and the RTC-lib

The engine is a network of interconnected "structure generators" — modules modelling compositional strategies Essl abstracted from Bach, Mozart, Beethoven, Webern, Stockhausen and Cecil Taylor. They do not quote or sample; they produce *allusions* to identified gestures, recombined endlessly. Multiple random generators, disciplined by serial thinking, span behaviours from strictly deterministic to chaotic. [[1](https://www.essl.at/works/Lexikon-Sonate.html)]

The whole system is built in Max/MSP on Essl's **Real Time Composition Library (RTC-lib)**, a collection of Max objects for algorithmic composition he began assembling in 1992 and which became a widely used toolkit in its own right. [[1](https://www.essl.at/works/rtc.html)]

### 🎹 The Hypertext Piano

The Okopenko connection is structural, not decorative: like a lexicon-novel read in any order, the music has no fixed beginning, end or dramaturgy — every moment is a viable entry point. Essl has presented it as concert performance (steering the generators live), as web piece (*Lexikon-Sonate online*, from 1997), and as unattended installation. [[1](https://www.essl.at/works/Lexikon-Sonate.html), [2](https://www.essl.at/works/lexson-online.html)]

### 🧬 Legacy

Three decades of continuous versions — shareware, freeware, online, app — make Lexikon-Sonate one of the longest-lived generative works in the repertoire, and an early, rigorous answer to the question of what a "composition" is when the composer ships an algorithm instead of notes. [[1](https://www.essl.at/works/Lexikon-Sonate.html), [2](https://essl.at/presse/Lexikon-Sonate.html)]
