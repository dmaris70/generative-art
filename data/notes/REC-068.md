Jesus' Blood Never Failed Me Yet (1971) is Gavin Bryars' tape-loop-and-ensemble piece built on a brief recording of an unnamed elderly rough sleeper singing a fragment of a religious song, recorded near Elephant & Castle and Waterloo Station for a documentary on London street life by Alan Power. Bryars loops the frail, perfectly pitched voice indefinitely while live strings and brass gather around it — one of the most quietly devastating works of British experimental music. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus%27_Blood_Never_Failed_Me_Yet)]

### 🔁 Thirteen bars

Working with the documentary's discarded audio, Bryars noticed two things: the old man's unaccompanied singing was in tune with his piano, and the first section of the song — 13 bars long — formed a natural, seamless loop. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus%27_Blood_Never_Failed_Me_Yet)]

* He copied the fragment onto a tape loop and, famously, left it playing in a copying room at Leicester Polytechnic while he fetched coffee.
* Returning, he found the room transformed: people moving slowly, some sitting alone and quietly weeping — which convinced him the loop needed nothing added except sympathy. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus%27_Blood_Never_Failed_Me_Yet), [2](https://artandtheology.org/2022/08/22/jesus-blood-never-failed-me-yet-gavin-bryars/)]

### 🎻 The swell

The composition is a single long crescendo of orchestration: strings and brass enter gradually, harmonising the loop in ever-richer, ever-denser layers that support the voice without ever drowning it. The old man himself died before he could hear what had been made of his singing. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus%27_Blood_Never_Failed_Me_Yet)]

### 📼 Three lengths, three eras

The work's duration has always been set by its medium:

* **1975** — first release, c. 25 minutes, on Brian Eno's Obscure label (sharing the LP with The Sinking of the Titanic), trimmed to fit vinyl. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus%27_Blood_Never_Failed_Me_Yet)]
* **1990** — a 60-minute version recorded in Bourges, France.
* **1993** — the 74-minute CD-length version on Point Music, with Tom Waits joining the loop in its final section; it was shortlisted for the Mercury Prize. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus%27_Blood_Never_Failed_Me_Yet)]

### 🧬 Afterlife

Choreographers took the piece up — Maguy Marin in 1981 and William Forsythe in 1993 made dance works on it — and it remains the standard counterexample to the claim that process music is emotionally cold: a rigorous loop structure whose entire effect is compassion. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus%27_Blood_Never_Failed_Me_Yet)]
