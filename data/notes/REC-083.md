For 1, 2 or 3 People (1964) is Christian Wolff's classic of interdependent performance: a set of ten score pages, playable by any one, two or three performers on any instruments, in which what you play is largely determined by what you hear the others do [[1](https://www.soundexpanse.com/rs13-wolff/)]. This catalogue entry is a realization by the Hamburg ensemble Nelly Boyd — details of the specific recording are partly uncertain (see below).

### 🎼 Cue Notation

Wolff's notation abandons fixed sequence for a grammar of contingency. Symbols instruct a player to sound a note after hearing another player, to coordinate an attack exactly with the next sound that occurs, to hold until a change is heard elsewhere. Beginning with works such as this one, Wolff foregrounded the performers' activities themselves — timing, cueing, selecting material — as the actual substance of the music [[1](https://www.foundationforcontemporaryarts.org/recipients/christian-wolff/), [2](https://www.soundexpanse.com/rs13-wolff/)]. No two realizations can be alike; the piece is a social situation as much as a score. David Tudor's electronic realizations made it famous in the 1960s.

### 🤝 The Nelly Boyd Group

Nelly Boyd is a Hamburg composer-performer collective founded in 2004, its members drawn from visual art, composition, electroacoustics, rock and free improvisation; it specializes in experimental and minimal music and has worked directly with Alvin Lucier, Rhys Chatham and Wolff himself [[1](https://www.nellyboyd.org/about/), [2](https://kampnagel.de/produktionen/nelly-boyd-ensemble)]. The group played a "Tribute to Christian Wolff" concert at Hamburg's Christianskirche during the 2008 Blurred Edges festival, with the composer present, and later joined the mass Hamburg performance of Wolff's Burdocks at Kampnagel [[1](https://www.nellyboyd.org/2013/01/christian-wolff-kompositionen-1950-1972/), [2](https://kampnagel.de/produktionen/christian-wolff-decoder-ensemble-katarakt-allstars-nelly-boyd-tonart-stark-bewoelkt-burdocks-tag-1)].

### ❓ Which Recording?

Sources diverge on the recorded realization:

* The ensemble's own site states its For 1, 2 or 3 People — played by Jens Röhm, Robert Engelbrecht and Jan Feddersen, recorded at the 2008 tribute concert — appeared in 2011 on Edition RZ, on a Wolff collection ("Kompositionen 1950–1972") alongside historic performers such as Tudor and Rzewski [[1](https://www.nellyboyd.org/2013/01/christian-wolff-kompositionen-1950-1972/)].
* A Muziekweb database entry instead credits a December 2011 performance with electric guitars and prepared piano [[1](https://www.muziekweb.nl/en/Link/U00000621105/CLASSICAL/For-1-2-or-3-people)] — likely a mis-parsing of the ensemble name as a performer, but unresolved here.

The instrumentation of this realization should therefore be treated as unconfirmed.

### 🧬 Why It Matters

Wolff's piece remains a touchstone of indeterminacy that is nevertheless rigorously disciplined — freedom located not in chance but in mutual attention. That a 21st-century Hamburg collective returns to it, with the 80-year-old composer in the pews, is evidence of how durable that social experiment has proven.
