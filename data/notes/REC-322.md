+/- (1996), released on the London label Touch on 2 April 1996 as TO:30, is the record Ryoji Ikeda regarded as his true debut as a sound artist: an album built exclusively from sine waves and white noise, the two extremes of sound — pure single-frequency order and total spectral chaos — arranged into music that operates at the threshold of hearing itself. [[1](https://touch33.net/catalogue/to30-ryoji-ikeda.html), [2](https://www.muziekgebouw.nl/en/ryoji-ikeda-s-sine-waves-and-white-noise-lz3y)]

### 🔊 Two raw materials

Ikeda's premise is almost mathematically austere: the sine wave is the most tightly defined sound possible, white noise the least. Everything on the album is generated from these elementary signals — no instruments, no samples, and, as commentators have noted, no material even subject to copyright. Rhythm arises from switching tones on and off at rapid rates; timbre from stacking and filtering. [[1](https://www.muziekgebouw.nl/en/ryoji-ikeda-s-sine-waves-and-white-noise-lz3y), [2](https://www.thevinylfactory.com/features/data-as-spectacle-a-ryoji-ikeda-overview)]

### 🎧 Structure: Headphonics, +, −

The disc runs in three arcs: the opening "Headphonics" pieces, then a "+" sequence (+, +., +..) and a "−" sequence (−, −., −..), closing with the one-minute title track "+/-". The durations stretch as each sequence proceeds — the longest panels exceed ten minutes of sustained, minutely varied tone. [[1](https://touch33.net/catalogue/to30-ryoji-ikeda.html)]

### 👂 Perception as the instrument

The album is explicitly psychoacoustic: its sonority changes with the listener's position relative to the loudspeakers, and playback over headphones produces a genuinely different piece than playback in a room. The famous ending distills this — the final track carries a barely audible high-frequency sine tone that most listeners only register at the moment it stops: a note known through its absence. The listener's own ears, head position and age (high-frequency hearing varies) become part of the score. [[1](https://touch33.net/catalogue/to30-ryoji-ikeda.html), [2](https://www.muziekgebouw.nl/en/ryoji-ikeda-s-sine-waves-and-white-noise-lz3y)]

### 🧬 Legacy

+/- opened a run of Touch releases (through 0°C and Matrix) that made Ikeda, alongside peers like Pan Sonic and Alva Noto, a defining figure of late-90s reductionist electronics; The Wire heard it bridging sine-tone minimalism and modernist techno. Its data-like purity led directly to Ikeda's later audiovisual monoliths (datamatics, test pattern), where the same signals became image as well as sound.
