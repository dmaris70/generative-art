A Sound Map of the Hudson River (1982) is a two-hour environmental sound work by the New Zealand-born American composer Annea Lockwood: the Hudson recorded in stages from its Adirondack headwaters to the sea, commissioned by the Hudson River Museum and later issued on Lovely Music (1989) [[1](http://www.lovely.com/albumnotes/notes2081.html), [2](https://www.dramonline.org/albums/annea-lockwood-a-sound-map-of-the-hudson-river)]. It matters as a defining work of field-recording-as-composition: no processing, no overdubs — the river itself, edited into a continuous downstream journey.

### 🌊 Source to sea

Lockwood made the recordings at sites along the river between 26 April and 5 December 1982, working her way downstream and recording from the bank [[1](https://catalog.libraries.psu.edu/catalog/1744266), [2](http://www.lovely.com/albumnotes/notes2081.html)]. Her governing observation: each stretch of the Hudson has its own sonic texture, shaped by terrain and varying with weather, season and the human environment — rapids, reed beds, ferry wakes, harbour water all "speak" differently.

### 🗺 The map in the title

The piece is literally accompanied by a map: numbered sites correlate to the specific locations, dates and times of day of each recording [[1](http://www.lovely.com/albumnotes/notes2081.html)]. Listening becomes a form of cartography — you traverse the river in compressed time, but every moment remains geographically and temporally anchored. In installation form the recordings have been projected across large multi-speaker arrays — at the 2018 London Contemporary Music Festival, a 40-foot circle of speakers — immersing listeners in water heard "zoomed in" to the few inches around a single microphone [[2](https://johnsonsrambler.wordpress.com/2018/12/18/lcmf-2018-a-sound-map-of-the-hudson-river/)].

### 🏞 The River Archive

The Hudson map grows out of Lockwood's River Archive, a project begun in the 1960s to record the world's rivers, and it became the first of three completed river portraits — the Danube and the Housatonic followed [[1](https://www.annealockwood.com/compositions/), [2](https://www.dramonline.org/albums/annea-lockwood-a-sound-map-of-the-hudson-river)]. Her premise throughout: moving water is an inexhaustible sound-composer, endlessly non-repeating, and attention to it is both an aesthetic and an ecological act.

### 🧬 Legacy

The Sound Map has become a touchstone for acoustic ecology and the sound-map genre it named. It continues to generate offspring: geographer Mark Sciuchetti has re-recorded Lockwood's Hudson sites to trace how the river's soundscape has changed in the decades since [[1](https://ecomusicology.info/a-new-or-another-sound-map/)] — the piece functioning, forty years on, exactly as an archive should.
