Sarangi: The Voice of a Hundred Colors is Ram Narayan's recording for the Nonesuch Explorer series (H-72030), made in the late 1960s — sources date the release variously to 1968 and 1969 — with Mahapurush Misra on tabla and Shirish Gor on tambura. It carried to Western listeners the instrument Narayan had single-handedly turned from a humble vocal accompanist into a solo concert voice. ### 🎻 The Hundred-Colored Instrument

The sarangi — its name popularly glossed as *sau rang*, "a hundred colors" — is North India's most vocal instrument and one of its hardest.

* A squat, carved box of wood and skin, bowed upright, with **three main playing strings** and **over 30 sympathetic strings** that shimmer in resonance behind every note. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_Narayan)]
* The player stops the strings not with fingertips but with the sides of the fingernails and cuticles — painful, groove-carving technique that lets the pitch slide continuously, mimicking every ornament of the singing voice.

### 🎤 From Accompaniment to the Front of the Stage

For centuries the sarangi shadowed vocalists (and carried the stigma of the courtesan salon). Narayan's biography is the story of its emancipation.

* Born near Udaipur in 1927, he was hired in 1944 by All India Radio, Lahore, as a vocal accompanist — the producer Jivan Lal Mattoo spotted the tell-tale grooves in his fingernails at a singing audition. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_Narayan)]
* After Partition he moved to Delhi, then in 1949 to Mumbai's film studios; frustrated by the accompanist's subordinate role, he gambled on a solo career, succeeding from 1956 at India's major festivals. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_Narayan)]
* He became the first internationally successful sarangi soloist, touring Europe and America from the 1960s. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_Narayan)]

### 🎧 On the Record

The album presents Narayan's mature solo art: slow alap unfolding a raga's contour with voice-like portamento, then compositions with Misra's tabla — the sympathetic strings supplying a built-in halo of echo. It is Hindustani music at its most cantabile, played on an instrument with no frets and no margin for error.

### 🧬 Legacy

Alongside his Nonesuch and EMI recordings, this LP helped standardize solo sarangi repertoire and technique. Narayan (1927–2024) taught worldwide and was honoured with the Padma Vibhushan; nearly every solo sarangi player since stands in his line.
