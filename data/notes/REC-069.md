Anthologie de la musique des Pygmées Aka (Ocora, 1978) is a three-LP set of field recordings made among the Aka people of the Central African Republic by ethnomusicologist Simha Arom — arguably the single most influential document of Central African vocal polyphony ever published. The recordings were collected between 1971 and 1977 in the Lobaye region, on missions run through the CNRS oral-tradition laboratory (LACITO) in Paris, and issued by Radio France's Ocora label (558.526/527/528); the set won the Prix du Président de la République of the Académie Charles Cros and was reissued as a 2-CD set in 2016. [[1](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb379338860), [2](https://www.radiofrance.com/les-editions/disque/centrafrique-anthologie-de-la-musique-des-pygmees-aka)]

### 🕌 The Tradition

Aka music is participatory and woven into daily life: songs for net-hunting, honey-gathering, games, and rituals such as those surrounding death and divination involve the whole community rather than specialist performers. [[1](https://www.radiofrance.com/les-editions/disque/centrafrique-anthologie-de-la-musique-des-pygmees-aka)] Its texture is a dense, cyclic vocal counterpoint — several independent, endlessly varied parts over percussion — with the yodel (rapid alternation of chest and head voice) as a defining timbre. The interlocking parts are often described in Western terms as hocket-like, though Arom himself analyzed them as true counterpoint built from a small set of named constituent parts subject to constant variation, and was reluctant to apply the hocket label; the terminology remains genuinely contested. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simha_Arom)]

### 🔁 Arom's Method

Arom, a French-Israeli horn player originally sent to Bangui in the 1960s to found a brass band, turned ethnomusicology into an experimental science.

* **Playback recording:** he devised interactive re-recording techniques — isolating individual singers' parts while checking them against the judgment of the musicians themselves — to disentangle polyphonies inaccessible to transcription by ear. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simha_Arom)]
* **Insider validation:** field analysis was corroborated with cognitive data from cultural insiders, a methodology later systematized in his landmark *African Polyphony and Polyrhythm* (1991). [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simha_Arom)]

### 🌍 Significance and Afterlife

The Anthologie made Aka polyphony world-famous. György Ligeti openly credited Arom's recordings and analyses as an influence on his polyrhythmic later style, and Steve Reich and Luciano Berio also engaged with his work. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simha_Arom)] The oral traditions of the Aka, including this polyphony, were later proclaimed by UNESCO a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity (2003). Arom's sound archives entered the Bibliothèque nationale de France in 2011 — the recordings on this set among the crown jewels. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simha_Arom)]
