EARS (2016) is the fourth album by American composer Kaitlyn Aurelia Smith, released on 1 April 2016 — and the record that made her the most visible new voice on the Buchla synthesizer, an instrument long associated with esoteric West Coast experimentalism rather than melody. One catalogue note: this release was on the independent label [Western Vinyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ears_(album)), not RVNG Intl. — Smith's RVNG release that same year was *Sunergy*, her collaboration with Buchla pioneer Suzanne Ciani. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ears_(album)), [2](https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2016/01/kaitlyn-aurelia-smith-interview/)]

### ⚡ The Buchla and Its Kin

Smith discovered the Buchla Music Easel by chance — a neighbor on Orcas Island, Washington, lent her one — and it became her core instrument.

* **Primary tool:** the Buchla Music Easel, a portable 1973 performance synthesizer with touch-plate keyboard, central to her practice. [[1](https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2016/01/kaitlyn-aurelia-smith-interview/)]
* **Supporting cast:** EMS Synthi, ARP 2600, Korg Mono/Poly, Moog Werkstatt and other analog machines fill out the palette. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ears_(album))]
* **The hybrid move:** EARS was her first record to blend synthesis with acoustic instruments — her own voice, flute, clarinet, saxophones, a woodwind quintet (Rob Frye of Bitchin Bajas on woodwinds), mbira and field recordings. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ears_(album))]

### 🔊 Synthetic Nature

The album's signature is deliberate confusion between organic and electronic: acoustic instruments are processed until they sound synthesized, while Buchla patches burble like wetlands and birdsong. Smith's wordless, heavily layered vocals are treated as another patchable signal. Critics heard a kind of humid, Rousseau-jungle pastoral — dense, fast-moving counterpoint rather than ambient wash. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ears_(album)), [2](https://www.allaboutjazz.com/ears-kaitlyn-aurelia-smith-western-vinyl-review-by-rob-caldwell)]

### 🎧 Shape and Reception

Eight tracks in 38:39, from "First Flight" and "Wetlands" to the 11-minute closer "Existence in the Unfurling," where the miniature-song format opens out into long-form synthesis. The album scored 82/100 on Metacritic ("universal acclaim") and landed on 2016 year-end lists from Pitchfork (#29), NPR Music (#30) and Resident Advisor (#14). [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ears_(album)), [2](https://www.metacritic.com/music/ears/kaitlyn-aurelia-smith)]

### 🧬 Legacy

EARS positioned Smith at the head of the 2010s Buchla revival, translating Morton Subotnick-era circuitry into something naturalistic and quietly pop-literate. It set up both the Ciani collaboration *Sunergy* (RVNG, 2016) and her breakthrough *The Kid* (2017), and helped re-domesticate modular synthesis for a generation of composers who treat patch cables as ecology rather than machinery. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaitlyn_Aurelia_Smith), [2](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/features/kaitlyn-aurelia-smith-on-new-album-the-kid-w502838)]
