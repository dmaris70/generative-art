WTC 9/11 (2009–10) is Steve Reich's string-quartet memorial to the September 11 attacks, composed for the Kronos Quartet and premiered by them on 19 March 2011 at Duke University. It returns Reich — who lived four blocks from the World Trade Center — to the documentary speech-melody technique of *Different Trains* (1988): recorded voices whose pitch contours are doubled and harmonized by the strings. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTC_9/11), [2](https://today.duke.edu/2011/03/reich_kronos.html)]

### 🎙️ Voices as Material

The roughly 15-minute work runs in three movements without pause — "9/11," "2010," "WTC."

* **Archival voices:** NORAD air-traffic controllers tracking the hijacked planes and FDNY radio traffic from the morning itself. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTC_9/11)]
* **Remembered voices:** interviews Reich recorded in 2010 with friends and neighbors from Lower Manhattan, and with women who sat *shmira* — the Jewish vigil of psalm-singing kept over the dead. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTC_9/11)]
* **The off-the-hook tone:** the first violin opens and closes the piece imitating a disconnected phone line — an ordinary sound made unbearable by context. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTC_9/11)]

The scoring is for three string quartets — one live, two prerecorded (or three live) — plus the voice tape. Reich said he "tried to make it longer," but "the piece wanted to be terse." [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTC_9/11)]

### 🖼️ The Cover Controversy

The September 2011 Nonesuch recording (paired with *Mallet Quartet* and *Dance Patterns*) became notorious before release: its cover set the album title against a doctored news photograph of the second plane an instant before impact. Composer Phil Kline called it "the first truly despicable classical album cover" he had seen; others defended its documentary honesty, including Kronos's David Harrington. After weeks of argument, Reich and Nonesuch withdrew the image, Reich writing that he did not want the cover to distract from listening to the music. [[1](https://slate.com/culture/2011/08/wtc-9-11-steve-reich-and-nonesuch-pull-the-album-s-controversial-cover-art.html), [2](https://www.nonesuch.com/journal/steve-reich-comments-on-the-wtc-911-album-cover-2011-08-11), [3](https://www.npr.org/sections/deceptivecadence/2011/07/22/138573809/does-this-steve-reich-9-11-album-cover-cross-the-line)]

### 🧬 Standing

The episode became a case study in the ethics of documentary art — where Reich's sampling aesthetic, uncontroversial applied to 1930s train announcements, collided with living grief. The work itself has held its place: Guardian critics ranked it among the finest concert works composed since 2000 (No. 17, 2019). [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTC_9/11)]
