Paragraph 7 is the choral heart of Cornelius Cardew's The Great Learning (1968–70), a seven-paragraph setting of a Confucian text scored largely in words and graphics for the Scratch Orchestra — the deliberately open London ensemble Cardew co-founded in 1969 with Michael Parsons and Howard Skempton. Written for "any number of untrained voices," it is the era's most persuasive proof that a few plain rules can make a crowd of non-musicians sound sublime. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scratch_Orchestra), [2](https://www.soundohm.com/product/the-great-learning)]

### 🎼 The Rules

The score is a single page of text drawn from Confucius's Great Learning, each phrase prefixed by an instruction like "sing 8 IF" — sing that word eight times, one full breath per repetition. [[1](https://www.newmusicnewcollege.org/PDFs/Cardew_score.pdf), [2](https://www.newmusicnewcollege.org/cardew.html)]

* Each singer begins on any note they like, singing softly.
* For each new line, a singer must move to a note they can hear being sung by a neighbour — not the note they just sang.
* If no new note is audible, they may choose freely.
* Singers proceed at their own pace, so the choir drifts apart in time while converging in pitch. [[1](https://www.newmusicnewcollege.org/cardew.html)]

### 🔊 Self-Organizing Harmony

The result is a slowly thinning cloud of tones that settles, unplanned, into radiant consonance — serene, Ligeti-like drift achieved without a conductor or notation. Because errors (mishearing, imperfect unisons) constantly inject new pitches, the piece never collapses to a single note; the "mistakes" are the engine of its variety. Brian Eno, who sang in the Scratch Orchestra performances, later made Paragraph 7 a central exhibit in his thinking about generative music and self-organizing systems. [[1](http://differentperspectivesinmyroom.blogspot.com/2018/09/cornelius-cardew-scratch-orchestra.html), [2](https://github.com/alexbainter/paragraph-7.0)]

### 📻 The 1971 Recording

On 15 February 1971 Cardew and the Scratch Orchestra recorded Paragraphs 2 and 7 for Deutsche Grammophon's avant-garde series — startlingly, a major classical label documenting an orchestra of amateurs. That LP remains the primary sonic record of the Scratch Orchestra and the standard reference recording of the work. [[1](https://www.soundohm.com/product/the-great-learning), [2](https://drivingmebackwards.wordpress.com/2022/01/02/cornelius-cardew-the-scratch-orchestra-the-great-learning-1971/)]

### 🧬 Afterlife

Cardew repudiated the piece within a few years, converting to a militant Maoism that condemned his own avant-garde past; he died in 1981, struck by a car. The music outlived the recantation: Paragraph 7 is still regularly mounted by choirs and community groups (recent performances range from the Warsaw Autumn festival to browser-based realizations), precisely because it needs no trained singers — only people willing to listen to each other. [[1](https://warszawska-jesien.art.pl/en/2022/program/utwory/the-great-learning-paragraph-2-7-cornelius-cardew), [2](https://github.com/alexbainter/paragraph-7.0)]
