Strumming Music (1974) is Charlemagne Palestine's signature work: roughly 51 minutes of two alternating notes on a nine-foot Bösendorfer Imperial grand, sustain pedal wedged down throughout, played so fast and so long that the undamped strings bloom into clouds of overtones. Released on the French Shandar label — home to La Monte Young, Terry Riley and Philip Glass — it is one of the defining piano records of minimalism, though Palestine himself has always resisted the label. [[1](https://jivetimerecords.com/2017/06/charlemagne-palestine-strumming-music-shandar-1974/), [2](https://www.soundohm.com/product/strumming-music-1)]

### 🎹 The strumming technique

Palestine's "strumming" is rapid alternation between single notes — beginning with two, gradually expanding to clusters — struck with unbroken intensity for the length of the piece:

* The depressed sustain pedal leaves every string free to resonate sympathetically.
* Struck tones compound into difference tones, beat patterns and singing upper partials; as the piano slowly drifts out of tune under the assault, the timbre keeps changing.
* Listeners soon cannot distinguish notes being played from resonances arising inside the instrument's body. [[1](https://jivetimerecords.com/2017/06/charlemagne-palestine-strumming-music-shandar-1974/), [2](https://getcentered.io/2018/09/12/charlemagne-palestine-strumming-music-review/)]

The physical demand is enormous — a trance discipline as much as a keyboard technique, descended from his years as a carillonneur at St. Thomas Church in Manhattan and his obsessive search for a "golden sound."

### 🥃 Ritual apparatus

Palestine's performances are framed as shamanic rituals: the piano surrounded by his companion stuffed animals, a glass of cognac on the instrument, clove cigarettes — props he treats not as eccentricity but as sacred paraphernalia establishing the ceremonial time the music requires [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne_Palestine)]. The Bösendorfer matters too: he insists on its extra bass strings and unusually resonant frame, and performed the work at mid-1970s New York venues such as the Idea Warehouse [[2](https://www.moma.org/calendar/exhibitions/3963/installation_images/44206)].

### 🔊 Sound over system

Where Reich and Glass built patterns, Palestine builds a continuous sound-mass whose interior detail the ear explores — closer to Tibetan ritual drone or to his contemporary Éliane Radigue than to pattern minimalism. The overtone cloud is the composition; the two struck notes are merely its engine.

### 🧬 Legacy

Long out of print, Strumming Music became a cult object until CD and vinyl reissues restored it; related 1974 Bösendorfer recordings have since surfaced on Alga Marghen [[1](https://blogthehum.com/2017/06/04/on-charlemagne-palestines-arpeggiated-bosendorfer-falsetto-voice-recorded-in-1974-and-issued-by-alga-marghen-for-the-first-time/)]. Its influence runs through drone, doom and contemporary piano writing — anywhere sheer sustained resonance is treated as musical subject matter.
