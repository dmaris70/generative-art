Through Glass Panes (Important Records, 2011) was Ellen Fullman's first proper solo full-length for the label, arriving after three decades of work on a single, extraordinary invention: the Long String Instrument. It is at once a portrait of that instrument in its mature state and a quietly radical chamber record, pairing the strings with cello, violin and other players. [[1](https://importantrecords.com/products/imprec336), [2](https://www.forcedexposure.com/Catalog/fullman-ellen-through-glass-panes-cd/IMPREC.336CD.html)]

### 🎻 The Long String Instrument

Fullman began developing the instrument in 1981: dozens of metal wires, fifty feet or longer, stretched across a room, tuned in just intonation and "bowed" longitudinally — the player walks slowly along the strings, stroking them lengthwise with rosin-coated fingers. Because the sounding length is the whole room, the instrument produces massive, organ-like standing waves alive with overtones. [[1](https://importantrecords.com/products/imprec336), [2](https://ellenfullman.bandcamp.com/album/through-glass-panes)]

* Fullman devised her own notation system to choreograph the performer's walking — where the body is along the string *is* the note.
* Compositions exploit sonic events at specific nodal points along the string length.

### 🎼 The Pieces

The pieces show unusual range for a single-instrument practice. "Never Gets Out of Me," a duet with cellist Theresa Wong, and "Flowers," a trio with Travis Weller and Henna Cho, adapt material from Fullman's "Stratified Bands: Last Kind Words," originally composed for Kronos Quartet and rooted in "Last Kind Words Blues," the haunted 1930 recording by Delta singer Geeshie Wiley. "Event Locations No. 2" is pure Long String Instrument, focused on the beating of close, uncomfortable harmonies; the title track uses a percussive "box bow" for a clipped, autoharp-like attack. [[1](https://psychotropicwonderland.com/ellen-fullman-through-glass-panes-important-2011/), [2](https://importantrecords.com/products/imprec336)]

### 🤝 Context

The record followed Fullman's collaborative LP with drone duo Barn Owl and a split with Eleh, and marked her shift from being known mainly as an instrument-builder and installation artist toward being heard as a composer — one whose blues source material connects the American experimental tradition (Harry Partch's just intonation, deep-listening drone) back to vernacular song. [[1](https://importantrecords.com/products/imprec336), [2](https://www.forcedexposure.com/Catalog/fullman-ellen-through-glass-panes-cd/IMPREC.336CD.html)]

### 🧬 Afterlife

Through Glass Panes circulated widely (it even streams freely via the Free Music Archive and Internet Archive), and set up the later acclaim of Fullman's work with Theresa Wong and the Long String Instrument's festival appearances worldwide — the instrument itself, rebuilt room by room, remaining her life's single ongoing composition.
