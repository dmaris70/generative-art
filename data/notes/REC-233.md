Adnos I–III is Éliane Radigue's monumental trilogy of slowly evolving synthesizer music, composed between 1973–74 and 1980 in her Paris home studio on the ARP 2500 modular synthesizer and magnetic tape. Each part runs over seventy minutes, and together they form the first large-scale statement of the mature electronic style — near-motionless surfaces concealing constant inner change — that made Radigue one of the most revered figures in drone and electronic music. [[1](https://importantrecords.com/products/imprec028), [2](https://elianeradigue.bandcamp.com/album/adnos)]

### 🌊 Stones in the stream

The trilogy's governing image is hydrological: moving stones in a riverbed does not stop the stream, but it alters the fluid shape of the water. In Radigue's gloss, a strong sound energy likewise redirects fluid areas of resonance, generating growing sonic activity — and the listening ear, in the conch formed by sound waves, filters and emphasizes regions of hearing the way an eye follows shimmer on water. The music enacts this: fixed sustained tones whose interference patterns, beatings and partials are the real, ever-shifting subject. [[1](https://elianeradigue.bandcamp.com/album/adnos)]

### 🎛 The ARP 2500 and tape

* Radigue worked almost exclusively with a single instrument, the ARP 2500 — chosen for the stability and precision of its oscillators and filters.
* Adnos II (completed 1979) was realized with the ARP plus three Revox quarter-inch tape decks, a mixing console, and an outboard third-octave filter run in sync with the ARP's own filters — an ascetic toolkit for music of extreme subtlety. [[1](https://importantrecords.com/products/imprec028)]

### 🕰 A trilogy across a conversion

Adnos I (1973–74) premiered in 1974 at the Festival d'Automne in Paris. Before continuing the cycle, Radigue stepped away from composition for around three years following her conversion to Tibetan Buddhism; she returned to complete Adnos II (1979) and Adnos III (1979–80), and the trilogy's patient, meditative temporality is usually read through that practice. [[1](https://importantrecords.com/products/imprec028), [2](https://elianeradigue.bandcamp.com/album/adnos)]

### 🧬 Editions and afterlife

The complete trilogy was issued as a 3-CD set by Important Records (IMPREC 028) and is available through Radigue's Bandcamp; it has been presented live in full by organizations such as Blank Forms. Adnos opened the path to the Trilogie de la Mort and, later, to Radigue's acoustic Occam series — the same listening applied to instrumentalists instead of oscillators.
