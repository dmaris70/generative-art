Composition 76: Version Two is the second of two complete realizations of Anthony Braxton's Composition No. 76 recorded on 22 September 1977 at Streeterville Sound, Chicago, and issued together as the Arista LP For Trio (1978). The same score is played twice by two entirely different trios — Version Two pairs Braxton with Roscoe Mitchell and Joseph Jarman of the Art Ensemble of Chicago — making the album a controlled experiment in how radically one notation can be inhabited. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_Trio), [2](https://www.discogs.com/master/963066-Anthony-Braxton-For-Trio)]

### 🎼 The system

Composition 76 (1977) is written for three multi-instrumentalists and mixes conventional five-line staves with an elaborate graphic apparatus — colours, shapes and codes:

* Colour is linked to character and intensity: green signals calm playing, red intensity, with darker hues indicating faster and/or louder execution.
* Brackets instruct the player to change instruments; each musician commands a whole arsenal of reeds, flutes and percussion.
* Coloured shapes trigger improvisational outbursts deliberately untethered from the surrounding pitch and rhythm material.

A 2018 Journal of Music Theory study transcribed both studio versions to show how the players collaboratively negotiate this score — a work "previously thought to be resistant to analysis." [[1](https://read.dukeupress.edu/journal-of-music-theory/article/62/2/249/136717/Improvisation-and-Collaboration-in-Anthony-Braxton)]

### 🔊 Version Two

On Version Two (20:22), Braxton plays multi-reeds, percussion and gongs; Mitchell woodwinds and saxophones; Jarman woodwinds, saxophones, vibraphone and gongs [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_Trio)]. Where Version One's trio (Henry Threadgill, Douglas Ewart) came from the AACM's Chicago orbit of Braxton's own generation, the Art Ensemble pair bring their theatre of "little instruments" — the music is sparse, ritualistic and slow-breathing, full of silences, gong strokes and instrument-switching pantomime rather than jazz phrasing.

### 🤝 Context

All four collaborators, like Braxton, emerged from the AACM (Association for the Advancement of Creative Musicians), and For Trio is one of the purest documents of that community's conviction that composition and improvisation form a single continuum. It sits mid-way through Braxton's remarkable Arista run (1974–80), later collected in Mosaic's complete-Arista box [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_Trio)]. Braxton's numbered-composition catalogue, with its schematic diagram titles, is maintained today by his Tri-Centric Foundation [[2](https://tricentricfoundation.org/scores)].

### 🧬 Significance

AllMusic judged the album a "seminal work" that repays careful listening and prefigures Braxton's later quartet music, where multiple compositions run simultaneously [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_Trio)]. Hearing Version Two against Version One remains the point: the "piece" is revealed as a space of possibilities, and the recording captures two expeditions through it on a single day.
