Verbal Brainwash (Verbal hjärntvätt, mid-1960s) is the signature text-sound composition of Åke Hodell (1919–2000), Swedish concrete poet, radio dramatist and anti-militarist agitator. It survives on the retrospective Verbal Brainwash and Other Works, a 3-CD set gathering all of Hodell's text-sound work from 1963–1977, released by Fylkingen Records in 2000 and reissued for his centenary in 2019. (This catalogue lists the compilation as a Sub Rosa release; the sources found credit it to Fylkingen Records, the label of the Stockholm new-music society where Hodell worked.) [[1](https://fylkingen.bandcamp.com/album/verbal-brainwash-other-works), [2](https://www.discogs.com/%C3%85ke-Hodell-Verbal-Brainwash-And-Other-Works/release/253331)]

### ✈️ From Fighter Pilot to Anti-Militarist

Hodell trained as a Swedish air force fighter pilot until a 1941 crash during practice. Recovering in hospital in Lund, he met the poet Gunnar Ekelöf and emerged with the staunchly anti-militaristic outlook that drives all his work. [[1](https://www.blankforms.org/events/ake-hodell-verbal-brainwash)]

### 🗣️ Text-Sound Composition

At Fylkingen in the 1960s, Hodell helped pioneer *text-sound composition* — a hybrid the Swedes distinguished from both German Hörspiel and French poésie sonore.

* The materials: electronically treated collages of the human voice, canned sound, tape loops and field recordings. [[1](https://fylkingen.bandcamp.com/album/verbal-brainwash-other-works)]
* The method of his anti-military pieces: obsessive verbal permutation — commands, drills and slogans repeated until language itself is exposed as an instrument of coercion, the "brainwash" of the title enacted on the listener. [[1](https://www.blankforms.org/events/ake-hodell-verbal-brainwash), [2](https://toneshift.wordpress.com/2019/01/10/verbal-brainwash-and-other-works-by-ake-hodell/)]

### 📻 Agitprop on State Radio

Hodell later used the Swedish Broadcasting Corporation's studios for multi-voiced denunciations of international abuses: *Where Is Eldridge Cleaver?* (1969) on Californian capital punishment; *Mr Smith in Rhodesia* (1970), in which children reading Oxford-English primers indict Ian Smith's regime — the piece earned a 15-year radio ban; and the sardonic *Spirit of Ecstasy, Racing Car Opera* (1977). [[1](https://www.blankforms.org/events/ake-hodell-verbal-brainwash)]

### 🧬 Legacy

Closest in ethic to Fluxus, Hodell made political dissent a formal principle rather than a subject. The Fylkingen retrospective fixed his place alongside Bengt Emil Johnson and Lars-Gunnar Bodin in the Swedish text-sound canon, and his permutational voice-pieces now read as precursors of both sound poetry's revival and sampled-voice agitprop. [[1](https://fylkingen.bandcamp.com/album/verbal-brainwash-other-works), [2](https://neural.it/2019/07/ake-hodell-verbal-brainwash-and-other-works/)]
