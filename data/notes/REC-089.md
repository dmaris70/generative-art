Composition No. 76 (1977) is Anthony Braxton's long-form work for three multi-instrumentalists, documented twice over on the album For Trio, recorded 22 September 1977 at Streeterville Sound in Chicago, produced by Michael Cuscuna, and released by Arista (AB-4181) in 1978 [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_Trio)]. Version One — the recording in this collection, 20:56 — pairs Braxton with fellow AACM multi-instrumentalists Douglas Ewart and Henry Threadgill; the piece matters as one of Braxton's most radical fusions of graphic notation, ritualized performance behaviour and post-AACM instrumental practice.

### 🎼 A score in colours, shapes and codes

The published score runs to 27 pages of colour plates on individual cardstock [[1](https://tricentricfoundation.org/store/composition-no-76)]. It mixes conventional five-line staff writing with graphic elements — colours, shapes and coded symbols that prescribe sound-types and actions rather than fixed pitches — and excerpts of the score were reproduced on the For Trio album cover itself [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_Trio), [2](https://blogs.loc.gov/music/2025/12/the-musical-language-of-anthony-braxton/)]. The work sits inside Braxton's larger "language music" system, in which entire compositions are built from classified sound-types; it also connects to the ritual and ceremonial stratum of his catalogue, though published specifics on 76's ritual choreography are thin — Braxton's own composition notes, sold with the score, remain the primary source.

### 🤝 One piece, two trios

For Trio's experiment is scientific in spirit: the same composition realized by two different trios on the same day.

* **Version One:** Braxton, Douglas Ewart, Henry Threadgill.
* **Version Two:** Braxton, Roscoe Mitchell, Joseph Jarman — both of the Art Ensemble of Chicago.

Each player commands an arsenal of woodwinds, brass and percussion, so the "trio" is really three mobile orchestras; comparing versions exposes exactly how much the notation determines and how much it leaves to the players [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_Trio), [2](https://www.discogs.com/master/963066-Anthony-Braxton-For-Trio)].

### 🔊 The sound

Quiet, spacious, deliberately anti-climactic music: flurries of flutes and small reeds, percussion tickings, long silences, occasional vocalized breath — closer to a slow ceremony than to the fire-breathing Braxton of the quartets. AllMusic's lukewarm 2.5-star review conceded the music "repays careful listening" and flagged it as a precursor of Braxton's later compositional systems [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_Trio)].

### 🧬 Afterlife

Long out of print, For Trio returned in Mosaic's 2008 box The Complete Arista Recordings of Anthony Braxton [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_Trio)], and the score remains in print through the Tri-Centric Foundation [[2](https://tricentricfoundation.org/store/composition-no-76)]. Composition No. 76 now reads as a hinge between the Arista-era experiments and the vast ritual structures — Ghost Trance Music and after — of Braxton's later decades.
