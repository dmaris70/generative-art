Symphonie pour un homme seul (1949–50) by Pierre Schaeffer and Pierre Henry is the first major work of musique concrète: a twenty-two-movement "symphony" assembled from recorded sounds — above all the sounds of the human body — at French radio in Paris. It premiered on 18 March 1950 at the Salle de l'École Normale de Musique, the first public concert of musique concrète, and laid the technical and aesthetic foundations for tape music generally [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphonie_pour_un_homme_seul), [2](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Symphonie-pour-un-homme-seul-by-Schaeffer-and-Henry)].

### 🔊 The body as orchestra

The "man alone" of the title supplies his own material: breathing, footsteps, whistling, crying, laughter, heartbeats, shouted and murmured speech fragments, set against noises from his world [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphonie_pour_un_homme_seul)]. Schaeffer described the conception as "an opera for blind people, a performance without argument, a poem made of noises, bursts of text, spoken or musical" [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphonie_pour_un_homme_seul)]. Movement titles like "Erotica" [[2](https://fresques.ina.fr/artsonores/fiche-media/InaGrm00004/pierre-schaeffer-pierre-henry-symphonie-pour-un-homme-seul-erotica.html)] signal how frankly corporeal the piece is.

### ⚙️ Turntables, not tape

Crucially, the work predates the studio's magnetic tape recorders: the montage was produced with shellac disc turntables and mixers, using locked grooves and hand-cued playback [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphonie_pour_un_homme_seul)]. That made live performance chronically difficult — a fact that drove successive revisions.

### 🔁 Revisions

* Reduced from twenty-two movements to **eleven** for a 1951 broadcast.
* Recast by Henry in **twelve** movements in 1966, the version most often heard today [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphonie_pour_un_homme_seul)].

An American excerpt premiere in Boston on 14 June 1952 drew baffled responses — DownBeat reported listeners finding it "certainly concrete" but wondering where the music was [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphonie_pour_un_homme_seul)].

### 🩰 Béjart's ballet

In 1955 Maurice Béjart choreographed the score as the first ballet set to musique concrète, under the same title — the collaboration that carried concrète out of the studio and into wide public view, and began Henry's long partnership with Béjart [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphonie_pour_un_homme_seul), [2](https://brahms.ircam.fr/works/work/9062/)].

### 🧬 Legacy

The Symphonie is the founding monument of the acousmatic tradition: proof that recorded actuality — a breath, a footstep — could be composed, not merely documented. Everything from the GRM school to sampling culture descends from the wager made in these twenty-two movements. A critical edition of the work has since been published by Contrechamps [[1](https://books.openedition.org/contrechamps/4375?lang=en)].
