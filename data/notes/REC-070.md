Nodal Excitation (1982) is the debut LP of Arnold Dreyblatt and The Orchestra of Excited Strings, released on the New York loft-jazz label India Navigation. A student of Pauline Oliveros, La Monte Young and Alvin Lucier, Dreyblatt founded the ensemble in New York City in 1979 to play his own just-intoned, overtone-saturated music — a physical, rhythmic answer to the drone minimalism of his teachers. [[1](https://www.dreyblatt.net/orchestra-of-excited-strings), [2](https://www.dragcity.com/products/nodal-excitation)]

### 🎻 The excited strings

The music's engine is an altered double bass strung with unwound piano wire. Instead of bowing or fretting, the player strikes the open strings rhythmically with the bow, "exciting" them at nodal points so that the string's natural harmonics — not stopped pitches — supply the melody. Drag City's description is compact and accurate: "ultra-rhythmic overtones created from striking piano strings strung to a bass". [[1](https://www.dragcity.com/products/nodal-excitation)]

* On the 1982 recording the excited basses are joined by hurdy-gurdy, a piano plucked directly on its strings, and portable pipe organ — every instrument tuned to a shared overtone series. [[1](http://bravojuju.blogspot.com/2008/01/arnold-dreyblatt-orchestra-of-excited.html)]
* Personnel included Dreyblatt and Michael Hauenstein on bass violas, Greg Lewis on hurdy-gurdy, Peter Phillips at the plucked piano and K. Mason Hill on pipe organ. [[1](http://bravojuju.blogspot.com/2008/01/arnold-dreyblatt-orchestra-of-excited.html), [2](https://www.discogs.com/sell/release/1260262)]

### 🔊 Overtones in strata

Because everything sounds only natural harmonics over a fixed fundamental, the ensemble behaves like one huge vibrating string. Partials coalesce in the room and in the ear: steady thirds and fifths sit at the bottom while high, elusive pings shimmer above — overtones emerging, as one listener put it, "in something like geological strata". The pulse is relentless and almost rock-like, which is why the record has been claimed equally by minimalism and by avant-rock audiences. [[1](http://bravojuju.blogspot.com/2008/01/arnold-dreyblatt-orchestra-of-excited.html), [2](https://www.dragcity.com/products/nodal-excitation)]

### 🎙️ Recording and release

The album was recorded and mixed December 1981 – January 1982 at Nickel Recording Co. in Hartford, Connecticut, and issued by India Navigation in 1982 as eight compositions, from the 2:20 "Addition" to the eight-minute "Formation" and the seven-minute title piece. [[1](http://bravojuju.blogspot.com/2008/01/arnold-dreyblatt-orchestra-of-excited.html), [2](https://www.dragcity.com/products/nodal-excitation)]

### 🧬 Afterlife

Long a collectors' rarity, Nodal Excitation was reissued by Drag City in 1996 and again on vinyl in 2015 with new liner notes, cementing its status as a cornerstone of second-generation American minimalism. Dreyblatt, based in Berlin since 1984, still tours versions of the Orchestra of Excited Strings on the same principles.
