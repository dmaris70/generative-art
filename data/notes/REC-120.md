Cinq études de bruits (Five Studies of Noises, 1948) by Pierre Schaeffer is the founding document of musique concrète: five short pieces assembled entirely from recorded sound at the Studio d'Essai of French radio (RTF) in Paris, and premiered over the airwaves on 5 October 1948 in a broadcast titled *Concert de bruits* — the first time a public heard music whose raw material was recordings themselves. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinq_%C3%A9tudes_de_bruits), [2](https://mediatheque.philharmoniedeparis.fr/mediatheque/doc/ALOES/0084538/cinq-etudes-de-bruits-1948-pierre-schaeffer?_lg=fr-FR)]

### 🔊 Five Studies, Five Sound-Worlds

* **Étude aux chemins de fer** — locomotives, whistles and wagon rhythms, recorded at special sessions Schaeffer organised at the Gare des Batignolles. [[1](https://fresques.ina.fr/artsonores/fiche-media/InaGrm00003/pierre-schaeffer-cinq-etudes-de-bruits-etude-aux-chemins-de-fer.html)]
* **Étude aux tourniquets** — toy tops and percussion instruments.
* **Étude violette** and **Étude noire** — built from piano sounds recorded for Schaeffer by the young Pierre Boulez.
* **Étude pathétique** — saucepans, canal boats, singing, speech, harmonica and piano. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinq_%C3%A9tudes_de_bruits)]

### ⚡ Composing Before Tape

The études predate the tape recorder as a studio tool: Schaeffer worked with radio's disc-cutting equipment and turntables, isolating fragments and using repetition to force the ear away from the sound's cause. The INA's commentary preserves his central question about the railway étude — could "real trains" become music? — answered by repetition and distortion that strip the sounds of context: "the further one moves from the anecdotal … toward the sonic object, the more musical it becomes." [[1](https://fresques.ina.fr/artsonores/fiche-media/InaGrm00003/pierre-schaeffer-cinq-etudes-de-bruits-etude-aux-chemins-de-fer.html)]

### 📻 A Premiere by Broadcast

There was no concert hall and no performers: the works reached their first audience as radio, fitting for pieces born inside a broadcasting institution. The *Concert de bruits* of 5 October 1948 is now treated as the birth date of electroacoustic music. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinq_%C3%A9tudes_de_bruits)]

### 🧬 Legacy

From this experiment grew Schaeffer's Groupe de Recherches Musicales, the theory of the *objet sonore* elaborated in his later *Traité des objets musicaux*, and ultimately the entire lineage of sampling and sound-based composition. The New York Times in 2011 flatly identified the railway étude as the first piece of musique concrète. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinq_%C3%A9tudes_de_bruits), [2](https://fresques.ina.fr/artsonores/fiche-media/InaGrm00003/pierre-schaeffer-cinq-etudes-de-bruits-etude-aux-chemins-de-fer.html)] The études remain in print, most recently in the remastered *Œuvres 1948–1952* edition.
