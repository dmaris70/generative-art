This entry is flagged uncertain, and research bears the flag out: no release titled *Kikoeru* ("audible" / "it can be heard") from 1995 could be verified in Akio Suzuki's documented discography, which around that period runs from *Soundsphere* (1990) to *Stone* (1994, recorded in Berlin) and later *Odds and Ends* (2002) [[1](https://www.discogs.com/artist/202206-Akio-Suzuki), [2](https://www.discogs.com/release/782702-Akio-Suzuki-Stone)]. The title may belong to an undocumented small edition, an event, or a mistitling; what is certain — and what this note therefore documents — is the practice the word names: Suzuki is the Japanese pioneer for whom hearing itself is the artwork.

### 🎧 Listening as Practice

Suzuki (born 1941 in Pyongyang) dates his art to a 1963 event at Nagoya Station in which he threw a bucket of objects down a staircase to listen to the result. The "self-study events" that followed explored what he calls "throwing" and "following" — projecting sound into a space and tracing what the space returns — establishing listening, not sound-making, as his medium [[1](https://www.akiosuzuki.com/en/bio/), [2](https://www.lespressesdureel.com/EN/auteur.php?id=4635&menu=0)].

### 🔊 Analapos

In the 1970s he built his own instruments, most famously the Analapos: two metal cylinders connected by a long coiled spring, an echo instrument he plays with voice and hands. Suzuki likens its structure to "two mirrors facing each other, reflecting into infinity" [[1](https://room40.bandcamp.com/album/analapos), [2](https://www.factmag.com/2020/01/28/akio-suzuki-analapos-room-40/)]. Recordings from a 1979 performance were issued by ALM in 1980 as *Analapos*, his first published work, reissued by Room40 in 2020 [[3](https://boomkat.com/products/analapos)]. He also performs on iwabue (stone flutes) and glass harmonica [[4](https://agosto-foundation.org/akio-suzuki-howling-objects)].

### 🌍 Points of Hearing

Since the 1980s Suzuki's work has increasingly dispensed with instruments altogether: his *oto-date* walks mark spots in a city where a listener is simply invited to stand and hear the place — scores consisting of a location and an ear. In 1988 he built a hilltop echo-observatory in Tango to spend a year listening to one landscape; the logic of *Kikoeru* as a title — "it is audible" — is exactly this practice's claim [[1](https://www.akiosuzuki.com/en/bio/)].

### 🧬 Standing

Suzuki is now recognised internationally as a founder of sound art as such — a maker of instruments, situations and silences whose influence runs through field recording, improvisation and acoustic ecology alike [[1](https://www.lespressesdureel.com/EN/auteur.php?id=4635&menu=0), [2](https://www.factmag.com/2020/01/28/akio-suzuki-analapos-room-40/)]. If a 1995 *Kikoeru* exists, it awaits confirmation; the listening it names is thoroughly documented.
