Laut und Luise is the signature collection of Sprechgedichte — "speak-poems" — by Viennese poet Ernst Jandl (1925–2000), a founding document of postwar German-language sound poetry. A dating caution for this catalogue entry: the year 1957 corresponds to the period when Jandl wrote his first experimental speech-poems and scandalized Vienna by publishing them in a literary journal, but the book *Laut und Luise* itself appeared only in 1966 (Walter Verlag, Olten), and the canonical recordings came later still — so "1957" marks the origin of the practice, not a publication. The 1957 journal episode is thinly documented online and is asserted here from standard Jandl biography rather than a citable source. [[1](https://www.lyrikline.org/en/poems/schtzngrmm-1230), [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Jandl)]

### 🗣️ The Sprechgedicht

Jandl's poems are scores for the voice: meaning arises from phonetics, not semantics. The title itself is a pun — *Laut* (sound/loud) und *Luise* (a woman's name echoing *leise*, quiet).

* **"schtzngrmm":** the word *Schützengraben* (trench) stripped of vowels and machine-gunned into consonant bursts — a WWI battle rendered entirely as phonemes, ending "t-tt" (dead). [[1](https://www.lyrikline.org/en/poems/schtzngrmm-1230)]
* **"ottos mops":** a univocalic comedy using only the vowel *o*. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Jandl)]
* Jandl's own delivery was the instrument; his live readings were famously electrifying events. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Jandl)]

### 📻 Recordings

Two early documents matter most.

* **BBC Radiophonic Workshop, July 1966:** Jandl recorded texts from *Laut und Luise* in London for George MacBeth's Third Programme poetry series — reportedly the BBC's first presentation of sound and concrete poetry — his voice multiplied by re-recording, tape-speed change and montage; issued in 1977 as *13 Radiophone Texte* (S Press Tonbandverlag). [[1](https://continuo.wordpress.com/2010/07/07/ernst-jandl-13-radiophone-texte-2/)]
* **Wagenbach, 1968:** the 7-inch *Laut und Luise — Ernst Jandl liest Sprechgedichte* (Verlag Klaus Wagenbach, Berlin) put Jandl's unaccompanied voice on record. [[1](https://www.theideaofthebook.com/pages/books/119/ernst-jandl/laut-und-luise-ernst-jandl-liest-sprechgedichte)]

### 🧬 Context and Legacy

Jandl worked alongside but apart from the Wiener Gruppe's concrete poets, translated Gertrude Stein and John Cage, and lived and collaborated with poet Friederike Mayröcker. He co-founded the Grazer Autorenversammlung (1973) and won the Büchner Prize (1984); his speech-poems entered Austrian school curricula and jazz-poetry collaborations alike, making him the rare avant-gardist who is also genuinely popular.
