Music of India: Morning and Evening Ragas (Angel Records, 1955) by sarod master Ali Akbar Khan is the first LP of Indian classical music ever released — the record that opened the Western long-playing catalogue to a tradition whose performances had never fit on a 78. It was recorded in a single session on 18 April 1955 in a guest house of the Museum of Modern Art, New York, the day before Khan's landmark MoMA concert. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_India:_Morning_and_Evening_Ragas), [2](https://archive.org/details/khan-ali-akbar-1955-music-of-india-morning-and-evening-ragas-side-a-archive)]

### 🤝 Menuhin's Invitation

Khan came to New York for the Living Arts of India Festival, a Ford Foundation-sponsored program initiated by violinist Yehudi Menuhin, who had heard India's masters on his 1952 visit. Menuhin had first invited sitarist Ravi Shankar; Shankar declined and recommended his brother-in-law and fellow Maihar-gharana disciple Khan instead. Menuhin introduced Khan as "an absolute genius... the greatest musician in the world," and his spoken introduction explaining the raga system to Western listeners opens the LP. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_India:_Morning_and_Evening_Ragas)]

### 🎼 Two Ragas, Two Times of Day

The album's very structure taught the Western listener the central premise that ragas belong to hours of the day.

* **Raga Sindhu Bhairavi** — a morning raga, nearly 20 minutes.
* **Raga Pilu Baroowa** — an evening raga, about 24 minutes.
* Khan's sarod — the fretless, steel-fingerboarded lute of the Senia-Maihar tradition, taught to him by his formidable father Allauddin Khan — is accompanied by Chatur Lal on tabla and Shirish Gor on tambura. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_India:_Morning_and_Evening_Ragas)]

Each side compresses the classical arc — unmetered alap exposition, then composition with tabla — into LP length, a form-fitting compromise that itself became a template.

### 📺 Firsts Upon Firsts

The same visit produced Khan's appearance on Alistair Cooke's CBS program *Omnibus* — early Indian classical music on American television — and the first major North American concert debuts for the tradition. The LP's success pushed EMI's HMV India to begin recording classical LPs at home, and helped draw Shankar to make his own Western concert debut in 1956. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_India:_Morning_and_Evening_Ragas), [2](https://www.sfcv.org/articles/music-news/celebrating-marins-ali-akbar-khan-his-centennial)]

### 🧬 Legacy

A decade before the sitar boom of the 1960s, this record laid the groundwork for the entire Western reception of Hindustani music. Khan founded the Ali Akbar College of Music in 1956 (later relocated to Marin County, California), training thousands of students, and remained — in Menuhin's phrase and general consensus — one of the supreme instrumentalists of the twentieth century.
