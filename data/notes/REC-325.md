Spectral Malsconcities (2018) is a chamber work by the American percussionist-composer Sarah Hennies (b. 1979), written for the piano/bass/percussion trio Bearthoven and released by New World Records on 16 October 2020 (80824) on an album pairing it with its sister piece *Unsettle* (2017) for the piano-and-percussion Bent Duo. Hennies works at the junction of minimalist process music and psychoacoustics — sound as something that happens as much in the listener's perception as in the room. [[1](https://www.newworldrecords.org/products/sarah-hennies-spectral-malsconcities), [2](https://newworldrecords.bandcamp.com/album/sarah-hennies-spectral-malsconcities)]

### 🔁 A system built never to line up

The piece runs as six linked, varied sections constructed so that the three players are never completely in sync. Desynchronization is the compositional engine: patterns are written to drift and collide, undermining conventional ensemble cohesion and producing beatings, phantom rhythms and shimmering interference that no single part contains. [[1](https://www.newworldrecords.org/products/sarah-hennies-spectral-malsconcities), [2](https://newworldrecords.bandcamp.com/album/sarah-hennies-spectral-malsconcities)]

### 🔊 The sound

The palette is intensely physical and deliberately "wrong": una corda flutterings, single notes bent at the edges, muted and prepared piano strings, vibraphone haze, bell clatter, twang and distorted overtones, with tension accumulating through density and inflection rather than harmonic argument. [[1](https://newworldrecords.bandcamp.com/album/sarah-hennies-spectral-malsconcities)]

### 🤝 Performers who broke the piece by playing it too well

Bearthoven — Karl Larson (piano), Pat Swoboda (bass), Matt Evans (percussion) — executed the polyrhythmic pages so precisely that the "mistakes" Hennies designed the music to generate never materialized. She embraced the result: "this piece is an example of performers elevating something beyond what I thought it could be." The companion work *Unsettle*, played by Bent Duo (David Friend and Bill Solomon) and grown from her 2012 piece *Settle*, stretches a strikingly economical score into a slow, half-hour intensification with no resolution. [[1](https://www.newworldrecords.org/products/sarah-hennies-spectral-malsconcities)]

### 🧬 Context and significance

Hennies extends the lineage of Reich, Young and Riley and of electroacoustic perception-explorers like Alvin Lucier, but bends it toward the body and identity: her broader practice (notably the acclaimed *Contralto*, on trans women's voices) treats perception, labor and queer/trans experience as musical material. Spectral Malsconcities — the invented title word is characteristic of her taste for the almost-meaningful — stands as one of the clearest documents of her ensemble writing: rigorous process, unstable surface, and psychoacoustic play audible to anyone.
