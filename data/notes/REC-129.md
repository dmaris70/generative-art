L'Œil écoute ("the eye listens") is a 1970 acousmatic work by Bernard Parmegiani, composed at the Groupe de Recherches Musicales (GRM) in Paris and premiered at the Gulbenkian Foundation in Lisbon on 9 May 1970, accompanied by a film directed by Walerian Borowczyk [[1](https://fresques.ina.fr/artsonores/fiche-media/InaGrm00020/bernard-parmegiani-l-oeil-ecoute.html), [2](https://bernardparmegiani.bandcamp.com/album/l-il-coute)]. Its paradoxical title states the acousmatic wager plainly: sound alone, projected from loudspeakers, must do the work of the image.

### ⚡ Studio 54

The piece is among the first composed with the electronic means of GRM's Studio 54, commissioned into service in 1970, which for the first time allowed electronic and concrète (recorded) sounds to be mixed with real fluency [[1](https://fresques.ina.fr/artsonores/fiche-media/InaGrm00020/bernard-parmegiani-l-oeil-ecoute.html)]. That hybrid is the work's signature: recognisable field sources are shadowed, extended and answered by purely synthetic material until the boundary stops mattering.

### 🔊 Seven Movements

The work unfolds chronologically through seven titled sections: "Train de passage" (passing train), "Passage d'insectes" (passing insects), "Harmonique," "Signes," "Souffles" (breaths), "Expansion" and "Alternatif" [[1](https://fresques.ina.fr/artsonores/fiche-media/InaGrm00020/bernard-parmegiani-l-oeil-ecoute.html)]. The trajectory moves from the concrete world — trains, insects — toward increasingly abstract sound-matter, a miniature of Parmegiani's whole aesthetic of morphing one sound-family into another.

### 🤝 Parmegiani at the GRM

Parmegiani (1927–2013) came to composition sideways: a sound engineer for French television in the 1950s, he joined Pierre Schaeffer's GRM in 1959 and became the leading figure of its second generation, carrying musique concrète into the electroacoustic era [[1](https://bernard-parmegiani.fr/en/itineraire/biographie/), [2](https://boomkat.com/products/l-oeil-ecoute-dedans-dehors)]. L'Œil écoute precedes his acknowledged masterpiece De Natura Sonorum (1975) and already shows its method: taxonomies of sound behaviour — passing, breathing, expanding — treated as musical form.

### 🧬 Editions and Afterlife

The work has remained in circulation through GRM-sanctioned editions, including the recent vinyl reissue pairing it with Dedans-Dehors on the Recollection GRM series, and a published edition by Maison ONA [[1](https://boomkat.com/products/l-oeil-ecoute-dedans-dehors), [2](https://maison-ona.com/catalog-R455ONA)]. For later generations of electronic musicians it stands as an early proof that "cinema for the ear" — narrative built entirely from sound morphology — could sustain a large-scale form without image, voice or pulse.
