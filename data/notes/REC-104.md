HPSCHD (1967–69) is a gargantuan multimedia collaboration between John Cage and Lejaren Hiller — the pioneer of computer-composed music — for seven amplified harpsichords and dozens of computer-generated tapes. Commissioned by Swiss harpsichordist Antoinette Vischer (Cage at first declined, disliking the instrument), it premiered on 16 May 1969 before some 6,000 people at the Assembly Hall of the University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign, where Hiller ran the Experimental Music Studios. It is chance composition scaled up to industrial, mainframe-era dimensions. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HPSCHD), [2](https://distributedmuseum.illinois.edu/exhibit/hpschd/)]

### 🎲 I Ching by FORTRAN

Cage had tossed coins for *Music of Changes* in 1951; here the divination was automated.

* A FORTRAN program encoding I Ching hexagram operations drove the composition, run on the university's mainframes. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HPSCHD)]
* The seven harpsichord solos chance-process Mozart's musical dice game and passages of Beethoven, Chopin, Schumann, Gottschalk, Busoni, Schoenberg, Cage and Hiller — two centuries of keyboard music shredded and recombined. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HPSCHD)]
* The tapes contain computer-synthesized microtonal scales dividing the octave into anywhere from 5 to 56 steps, sounds no harpsichord could play. [[1](https://distributedmuseum.illinois.edu/exhibit/hpschd/)]

### 📽️ The Urbana Mega-Event

The five-hour premiere was less concert than environment: seven harpsichordists (including David Tudor, Vischer, Yūji Takahashi, William Brooks, Neely Bruce and Philip Corner), 208 computer tapes on 52 monaural machines, 6,400 slides and 40 films — much of the imagery on loan from NASA, months before Apollo 11 — thrown onto rectangular screens, a 340-foot circular scrim and the hall's domed ceiling, with the audience wandering freely. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HPSCHD)] Cage: "When I produce a happening, I try my best to remove intention in order that what is done will not oblige the listener in any one way." [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HPSCHD)]

### 🎛️ KNOBS: Chance Sold by Mail

The 1969 Nonesuch LP (a 21-minute studio version) extended indeterminacy into the living room: the first 10,000 copies each included a unique computer printout, *KNOBS*, generated on a CDC 6400 at SUNY Buffalo, prescribing volume, treble and bass settings for each stereo channel every five seconds — so every purchaser could realize their own performance. It is an early landmark of mass-produced generative art. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HPSCHD)]

### 🧬 Afterlife

Sketches and tapes are preserved in the University of Illinois' Sousa Archives, and the work has been revived at full scale, including an ISSUE Project Room production. The Nonesuch recording was reissued in 1998 and again by the Electronic Music Foundation in 2003.
