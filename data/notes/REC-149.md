4 Rooms (2006) is a work by Danish sound artist Jacob Kirkegaard, recorded inside the Chernobyl exclusion zone in October 2005 and released by Touch (TONE 26) on 26 April 2006 — the 20th anniversary of the reactor disaster. Kirkegaard applied the recursive re-recording process of Alvin Lucier's *I Am Sitting in a Room* (1970) to four abandoned public rooms, letting each empty space sing its own resonance. [[1](https://fonik.dk/works/FourRooms.html), [2](https://touch33.net/catalogue/tone-26-jacob-kirkegaard-4-rooms.html)]

### 🔁 Lucier's process, inverted

Lucier seeded his room with his own stuttering voice and let repeated playback dissolve speech into resonance. Kirkegaard removed the human entirely.

* In each room he recorded roughly 10 minutes of "silence" — the room's ambient sound — with no external source introduced; he even left the room while the recorder ran. [[1](https://fonik.dk/works/FourRooms.html)]
* The recording was then played back into the room and simultaneously re-recorded, and this cycle repeated up to ten times. [[1](https://fonik.dk/works/FourRooms.html)]
* With each generation the room's resonant frequencies reinforce themselves: what begins as near-silence slowly unfolds into a dense drone with shifting overtones. [[1](https://touch.kudosrecords.co.uk/release/tone26/jacob-kirkegaard-4-rooms)]

### 🌍 The rooms

Kirkegaard deliberately chose spaces that had been active meeting points before the 1986 evacuation: a church in the village of Krasno, and an auditorium, a gymnasium and a swimming pool in the workers' city of Pripyat. [[1](https://fonik.dk/works/FourRooms.html)] Each track is one room; the piece is, in effect, four portraits of enforced emptiness.

### 🔊 What you hear

Because no sound is added, everything audible is the room amplifying itself — hums, air, distant weather, the microphone chain's own noise, all filtered through the space's geometry into sustained, organ-like tones. The conceptual charge is unavoidable: a process that makes absence audible, applied to rooms emptied by radiation, twenty years on. [[1](https://touch33.net/catalogue/tone-26-jacob-kirkegaard-4-rooms.html)]

### 🧬 Significance

4 Rooms sits at the junction of process music, field recording and memorial art. It demonstrated that Lucier's 1970 experiment was not a one-off but a general instrument — a way of interrogating any space — and it established Kirkegaard's signature method of listening to phenomena that are otherwise inaudible (he has since recorded otoacoustic emissions of the ear itself and geothermal vibrations). It remains among the most quietly devastating works in the Touch catalogue. [[1](https://fonik.dk/works/FourRooms.html), [2](https://touch33.net/catalogue/tone-26-jacob-kirkegaard-4-rooms.html)]
