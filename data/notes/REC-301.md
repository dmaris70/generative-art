Jean Ritchie and Doc Watson at Folk City (1963) captures two towering Appalachian tradition-bearers, live at Gerde's Folk City in Greenwich Village at the height of the folk revival — Ritchie, the "Mother of Folk" from Viper, Kentucky, with her lap dulcimer, and the blind North Carolina guitarist Doc Watson, then just breaking through to Northern audiences. Issued on Folkways, it is one of the defining documents of traditional Southern music meeting the New York revival on its own terms. [[1](https://www.si.edu/object/siris_arc_341499), [2](https://www.discogs.com/release/5598657-Jean-Ritchie-And-Doc-Watson-Jean-Ritchie-And-Doc-Watson-At-Folk-City)]

### 🤝 A brand-new partnership

Ritchie and Watson had been introduced only months earlier, in 1963, by the folklorist Ralph Rinzler — the man who brought Watson from Deep Gap, North Carolina to the urban folk circuit and later co-founded the Smithsonian's Folklife Festival. The Folk City engagement followed almost immediately, so the record preserves a musical friendship at the moment of its formation. The tape was rolled on location by Ritchie's husband, the photographer and filmmaker George Pickow. [[1](http://hardysfolkcity.blogspot.com/2008/10/jean-ritchie-and-doc-watson-live-at.html), [2](https://allerlei2013riffmaster.wordpress.com/2014/02/15/doc-watson-jean-ritchie-at-folk-city-1963/)]

### 🎻 The music

The set mixes duets with solo turns — six songs together, with seven Watson solos and four from Ritchie, joined on some numbers by Roger Sprung on banjo and fiddle. [[1](http://hardysfolkcity.blogspot.com/2008/10/jean-ritchie-and-doc-watson-live-at.html)]

* Murder ballads beside spirituals and dance tunes: "Pretty Polly", "House Carpenter", "Hiram Hubbard", "Go Dig My Grave", "Amazing Grace", "Wabash Cannonball". [[1](https://allerlei2013riffmaster.wordpress.com/2014/02/15/doc-watson-jean-ritchie-at-folk-city-1963/)]
* Ritchie's Appalachian dulcimer — the fretted zither she almost single-handedly popularized — carrying unaccompanied-ballad repertory from her Cumberland Mountains family tradition.
* Watson's flatpicked guitar, translating fiddle tunes to the flat-top with a speed and clarity that would reshape American guitar playing.

### 🌍 Gerde's Folk City

Gerde's, on West 4th Street, was the revival's crucible — the room where Bob Dylan made his professional New York debut in 1961. Programming source singers like Ritchie and Watson beside the young songwriters was exactly the exchange the revival ran on, and this album is among its best surviving records. [[1](http://hardysfolkcity.blogspot.com/2008/10/jean-ritchie-and-doc-watson-live-at.html)]

### 🧬 Afterlife

Smithsonian/Folkways reissued the concert in 1990 with three additional performances — "East Virginia", "Blue Ridge Mountain Blues" and "Pretty Saro" — and it remains in print through Smithsonian Folkways, part of the permanent Moses Asch archive. Watson went on to Newport and a six-decade career; Ritchie's dulcimer style became the instrument's modern foundation.
