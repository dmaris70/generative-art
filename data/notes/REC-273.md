This Ocora recording presents Munojot Yulchieva (Munâjât Yultchieva, b. 1960), the pre-eminent female voice of Uzbek classical song, with the ensemble of her lifelong mentor, rubab player Shavkat Mirzaev. Two catalogue cautions: the Ocora CD (C 560060, *Ouzbékistan*) is dated 1994 in discographies, with a second album, *A Haunting Voice*, recorded in Tashkent for Germany's Network label in 1997; and Yulchieva's core repertoire is strictly speaking the Ferghana–Tashkent maqam style of her native Andijan region, a sister tradition to the Bukharan Shashmaqam proper. ### 🕌 The Shashmaqam Tradition

The Shashmaqam ("six maqams") is the great courtly art-music cycle of Central Asia, developed in Bukhara and shared by Uzbeks and Tajiks: six suites — Buzruk, Rost, Navo, Dugoh, Segoh, Iroq — each a long arc of instrumental preludes and sung sections built on distinct modes and usul rhythmic formulas. Vocal lines typically begin low and climb across a performance to a high climactic zone (the *awj*) before descending to rest. The tradition was proclaimed a UNESCO Masterpiece of Intangible Heritage in 2003 (Representative List 2008), jointly for Uzbekistan and Tajikistan. [[1](https://www.ichlinks.com/archive/elements/elementsV.do?nation=UZ,TJ&elementsUid=13829896142137001111), [2](https://voicesoncentralasia.org/shashmaqam-music-and-poetry-of-central-asia/)]

### 🎙️ The Voice

Yulchieva — whose given name means "prayer" — trained at the Tashkent Conservatory, where Mirzaev steered her away from opera and toward maqam. Her deep, focused contralto, delivered with almost motionless intensity, is applied above all to Sufi devotional poetry, where classical ghazals of longing read simultaneously as earthly and divine love. She was named People's Artist of Uzbekistan in 1994. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munojot_Yo%E2%80%98lchiyeva), [2](https://www.womex.com/virtual/oxus_culture/munojot_yulchieva)]

### 🪕 The Ensemble

Mirzaev's ensemble uses the classical Central Asian chamber consort: rubab and tanbur (plucked lutes), dutar (two-string lute), gijak (spike fiddle), nay (flute), chang (hammered dulcimer) and the doira frame drum that carries the usul cycles. [[1](http://www.medieval.org/music/world/cds/wne28297.html)]

### 🧬 Significance

These 1990s recordings arrived just as post-Soviet Uzbekistan was re-embracing maqam after decades in which Soviet cultural policy had alternately folklorized and suppressed it. Ocora's edition carried the tradition's greatest living voice to Western listeners, and Yulchieva went on to international festival stages — a singer routinely compared, in stature within her tradition, to Umm Kulthum in hers. [[1](https://www.womex.com/virtual/oxus_culture/munojot_yulchieva)]
