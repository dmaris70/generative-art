Swiss Mountain Transport Systems (Gruenrekorder, 2011) is an album of unprocessed location recordings by American phonographer and sound ethnographer Ernst Karel: gondolas, funiculars and chairlifts recorded from inside their cabins as they climb and descend the Alps. Released on 1 September 2011 on the German field-recording label Gruenrekorder, it treats Switzerland's mountain infrastructure as an accidental orchestra — and became a touchstone of the 2010s wave of "infrastructural" field recording. [[1](https://www.gruenrekorder.de/?page_id=5552), [2](http://ek.klingt.org/swissmountaintransportsystems.html)]

### 🚠 The System as Instrument

The recordings were made in the summer and fall of 2008, riding transport systems of different types, vintages and elevations across Switzerland — mostly from within the enclosed mobile environment of the cabin. [[1](http://ek.klingt.org/swissmountaintransportsystems.html)]

* **Drones:** motors, cables and sheave wheels produce quasi-harmonic mechanical drones that shift as gradient and load change.
* **Rhythms:** tower crossings and gear mechanisms supply intermittent, irregular percussion — machine rhythms no one composed.
* **Windows:** through the mechanism come transient acoustic glimpses of the surrounding landscape — voices, cowbells, birds, weather — "a vast surrounding landscape inhabited by humans and other animals." [[1](http://ek.klingt.org/swissmountaintransportsystems.html)]

### 🎙️ No Processing, All Framing

Karel's method is deliberately restrained: nearly 80 minutes of unprocessed stereo recordings, composed only through choice of ride, microphone placement and edit. Reviewers heard it as proof that machines "give us unintended compositions," closer to Éliane Radigue-style drone music than to documentary. [[1](https://www.squidco.com/cgi-bin/news/newsView.cgi?newsID=1404), [2](http://olewnick.blogspot.com/2011/09/ernst-karel-swiss-mountain-transport.html)] The project is framed as "a sonic investigation of the integration of such technology into the Swiss social-geographical landscape" — an ethnography of Alpine modernity conducted entirely by ear. [[1](http://ek.klingt.org/swissmountaintransportsystems.html)]

### 🤝 The Sensory Ethnography Context

Karel was for years the sound specialist of Harvard's Sensory Ethnography Lab, mixing and sound-designing its acclaimed nonfiction films (*Leviathan*, *Sweetgrass*, *Manakamana* — the last itself set on a cable car). This album is the purest statement of that program: listening as fieldwork.

### 🧬 Afterlife

The source material spawned a family of works: a multichannel installation version made with artist Helen Mirra shown at the 2014 Whitney Biennial, and a radio/cinematic edit screened at the Viennale and Lincoln Center's Art of the Real. [[1](http://ek.klingt.org/swissmountaintransportsystems.html)] Within field-recording circles the album is cited as a model of conceptual rigor — a record whose "score" is a national transport network. [[1](https://www.antigravitybunny.com/?p=6049)]
