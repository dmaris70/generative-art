The Ursonate ("primal sonata," also Sonate in Urlauten — sonata in primal sounds) is Kurt Schwitters' roughly half-hour sound poem, developed and performed between 1922–23 and 1932 as part of his one-man Merz movement. It structures pure phonetic material — invented syllables with no dictionary meaning — into the four-movement form of a classical sonata, complete with themes, development, a cadenza and a coda, and it stands as the most influential sound poem of the twentieth century. [[1](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/blog/uncategorized/49404/9-versions-of-kurt-schwitters-ursonate), [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ursonate)]

### 🎼 A sonata made of phonemes

* The opening theme grew from a single line by Raoul Hausmann, "fmsbwtözäu," which Schwitters heard Hausmann perform and then developed for a decade.
* The final text was published in 1932 as the last issue (no. 24) of Schwitters' journal Merz, in a famous functional typography by Jan Tschichold.
* Schwitters performed it constantly at recitals, treating the score as a basis for disciplined, quasi-musical delivery rather than free improvisation. [[1](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/blog/uncategorized/49404/9-versions-of-kurt-schwitters-ursonate), [2](https://inlibris.com/item/bn61490/)]

### 📻 The recording question

What survives of Schwitters' own voice is genuinely contested, and any catalogue entry should say so:

* On 5 May 1932 Schwitters recorded material at Süddeutscher Rundfunk (sources place the session in Frankfurt or Stuttgart) — but this documented 1932 recording covers only the Scherzo, not the whole work. [[1](https://kunstradio.at/PROJECTS/SCHWITTERS/EXHIBITS/ursonate1.html), [2](http://kunstradio.at/PROJECTS/SCHWITTERS/schlichting.html)]
* A "complete" Ursonate attributed to Schwitters surfaced via artist Jack Ox and was released by WERGO in 1993.
* A 2006 voice-analysis expertise commissioned by the Kurt and Ernst Schwitters Foundation (Prof. Jens-Peter Köster, University of Trier) concluded that the WERGO recording is actually a performance by his son Ernst Schwitters, likely from 1958; whether Kurt ever made a complete recording remains open. [[1](http://kunstradio.at/PROJECTS/SCHWITTERS/schlichting.html)]

### 🗣 Sound and technique

The piece demands virtuosity: rolled r's, machine-gun consonant clusters, tender vowel melodies, strict tempo relationships between sections. Its four movements mimic sonata rhetoric so faithfully that listeners follow the "argument" with no semantic content at all — meaning generated purely by form.

### 🧬 Legacy

The Ursonate became a rite of passage for later vocalists and poets — versions exist by Eberhard Blum, Jaap Blonk, Christian Bök and many others — and its fusion of Dada phonetics with classical architecture made it a founding document for text-sound composition and contemporary vocal performance alike. [[1](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/blog/uncategorized/49404/9-versions-of-kurt-schwitters-ursonate)]
