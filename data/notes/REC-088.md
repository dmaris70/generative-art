Composition 6E is an early entry in Anthony Braxton's diagram-titled catalogue: his works carry graphic schematics rather than names, later assigned opus numbers. One catalogue note first: 6E is often loosely associated with Braxton's Arista period, but the discographical record places it earlier — it was composed in early 1968 and recorded on 27 March 1968 in Chicago, appearing on his debut album *3 Compositions of New Jazz* (Delmark), not on the 1974 Arista sessions. [[1](https://jazzdiscography.com/Artists/Braxton/brax-comp.php), [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3_Compositions_of_New_Jazz)]

### 🎼 The Diagram Titles

Braxton titled the pieces on *3 Compositions of New Jazz* with graphic formulas — clusters of letters, numbers and drawn shapes — an act that framed improvised music as *composition* with the same seriousness as the European avant-garde. The two Braxton works on the album were later identified as Compositions 6E and 6D; the Composition 6 series as a whole occupied him from roughly 1967 to 1971 and remained a repertoire he revisited for decades. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3_Compositions_of_New_Jazz), [2](https://jazzdiscography.com/Artists/Braxton/brax-comp.php)]

### 🤝 An AACM Ensemble Without a Rhythm Section

The recording (Sound Studios, Chicago, March–April 1968, produced by Robert G. Koester) unites first-generation AACM members:

* **Anthony Braxton** — alto and soprano saxophones, clarinet, flute, accordion, bells, snare drum, mixer
* **Leroy Jenkins** — violin, viola, harmonica, recorder, slide whistle, percussion
* **Wadada Leo Smith** — trumpet, mellophone, xylophone, kazoo
* **Muhal Richard Abrams** — piano, cello, alto clarinet (on two tracks)

No bass, no drum kit: texture, silence and multi-instrumentalism replace the jazz engine room. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3_Compositions_of_New_Jazz)]

### 🔊 The Music

Composition 6E, the twenty-minute opening track, is described as beginning with unaccompanied voice singing the theme before Braxton, Smith and Jenkins spin it into extended collective improvisation — song-like material dissolving into pointillist group invention. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3_Compositions_of_New_Jazz)]

### 🧬 Significance

AllMusic calls the album "outrageously forward-thinking, if not — arguably — downright visionary." [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3_Compositions_of_New_Jazz)] The Composition 6 pieces became Braxton's proof that notated structure and free improvisation could be a single practice; when Arista later signed him and released *New York, Fall 1974* — the quartet record with Kenny Wheeler, Dave Holland and Jerome Cooper that carried the diagram titles to a major label — it was this Chicago groundwork it built upon. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York,_Fall_1974)]
