Érosphère (1980) is a large acousmatic cycle by François Bayle, the long-time director of the Groupe de Recherches Musicales (GRM) in Paris. Its complete version was premiered at the Maison de Radio France on 21 January 1980, during the GRM's Cycle Acousmatique concert series. Bayle described the title as naming the law of gravitation exerted by desire — an idea he tied directly to the acousmatic situation itself, in which one understands and "sees" on the basis of what cannot be seen. [[1](https://electrodoc.musiques-recherches.be/en/c/121/bayle-francois), [2](https://francoisbayle.bandcamp.com/album/rosph-re)]

### 🔊 Architecture of the cycle

Érosphère is built as three substantial pieces interleaved with three short preludes:

* **Tremblement de terre très doux** ("Very gentle earthquake")
* **L'Infini du bruit** ("The infinity of noise")
* **Toupie dans le ciel** ("Spinning top in the sky")
* Three **Éros** preludes — *Éros bleu*, *Éros rouge*, *Éros noir*.

The whole describes an arc leading from an angular, human, everyday hubbub toward a sky free of constraints. [[1](https://electrodoc.musiques-recherches.be/en/c/121/bayle-francois), [2](https://francoisbayle.bandcamp.com/album/rosph-re)]

### ⚡ Studio 123

The Éros preludes were created from computer-generated sounds at the GRM's Studio 123 at the Maison de la Radio — early digital material folded into a musique-concrète lineage that Bayle had inherited directly from Pierre Schaeffer, who put him in charge of the GRM in 1966; the group became a department of the Institut National de l'Audiovisuel in 1975. [[1](https://brahms.ircam.fr/en/francois-bayle), [2](https://electrodoc.musiques-recherches.be/en/c/121/bayle-francois)]

### 🎧 Acousmatic utopias

Bayle liked to call his large works "utopias" — suites, trajectories, initiations — and Érosphère is a central example: music intended for pure loudspeaker listening, projected in concert over the GRM's orchestra of loudspeakers, where trajectory and image replace instrument and gesture. [[1](https://brahms.ircam.fr/en/composer/francois-bayle/workcourse)]

### 🧬 Afterlife

The cycle sits at the centre of Bayle's 1970s–80s peak alongside Grande polyphonie and Son Vitesse-Lumière, and has remained in circulation through reissues of his electroacoustic works and a Bandcamp edition from the composer's own catalogue. (Note: the catalogue spelling "Erosphères" is a variant; the composer's title is Érosphère.)
