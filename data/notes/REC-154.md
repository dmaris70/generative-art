Landscape in Metamorphoses is a 26-minute phonographic composition by the Indian sound artist and researcher Budhaditya Chattopadhyay, built from field recordings made between February and April 2007 in Tumbani, on the Bengal–Bihar border, and released by the German field-recording label Gruenrekorder. It documents — in sound alone — a green agricultural landscape being consumed by an industrial belt. [[1](https://www.gruenrekorder.de/?page_id=301), [2](https://archive.org/details/LandscapeInMetamorphoses)]

*A note on the date: this catalogue lists the work as 2011, but Gruenrekorder's own page gives the release as 2008 (Gr 057), with the recordings made in 2007; the 2011 attribution appears to be an error, perhaps confusing it with Chattopadhyay's later Gruenrekorder work such as* Elegy for Bangalore. [[1](https://www.gruenrekorder.de/?page_id=301), [2](https://www.gruenrekorder.de/?page_id=9517)]

### 🌍 A landscape mid-transformation

Tumbani was Chattopadhyay's childhood terrain. Returning with microphones, he found the pasture of his memory turning into cement- and concrete-industry ground, with tribal communities and their lifeways being displaced. The composition follows that trajectory: village markets, birds and rural labour gradually give way to construction zones and machine drone. Reviewers traced the arc precisely — a soundscape that "transforms from agricultural and pastoral to urban, overpopulated and industrialized" until, in Tobias Fischer's words, "richly detailed sonic exuberance has made way for a static and oppressive industrial noisescape". [[1](https://www.gruenrekorder.de/?page_id=301)]

### 🎧 Phonography as documentary and elegy

The work is explicitly double-natured: an audio documentary analysing what commodity extraction does to nature, peoples, traditions and cultural memory — and a private lamentation. Chattopadhyay has said that in the act of recording he realized the topography of his childhood "already disappeared into nostalgia"; the piece mourns the loss of his own memory-associations as much as the land. [[1](https://archive.org/details/LandscapeInMetamorphoses), [2](http://www.tokafi.com/15questions/15-questions-budhaditya-chattopadhyay/)]

### ⚡ Means of production

* Recorded on a Sony MiniDisc recorder with OKM II binaural microphones and a Neumann KMR shotgun — modest, portable tools suited to months of walking the site. [[1](https://www.gruenrekorder.de/?page_id=301)]
* Issued in 2008 as a CD-R edition of just 50 copies, plus downloads — characteristic of Gruenrekorder's documentary-scale publishing. [[1](https://www.gruenrekorder.de/?page_id=301)]

### 🧬 Significance

The piece helped establish Chattopadhyay — now a widely published theorist of sound and decolonial listening — as a distinctive voice bringing South Asian sites and politics into a field-recording culture then dominated by European and North American nature phonography. Its method, letting industrialization narrate itself through slow sonic replacement, remains a model for politically attentive soundscape composition.
