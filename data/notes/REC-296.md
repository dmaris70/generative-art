Legacy (Planet Mu, 2013) is the debut album of RP Boo — Kavain Wayne Space — the Chicago DJ and producer widely credited as the originator of footwork, released a decade and a half after he invented the style. It compiles tracks spanning his career, some over ten years old, and its arrival finally let listeners outside Chicago piece together his true place at the root of the genre [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RP_Boo), [2](https://planet.mu/releases/legacy-volume-2/)].

### 🥁 The System: 160 and Broken

Footwork is battle music: made for Chicago's dance circles, it runs near 160 bpm but withholds the beat's resolution — kicks land off-grid, toms stutter in triplet crossfire, and short vocal samples are chopped into percussive stabs repeated past meaning. RP Boo's productions are the style's rawest statement, built from deliberately sparse means into rhythmically treacherous, hypnotic loops [[1](https://www.spin.com/2013/05/rp-boo-legacy-planet-mu-footwork/), [2](https://www.tinymixtapes.com/music-review/rp-boo-legacy)].

### ⚡ "Baby Come On" and the Origin Claim

Space came up as a dancer and DJ in Chicago's ghetto house scene before his 1997 track "Baby Come On" — often cited as the first footwork track — pushed juke's four-on-the-floor into the fractured, half-abstract territory the genre now occupies [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RP_Boo), [2](https://planet.mu/releases/legacy-volume-2/)]. For years his tracks circulated on battle tapes and among dancers rather than as releases, which is why his "debut" is effectively an archive.

### 🎧 Reception

Critics received Legacy as both history lesson and shock of the new: Planet Mu's most alien-sounding footwork release, harder-hitting than records a decade younger [[1](https://www.tinymixtapes.com/music-review/rp-boo-legacy), [2](https://drownedinsound.com/releases/17636/reviews/4146389), [3](https://truantsblog.com/2013/review-rp-boo-legacy/)]. The paradox reviewers kept circling: tracks made purely as functional battle tools for dancers turned out to be some of the most avant-garde electronic music of their era.

### 🧬 Context and Afterlife

Planet Mu had introduced footwork to international audiences with the Bangs & Works compilations and DJ Rashad's early releases; Legacy supplied the missing origin story [[1](https://planet.mu/releases/legacy-volume-2/)]. Its influence is audible across the genre's global diaspora — from Japanese juke scenes to experimental club music — and RP Boo has since toured worldwide and released further albums, but Legacy remains the foundational document: the sound of a style's inventor, heard first-hand, fifteen years late.
