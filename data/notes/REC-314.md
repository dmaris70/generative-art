Rainforest (1968) is David Tudor's founding work of object-resonance electroacoustics, commissioned by the Merce Cunningham Dance Company — for an initial fee of $500 plus $25 per performance — as the score for Cunningham's dance *RainForest*, whose set was Andy Warhol's floating *Silver Clouds*. Instead of loudspeakers, sound is sent through transducers bolted to physical objects, each object resonating as a unique instrument. The series evolved through four numbered versions (1968–73). [[1](https://www.getty.edu/research/exhibitions_events/events/david_tudor_symposium/pdf/driscollrogalsky.pdf), [2](https://www.artforum.com/columns/david-tudors-rainforest-iv-187102/)]

### ⚡ Coneless Loudspeakers

The enabling device was an audio transducer — essentially a speaker without a cone, descended from a US Navy underwater design — which Tudor had first used on roving carts in *Bandoneon !* (1966). For Rainforest I he built an eight-channel amplifier and a suitcase-sized set of objects (aluminium sheets, a steel tray, wooden planks), driven by "oscillators that made animal-like and bird-like sounds"; because the small objects had little acoustic presence, contact microphones re-amplified their resonances into the hall. It was usually performed as a duo, "piano-four-hands" style, often by Tudor and Gordon Mumma. [[1](https://www.getty.edu/research/exhibitions_events/events/david_tudor_symposium/pdf/driscollrogalsky.pdf)]

### 🎤 Rainforest II — the Uncertain One

This catalogue entry is flagged uncertain, with reason. Rainforest II (c. 1968–69) is the version Tudor described as using **vocal input** — one or more speaking or singing voices fed through the objects, which act as "mechanical filters." But Rogalsky's research found *no record of it ever being performed as a concert work in its own right*: it was most likely realised inside Merce Cunningham *Events*, with Tudor processing John Cage's vocalisations. Confusingly, the New World Records CD of the 1972 Radio Bremen performance (Tudor with Cage's *Mureau*) is mislabelled "Rainforest II" — that tape-input version is properly Rainforest III. [[1](https://www.getty.edu/research/exhibitions_events/events/david_tudor_symposium/pdf/driscollrogalsky.pdf), [2](https://davidtudor.org/Works/rainforest.html)]

### 🌲 From Suitcase to Environment

Rainforest III (1972) toured Europe as a simultaneous performance with Cage, using Pepsi Pavilion tape sources — whale song, brainwaves, mosquitoes in a jar. Rainforest IV emerged from Tudor's 1973 workshop at Chocorua, New Hampshire: a five-hour barn performance with large suspended sculptural speakers, by students including Bill Viola and John Driscoll, who became the group Composers Inside Electronics. [[1](https://www.getty.edu/research/exhibitions_events/events/david_tudor_symposium/pdf/driscollrogalsky.pdf)]

### 🧬 Legacy

Rainforest IV became one of the most performed sound installations ever; versions I and IV are paired on Mode's recorded edition, and MoMA acquired *Rainforest V*, the fully autonomous installation form. [[1](https://moderecords.com/catalog/064tudor/), [2](https://www.moma.org/explore/inside_out/2016/06/01/moma-collects-david-tudors-rainforest-v-variation-1/)]
