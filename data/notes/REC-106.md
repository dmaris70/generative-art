Earth's Magnetic Field (1970) is a computer-music landmark by the American composer Charles Dodge: a year of geomagnetic activity around the planet, translated note for note into synthesized sound. Realized at the Columbia-Princeton Electronic Music Center and issued the same year on Nonesuch, it is one of the earliest and most influential examples of what would later be called data sonification. [[1](https://sonificationart.wordpress.com/2017/06/01/earths-magnetic-field-realizations-in-computed-electronic-sound/), [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dodge_(composer))]

### 🧲 The data: Kp indices and Bartels' "musical diagrams"

The raw material is the **Kp index** — a 0-to-9 planetary measure of geomagnetic disturbance driven by the solar wind, recorded every three hours at observatories worldwide. Geophysicists plotted these indices in a notation-like graphic invented by Julius Bartels and known, fortuitously, as "musical diagrams" — the resemblance that first suggested the piece. Dodge worked with three physicists, Bruce R. Boller, Carl Frederick and Stephen G. Ungar, taking the full year of 1961: 2,920 successive readings. [[1](https://sonificationart.wordpress.com/2017/06/01/earths-magnetic-field-realizations-in-computed-electronic-sound/), [2](https://somerecords.wordpress.com/2007/03/17/charles-dodge-earths-magnetic-field-1970/)]

### 🔁 The mapping

* The 28 distinct Kp values were mapped onto pitch — a diatonic scale in one realization, a chromatic gamut in another — so the sun's agitation of the magnetosphere literally writes the melody. [[1](https://sonificationart.wordpress.com/2017/06/01/earths-magnetic-field-realizations-in-computed-electronic-sound/)]
* In the first half the tempo breathes in accelerando–ritardando waves; the second half holds a fixed tempo in which two alternating patterns trade off, the second triggered by sudden solar-storm disturbances in the data. [[1](https://sonificationart.wordpress.com/2017/06/01/earths-magnetic-field-realizations-in-computed-electronic-sound/)]
* Timbre was Dodge's own intervention: the piece is cited as the first to use comb filters (plus all-pass filtering) explicitly as a compositional timbre control. [[1](https://sonificationart.wordpress.com/2017/06/01/earths-magnetic-field-realizations-in-computed-electronic-sound/)]

### ⚡ The technology

The sound was computed between June and September 1970 using the general-purpose sound-synthesis program written by Godfrey Winham at Princeton — the Music IV lineage of software synthesis — with Dodge programming the computer "instruments" himself. The Columbia-Princeton center, founded in 1959, was the first electronic-music studio in the United States; Dodge's piece pushed it from tape composition into fully computed sound. [[1](https://archive.org/details/lp_earths-magnetic-field_charles-dodge), [2](https://sonificationart.wordpress.com/2017/06/01/earths-magnetic-field-realizations-in-computed-electronic-sound/)]

### 🧬 Legacy

Half a century on, the record is the standard citation whenever scientists or artists sonify space-weather data, and it anticipated a whole genre of data-driven generative music: the composer designs the mapping, and the planet performs. Dodge went on to become a central figure in American computer music, notably in early speech synthesis.
