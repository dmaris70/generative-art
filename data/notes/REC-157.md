F.Guyana is an album of field recordings by the French nature recordist Marc Namblard, released by the German label Gruenrekorder (Gruen 175) in 2017 as a CD edition of 500 copies: eleven "episodes", 72 minutes 22 seconds, recorded in the forests and coastal regions of French Guiana. (The catalogue's date of 2015 is uncertain — the recordings were made in November–December of 2014, 2015 and 2016, between the dry season and the "little rainy season", but the release itself is from 2017.) [[1](https://www.gruenrekorder.de/?page_id=16085), [2](https://www.gruenrekorder.de/?page_id=17548)]

### 🌍 The places

The tracks are named for sites as much as species: the coastal town of Awala; Crique Popote; the Kaw Mountain; Petit Saut; Boeuf Mort at dark of night; a closing "Caribbean inspiration". Namblard, Parisian-born and based in Lorraine, came to nature recording from a fine-art background and has worked since 2000 as a nature guide, wildlife sound recordist and sound artist. [[1](https://www.gruenrekorder.de/?page_id=16085), [2](https://marcnamblard.bandcamp.com/album/fguyana)]

### 🐸 What you hear

* Cane toads (*Rhinella marina*) and yellow-rumped caciques (*Cacicus cela*) at Crique Popote.
* Woodpeckers and hummingbirds; white-bearded manakins (*Manacus manacus*) and red-and-green macaws (*Ara chloropterus*).
* Howler monkeys, stingless bees, and dense "forest drones" of insects.
* A track devoted to "post-explosive breeding" — the sudden mass chorus of frogs after rain on the Kaw Mountain. [[1](https://www.gruenrekorder.de/?page_id=16085)]

### 🎙 Documentary method

Unlike ambient-leaning field-recording releases, F.Guyana is explicitly educational: each episode is annotated in the booklet with its location, the wildlife present and an explanation of what is happening acoustically, accompanied by colour photographs. The reviewer at Neural noted this documentary framing as central to the record's character. [[1](https://www.gruenrekorder.de/?page_id=16085), [2](https://neural.it/2018/07/marc-namblard-f-guyana/)]

### 🧬 A second panel

The project continued: Gruenrekorder issued a companion volume, F.Guyana part II, extending the same three-year body of Guianese recordings. Together they form one of the more substantial recent portraits of a neotropical soundscape on a label that has specialized in serious phonography since the early 2000s. [[1](https://www.gruenrekorder.de/?page_id=16096), [2](https://www.gruenrekorder.de/?page_id=90)]
