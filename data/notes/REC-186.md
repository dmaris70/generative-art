Stripsody (1966) is the best-known composition by Cathy Berberian — the American mezzo-soprano who was the defining voice of the postwar vocal avant-garde — and her first work for solo voice: a virtuoso piece assembled entirely from the onomatopoeia of comic strips. The "score" replaces staves and notes with drawn cartoon panels; the singer performs the BANGs, BOINGs, sniffs, gulps and cat-fights as a rapid succession of comic vignettes. [[1](https://www.wisemusicclassical.com/work/65389/Stripsody--Cathy-Berberian/), [2](https://cathyberberian.com/biography/)]

### 🎼 A graphic score of comic strips

The notation is a three-way collaboration between music and drawing: sound-words and images are laid out along a rough three-line grid indicating high, middle and low register, with time flowing left to right. Accounts of who drew it differ — the published score's vignettes are credited to the Italian cartoonist Roberto Zamarin, while other scholarship describes the project as bringing together Berberian's musicality with the visual aesthetics of Eugenio Carmi, with Umberto Eco as cultural facilitator; the collaboration's exact division of labour is genuinely contested in the literature. [[1](https://interdisciplinaryitaly.org/stripsody-transforming-comics-into-vanguard-art/), [2](https://musicomix.wordpress.com/stripsody/), [3](https://andreagarbuglia.wordpress.com/2011/01/21/berberians-stripsody/)]

### 🗣 The instrument is the performer

Stripsody is a catalogue of extended vocal technique disguised as slapstick: sirens, radio jingles, Tarzan yells, animal noises, snoring, a western shoot-out — each executed with split-second timing and theatrical gesture. Berberian premiered it herself in 1966 at the contemporary music festival in Bremen, performing with the humour and stage presence that made her its only fully idiomatic interpreter for years. [[1](https://interdisciplinaryitaly.org/stripsody-transforming-comics-into-vanguard-art/), [2](https://www.wisemusicclassical.com/work/65389/Stripsody--Cathy-Berberian/)]

### 🤝 From muse to composer

By 1966 Berberian was already the dedicatee and first performer of landmark vocal works by Luciano Berio (her former husband) and John Cage. Stripsody inverted that relationship: the interpreter became the composer, writing the piece only she could sing — pop-culture material treated with avant-garde seriousness, at the exact moment Pop Art was making the same move in painting. [[1](https://cathyberberian.com/biography/), [2](https://interdisciplinaryitaly.org/stripsody-transforming-comics-into-vanguard-art/)]

### 🧬 Afterlife

The score has become a classic of graphic notation — reproduced in anthologies of visual music and abstract comics alike — and the piece is now standard repertoire for adventurous singers, a rare case of an avant-garde vocal work that audiences reliably love.
