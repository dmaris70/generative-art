### 1. Decision

The Foundation will produce the relief of *Every 25-Token Sketch* as a Foundation production,
at an estimated 50,000 EUR excluding VAT (range 41,000 to 61,000), charged to the
production-support sub-cap and not to the acquisition envelope. The work will be owned by the
Foundation, documented in full from before its fabrication, and shown as the demonstrator of the
collecting programme: the one object in which the programme's claim, that a rule can be the
medium of a work, is visible without explanation. It is not entered in any of the seven axes,
not counted toward node concentration, and not described as an anchor.

The decision is subject to the four conditions in section 8, of which the independent review is
the one that can stop it.

### 2. The work

A black steel wall panel, 2000 × 1500 mm, carries a machine that reads a library. The library is
every program of one to twenty-five tokens in a fifteen-symbol pixel language: 9.5 × 10²⁶
programs, of which the machine reads one a second, shortest first. Five flip-dot fields across
the top show the last five programs read. A larger field below holds the picture the machine's
selector has found best so far; when a better one arrives and the old one has lasted, a thermal
printer behind the panel prints the old one as a ticket into a pocket, where it stands for a
visitor to take. Twenty-eight digit wheels name the program; the last twenty-four will never
turn. A brass ruler along the foot marks the reading's place in the library on a logarithmic
scale; its pointer crosses a sixth of the ruler on the first day and then slows without end.
Engraved in the face at the right, the twenty-five lengths of the library and the day or year
each begins to print: six of those lines will ever be true for a visitor.

Nothing in the work is a signal from outside it. Every dot is a count from inside the machine.
The screen version, from which the relief derives, is submitted to Processing Community Day
Tokyo 2026, where the exhibition at NEORT++ is open on 24 October 2026 [confirm].

### 3. Why the Foundation produces it

The Thesis holds that the unit of value in the collection is the system, that the asset is the
decrypted system with its documentation and its outputs, and that the object is a validation
node. Three facts about this work follow from that doctrine and make the production a proper
act of the Foundation rather than a courtesy to its founder.

First, the work is the pillar in its strictest form. The programme collects art in which a rule,
a code, a system or a sign is the operative medium. Here the rule is not a method behind the
work; it is the whole of it. The enumeration is exhaustive and ordered, the selector is stated,
the found are printed. The lineage is exact and the work does not borrow its authority from it:
Vera Molnár's systematic series of the 1970s and Manfred Mohr's algorithmic works from
*Cubic Limit* (1973 to 1975) established that an enumeration can be a work; Sol LeWitt's
*Sentences on Conceptual Art* (1969) held that "the idea becomes a machine that makes the art";
Takis's electromagnetic reliefs from 1959 onward put a physical force on the wall as the work's
material. This piece puts an enumeration on the wall as a machine, and prints its found. The
Foundation's position is that the relief is a contemporary instance of the pillar, not a
pastiche of its history; the independent review (section 8) will test that position.

Second, the work is the documentation doctrine fulfilled before the object exists. The Thesis
asks for a decrypted system, captured cognition, evidence and program outputs. For this work
the complete record exists in advance: the code that is the work, every design decision with its
reasoning, the measurements that fixed its rule, the drawings, the firmware, the cost. No
acquired work can offer this. As a demonstrator of what the Foundation means by a corpus, the
relief is more exact than any purchase.

Third, the work produces the Foundation's public relationship as its output. The tickets are
the found pictures, printed one at a time for whoever is present. The Foundation intends them to
carry access to its activities [function to be decided; see section 8]. The programme's claim
and the Foundation's door become the same gesture.

### 4. Why it is not a collection node

Gate G3, anchor strength, fails and is not waived. The work has no reception, no provenance, no
place in the historical corpus the axes are built on, and it is new. A failed gate ends an
acquisition case. The production route has no such gate, and this record does not pretend
otherwise: the relief enters the Foundation as a production, under the production-support
sub-cap of 500,000 EUR a year, at about a tenth of it. It is insured and carried at replacement
cost, not at a market value it does not have. Should the work later acquire a reception that
would pass G3, its entry into the axes would be a separate decision, recorded separately, and
would not be made by the principal alone.

### 5. The conflict rule

The work's concept and direction are the principal's. A founder's work entering the founder's
foundation is the case AMD-06's conflict rule exists for. The Foundation satisfies it as follows,
and each term is a condition of the production.

1. **No consideration to the principal.** No fee, royalty, purchase price or reimbursement is
   paid to the principal for the work, its design, or the right to produce it. The principal
   assigns the work and its documentation to the Foundation without charge.
2. **Production at cost from third parties.** All payments go to the fabricator, the module
   maker, the engraver and suppliers against invoices, within the sub-cap, on the Foundation's
   ordinary procurement terms.
3. **Independent review before contract.** A reviewer with no financial or governance tie to
   the Foundation or the principal, with standing in computational or kinetic art [name to be
   agreed by the board], gives a written opinion on whether the work merits production as the
   programme's demonstrator. The principal does not take part in the choice of reviewer or in
   the decision that follows the opinion. The opinion is annexed to this record whatever it
   says.
4. **Disclosure.** The authorship statement (Annex A) accompanies every public showing. The
   Foundation does not present the work as an acquisition, an anchor, or an assessment of its
   founder's standing as an artist.
5. **Recusal.** The principal records this decision and signs it as the Foundation's
   principal; the board [or the relevant governance body: confirm] ratifies the conditions, and
   the review decision under term 3 is taken without the principal present.

### 6. Authorship and documentation

The authorship statement in Annex A is part of the work and is engraved nowhere on it; it is
the wall label's companion and the catalogue's first page. Its substance: concept, direction
and every decision by the principal; design development, code, drawings, measurements and
firmware produced in working sessions with an AI system, Claude (Anthropic), under the
principal's direction, with the whole exchange preserved; fabrication by a contractor named at
completion. The Foundation's position on AI-assisted authorship is that it is disclosed, that
the record of it is released, and that the record is itself part of the knowledge the
Foundation exists to hold.

Under AMD-09 the documentation is released openly on completion, with no embargo: the source,
the drawings, the construction specification, the measurements and the decision records,
including this one. The working transcript is released with the personal and commercial
particulars it contains removed [confirm scope with counsel].

### 8. Conditions precedent

1. The screen version is shown publicly at Processing Community Day Tokyo 2026 [24 October
   2026, confirm], and the response is recorded with the review.
2. The independent review under section 5, term 3, is received and is favourable.
3. Step 1 of the construction programme is completed with the module maker, and the
   specification is reissued with the vendor's numbers; the estimate is then a quote.
4. The ticket's function is decided by the board [access to the Foundation's activities is the
   intent], so that the printed verification line and the wall label can be final.

### 9. Risks and the counter-case

- **That the Foundation is seen to open with its founder's art.** The counter-case is real and
  the Foundation does not answer it by denial. It answers it by role, terms and candour: the
  work is a production and a demonstrator, the founder takes nothing, an outside reviewer
  decides, and the authorship is stated in full.
- **That the work is not good.** The principal's judgement of it is not independent, and
  neither is the record of its making. The Tokyo showing and the review are the correction. If
  the review is unfavourable the production does not proceed, and this record is closed with
  the opinion attached.
- **That AI-assisted authorship is contested.** The Foundation's answer is disclosure and
  release. The risk is only in obscurity, and the Foundation will not have it.
- **That cost or lead time move.** The modules are the uncertainty; step 1 converts the
  estimate to a quote before the Foundation is committed.

## Annex A — Authorship statement

*Every 25-Token Sketch — a machine reading a library*, 2026. A Foundation production.

This work was conceived and directed by [Dimitris Maris; confirm the name as it is to appear],
who set its subject, its rule, and every decision that gave it form: a library of programs read
in order, one a second; a selector with a stated preference; the found printed and given away;
a counter whose last twenty-four wheels never turn; a ruler the pointer will never cross.

Its design was developed, its code written, its drawings and firmware produced, and its
behaviour measured in working sessions between the principal and an AI system, Claude, made by
Anthropic, over [period]. In those sessions the principal proposed, judged, rejected and
chose; the system drafted, built, tested and reported. Every exchange is preserved, and the
Foundation releases the record with the work. The Foundation holds that a work made this way
is neither less the author's nor more the machine's for being so made, and that the honest
account of its making is part of the work.

The relief was fabricated by [contractor], [place], from the Foundation's specification. The
flip-dot modules are by [maker]. The enumeration, the controller and the printer software, and
the motion firmware are published under an open licence at [address].

The work descends from an exact lineage, which it does not claim as its own authority: Vera
Molnár's and Manfred Mohr's enumerations of the 1970s, which made an ordered series into a
work; Sol LeWitt's instruction pieces, in which the idea is the machine that makes the art; and
Takis's electromagnetic reliefs, which put a physical force on the wall as material. Its
library is counted, not imagined: the twenty-five lengths engraved in the face are the true
sizes of the language, and the machine will read six of them in a human lifetime.

The tickets are the found. Take one.
