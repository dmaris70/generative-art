# The motion board carrier

A passive two-layer PCB that holds an ESP32-DevKitC and five plug-in TMC2209 driver modules in
the StepStick format, with the terminals, the sensor headers, the 24 V input and a 5 V buck
module. Nothing on it is custom but the board itself: every active part is a stock module in a
socket, so a failed driver is a five-euro swap and the ESP32 is a ten-euro one. The firmware
in `../motion.ino` is written to this board's pin map.

The alternative, an off-the-shelf ESP32 printer board with four onboard drivers plus one
external driver, saves the PCB but costs a vendor-specific pin remap and a fifth driver hung
off a ribbon; it is the fallback if a PCB cannot be made, and the firmware's pin tables are the
only thing to change.

## Modules in sockets

| socket | module | note |
|---|---|---|
| U1 | ESP32-DevKitC V4, 38 pins (ESP32-WROOM-32) | two 19-pin female headers; USB to the controller |
| D1 … D4 | BIGTREETECH TMC2209 V1.3 (StepStick format, 16 pins, R_SENSE 0.110 Ω) | drums 25 … 28; UART address by MS1/MS2 straps on the carrier |
| D5 | the same | the ruler; on its own UART, address 0 |
| U2 | 24 V → 5 V buck, 2 A (Pololu D24V22F5 or equivalent, 4-pin) | feeds the DevKit's 5 V pin; the DevKit makes 3.3 V |

Verify the TMC2209 module's header against the vendor's current drawing before the PCB is
ordered; the layout below is the BIGTREETECH V1.2/V1.3 header, UART on the PDN pin.

```
TMC2209 StepStick, seen from above, pin 1 at the top left
  logic row              power row
  1  EN                  9  GND
  2  MS1 (AD0)          10  VM  (24 V)
  3  MS2 (AD1)          11  2B
  4  PDN_UART           12  2A
  5  PDN_UART (bridged) 13  1A
  6  CLK (to GND)       14  1B
  7  STEP               15  VIO (3.3 V)
  8  DIR                16  GND
```

supplied and ignored.

## Connectors

| ref | part | pins | what |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 … T5 | pluggable screw terminal, 3.5 mm, 4-way | 2B 2A 1A 1B | motors: drums 25 … 28, the ruler |
| T6 | screw terminal, 5.08 mm, 2-way | VM GND | 24 V in from the 5 A branch |
| J1 … J4 | JST-XH 3-way | 3V3 GND SIG | the drums' index sensors (transmissive slot type, open-collector output) |
| J5 | JST-XH 2-way | SIG GND | the ruler's home switch |
| USB | on the DevKit | | to the controller's USB hub |

## Board

- 120 × 100 mm, 2-layer, 1.6 mm FR4, 2 oz copper; motor and VM traces 1.5 mm wide, GND pour
  both sides; four 3.2 mm mounting holes on a 110 × 90 mm pattern on M3 standoffs.
- The five driver sockets in a row along the top edge with their terminals outboard, so a
  module can be pulled without unplugging anything; the DevKit along the bottom edge with its
  USB at the board's edge; the buck module beside it; the sensor headers along the left edge.
- A 2-pin header on EN_ALL with a jumper to GND for bench tests without the ESP32.
- An LED on 5 V and one on 3V3; a test point on VM.
- Silkscreen: every socket and connector labelled with its name in this table and the axis it
  serves (DRUM 25 … 28, RULER), and the address straps printed beside each driver.

The drawing is `carrier.svg` in `../../`, generated by `tools/relief-drawings.py`. The PCB
layout itself is routine for any board house (JLCPCB, Eurocircuits, Aisler): the contractor
draws it in KiCad from this netlist and the drawing, and the artist approves the layout at the
shop-drawing stage.

## Bring-up

1. Fit the DevKit and the buck only. Apply 24 V. Check 5 V at the DevKit and 3.3 V at the
   driver sockets' pin 15. Check 0 V on the motor terminals.
2. Power off. Fit one driver in D1. Power on. Flash the firmware. `status` shows the board
   alive. The driver answers over UART: the firmware prints nothing if it does not, and
   `TMCStepper`'s `test_connection()` can be called from the serial monitor build if needed.
3. Connect drum 25's motor to T1. `home drums` turns it until its index; `drum 1 5` turns it
   to 5. If it turns the wrong way, set `DIR_INVERT[0]` in the firmware.
4. Repeat for D2 … D4, then D5 with the ruler's motor and the home switch.
5. Run the firmware's `test`; measure the index and home offsets; enter them in
   `motion_core.h`; rebuild; repeat `test`.
