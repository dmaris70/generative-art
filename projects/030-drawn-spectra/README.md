# Drawn Spectra

*An algorithmic philosophy — the freehand gesture as the score, the page as an instrument.*

---

## The manifesto

**Drawn Spectra** begins where the straight line ends. Its atomic unit is not the taut segment but the *freehand arc* — a continuous curve laid down as if by a hand moving across a surface, carrying speed, hesitation, and tremor in its every inflection. The plane it inhabits is not neutral: the horizontal axis is time, the vertical axis is frequency, and so every stroke is simultaneously a shape and a sound-in-waiting. To draw here is to compose. The movement's whole conviction is that a gesture, faithfully inscribed, already contains its music — that the curve rising steeply from low to high is a glissando, that a trembling horizontal band is a held tone with vibrato, that a scatter of short flecks is percussion. A meticulously crafted algorithm in this idiom does not illustrate sound; it *transcribes* the imagined gesture with the fidelity of a master draughtsman.

The generative core is the arc-bundle. A single event is never one line but a ribbon of near-parallel strands, each following the same frequency contour offset slightly in pitch and perturbed by an independent hand-tremor — so that a sweep reads as a thick, breathing band rather than a wire. These contours are built from a handful of control points interpolated smoothly, then modulated: some events oscillate like a bowed vibrato, some arch and fall, some climb in a single unbroken ascent, some stutter into brief staccato marks. The composition is the distribution of these events across the time-frequency field — dense clusters and open silences, low drones and high filigree — negotiated so the page reads as a coherent score rather than a spill of marks. This balance is the painstaking work: the product of deep computational expertise is visible precisely in how inevitable the arrangement feels.

Noise is the signature of the hand. Perlin fields displace every sampled point along each stroke by a small, coherent wander, so the lines carry the microscopic unsteadiness of a real gesture without dissolving into scribble. The tremor is tuned, not thrown — enough that no two strands in a bundle are identical, little enough that the collective shape stays legible. This is the difference between a master-level implementation and a novelty: the randomness is *governed*, each octave of noise chosen to sit beneath the threshold of chaos, refined through countless iterations until the marks feel drawn rather than computed.

Colour, when it enters, obeys the axis. Because height is frequency, the palette can map pitch to hue — low strokes settling into deep graphite and umber, high strokes lifting toward cooler light — so that the image is read the way the ear would read it, register by register. But the default is austere: black ink on prepared paper, a faint time-frequency grid beneath like the ruling of a staff, the marks accumulated at low opacity so that overlapping strands deepen into weight exactly where a real hand pressed and returned. The spectral tint is a dial, not a demand; the movement trusts that the gesture alone carries the meaning, and adds colour only as a second, optional reading.

What Drawn Spectra is finally after is the moment a viewer stops seeing decoration and starts *hearing* the page — realising that this curve would sing, that band would drone, those flecks would snap. Beauty lives in the process: the arcs are generated stroke by stroke, each seed a different performance of the same instrument, none of them anticipatable from a single mark. The implementation should feel like the residue of long practice at the very top of the field — every contour type, every tremor octave, every rule for where events may fall refined until the emergence is reliable yet never mechanical. Draw the arc. Let the plane sound it. Trust the hand.
