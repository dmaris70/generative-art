# How pairs are rated (living document)

Scale: **1** discard · **2** weak · **3** acceptable · **4** good · **5** keep — could hang.
Claude rates first and says why; Dimitris corrects; after each round this file is revised from the corrections, and the next round is
rated with the revised rubric. Ratings live in `round-NN.json` (Claude) and `round-NN-user.json` (Dimitris).

## Rubric v1 (Claude's own, before any correction) — superseded by the changes below
Rewarded: a place is felt (not a texture); depth from arm's length to far range; weather doing something; full tonal range with real
blacks; the two sheets unlike each other but belonging together; restraint; nearness to the source's feeling (a shoulder in cloud, seen by
a body). Penalised: a blank or near-blank sheet (black, grey or blur); marbled/"computer" rock; visible artefacts (terrace contour lines,
melted towers, comb streaks, mirrored tear, stepped lakes, spike cairns); two near-identical sheets; centred pictogram peaks.

## What corrections have taught

### Round 1 (48 pairs, 2026-09-21) — agreed on 36, every difference was one point; his mean 2.98, mine 2.94; he gave no 5
He rated **higher** than I did: the quiet ones — white-on-white snow arête (aoraki 32651), a cairn on a snow dome (nevis 31815), a soft
veiled valley (watzmann 34065); a pair whose *one* sheet is very good (astraka 31209, "the left is very good"); rock **texture** for its own
sake (robson 35103, "i like texture"); **antithesis** between the two sheets (trollveggen 35507) even where I saw a smeared wall; and the
rounded towers I called melted (roraima 33661).
He rated **lower**: the crisp, detailed range view (brenta 31310, "too detailed"); a pair with a flat black sheet (darrans 34497); my only 5
(robson 32017 → 4); two dark-ridge pairs I liked for depth (invented 32550, watzmann 35608).

**Rubric v2 — changes**
1. Quiet, minimal and veiled pairs are worth more than I gave them: restraint is a positive, not an absence. Do not mark a pair down for
   "slight" or "low in incident" if it is coherent.
2. Crisp topographic detail is a *fault* ("too detailed"): it reads as a rendering of terrain, not as a photograph of weather. Prefer
   suggestion to description.
3. Texture is a pleasure in itself. Do not dismiss a textured sheet as "computer texture" unless it is actually patterned or marbled.
4. The pair is judged as a pair: strong contrast of character between the sheets is a merit; one very good sheet can carry a pair to 3.
5. A flat black (or blank) sheet costs a full point more than I charged: such a pair is a 2 at most.
6. I am too quick to call smoothing artefacts "wrong" (rounded towers, smeared walls) — he reads some of them as form. Mark down only what
   breaks the picture (mirrored tear, comb streaks, stepped lake, contour-line terraces).
7. 5 is reserved: nothing in round 1 could hang. Do not give a 5 for nearness to the source alone.

**What did not track his ratings**: the harness flags (clean pairs 3.00, flagged 2.92), and every tone metric and temper value
(|r| ≤ 0.29). By mood: exposure 3.57, clearing 3.08, looming 2.90, passage 2.89, whiteout 2.60. By pair: turned 3.24, back 3.0,
one view 2.91, later 2.89, down 2.50. By key: low 3.23, full 3.0, high 2.67. Small samples — directions, not findings.

### Round 2 (48 pairs, rated with v2) — agreed on 31 (round 1: 36); mean difference 0.38 (0.25); his mean 3.38, mine 3.25; he gave two 5s

**Rubric v3 — changes**
1. Emptiness is a merit when it is *soft and pale or grey*: a nearly empty sheet beside a quiet one can be the poem. The "blank sheet ≤ 2"
   rule (v2 #5) now applies only to a dead, edgeless **black** rectangle (darrans 34497). A black *shape with an edge* is a gesture.
2. Two sheets that nearly repeat are not a fault; repetition with a small shift reads as time. Stop marking "sheets too alike".
3. What the 5s share: a large calm field and **one decisive dark gesture** — a line, a wall, an edge — and nothing else. Extreme economy.
   Look for that, and be willing to give 5.
4. Quiet is not enough by itself: a whiteout whose only incident is a spike-like cairn, or a dark textured slope with no gesture, is a 3.
   I over-rewarded these in round 2.
5. Antithesis helps only when each sheet also stands alone.

### Round 3 (45 pairs, rated with v3) — agreed on 31 (69 %; round 2: 65 %, round 1: 75 %); mean difference 0.31; his mean 3.24, mine 3.38

**Rubric v4 — changes**
1. Described terrain and spike-cairn whiteouts are 2s, not 3s, unless something else carries them.
2. 4 needs more than a formula: antithesis, softness or layered ridges alone are a 3. Give 4 when there is a *place* and a *gesture*.
3. 5 = calm field + one decisive dark gesture, **or** a near dark mass with a clearly legible far place behind it.
4. Do not give 2 to murk that still holds a form; 2 is for pattern, dead black, or nothing at all.

### Round 4 (48 fresh seeds by seed, after the six generator changes; rated with v4) — agreed on 32 (67 %); his mean 3.38, mine 3.38
**Did the changes work?** His mean rose from 3.20 (rounds 1–3) to 3.38: +0.18 with a standard error of 0.12 — suggestive, not established.
The clearer effect is at the bottom: pairs he rated 2 or less fell from 20 % to 8 %; pairs at 4 or more rose from 38 % to 42 %. The
changes removed failures more than they created keepers (two 5s in 48, same rate as before).
Of my three 5s he confirmed one (jorasses 144371) and lowered two to 4 (invented 73549, denali 127707) — a black wall or diagonal is not
enough by itself. His other 5 I had at 4: **invented 79798** — far spires in veil with one *grey* peak rising across the cut. So the gesture
need not be black or huge; it can be mid-grey and gentle, and crossing the cut counts. He raised described or patterned pairs I marked down
(eiger 111043, watzmann 136039, arthurs 98545, ushba 106877) and lowered several of my "dark diagonal + veil" 4s (ushba 63134, meije 86047,
nevis 121458, fitzroy 131873, drakensberg 92296): that grammar is becoming a formula, and he sees it.

**Rubric v5 — changes**
1. "Dark diagonal against veiled distance" is a 3 unless something particular happens in it. Do not give 4 for the grammar alone.
2. The gesture can be grey and modest; a form that crosses the cut is a merit.
3. Described range views are 3–4 for him when calm and wide; stop defaulting them to 3.
4. My 5s are still over-given: two of three lowered. Give 5 only when both sheets are needed and neither is merely dark.

By mood this round: passage 3.64, clearing 3.38, exposure 3.33, whiteout 3.25 (n = 4), looming 3.00. The invented ground rated highest
(3.78 over 9 pairs) — better than any real range in this round.

**Rubric v6 — what I now believe, with less confidence than before**
1. He responds to *atmosphere and space* — wide plains under weather, pale veiled basins, an empty sky over a low range — more than to
   *shape*. I have been rating silhouettes and gestures; he is rating air.
2. A dark mass in front is not a merit in itself and is wearing thin through repetition.
3. Rendering artefacts I penalise (comb streaks, glassy slopes) often do not bother him.
4. Between 3 and 4 my judgement is not informative. Say so, rather than explain each one confidently.

### Choices, set 1 (30 matchups) — predicted 25 of 30; on the honest test, 9 of 14
The 16 matchups where his two earlier scores differed: I predicted all 16, but I had seen those scores — what they do show is that **he is
consistent**: all 16 of his choices agree with his own earlier ratings. The 14 matchups where he had scored both pairs the same are the
real test of rubric v6: **9 of 14** (chance gives ≥ 9 about one time in five, p ≈ 0.21). Better than the coin toss of round 5, not yet
evidence. The five misses — three of them contradict "atmosphere over shape" directly: he chose the black blade with cloud sea and far
tower over a wide basin under sky (22); a lit patch under a dark rim, streaky blur and all, over a wide valley with lakes (11); the dark
band / dark rim / lit outcrop over a plain under dark cloud (9). The other two: dark ridges out of pale mist over a veiled far place (15);
one dark peak centred on the cut with a lit far summit over a calm valley beside a black wall (30).
**Reading**: width and openness are not what he wants either. In four of the five misses he chose the pair with a **small lit thing against
dark** (lit outcrop, lit patch, lit far summit, pale mist under dark ridges). Hypothesis for set 2, to be tested not assumed:
*a point of light held by darkness* outranks both the dark gesture and the open space.

### Choices, set 2 (40 same-score matchups, pre-registered) — predicted 20 of 40: exactly chance
The "point of light held by darkness" hypothesis, tagged in advance on 16 matchups: he chose that pair in **6 of 16** — if anything the
reverse. **The hypothesis is wrong.** The other 24: 14 of 24. Pooled with set 1's honest matchups: 29 of 54 (p ≈ 0.34). 
**Conclusion, stated plainly: Claude cannot predict which of two pairs Dimitris prefers once his own score is held equal.** Three successive
theories (dark gesture; atmosphere and space; light held by dark) were each fitted to a handful of cases and each failed on fresh ones.
In the ten hypothesis misses he chose, against the lit-in-dark pair: a cloud sea with a far range (2), painterly swirling strokes (6), two
plain dark bulks in haze (10), a pale gentle whiteout (17), a streaked slope under dark cloud (21), soft veiled slopes (23), a veiled distance
with one dark shoulder (28), a soft veil with one small lit patch (32), a near-blank pale sheet (33), a blurred slope against a ridge over a
cloud sea (39). Softness recurs — but "softness" was already theory two. No new theory is offered.

**What is established, after 237 ratings and 70 choices**
- He is consistent (16/16 choices agree with his own earlier scores).
- Claude can recognise his 2s (pattern, murk, dead black, nothing) and no longer gives false 5s.
- Claude's 3-versus-4 and within-tier judgements carry no information. They should not drive the generator.
- Generator changes that *were* supported by his numbers: fewer failures after less smear / mood weights / weather limits / no looming
  (ratings ≤ 2: 20 % → 8 %).
**What to do with this**: use Claude and the harness to *cull* (remove likely 2s), and leave selection among the rest to him.
